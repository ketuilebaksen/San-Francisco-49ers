A starting-caliber pass rusher just told his team he wants out. Will McDonald IV, the 15th overall pick in the 2023 draft, a player who had ten and a half sacks just two years ago, has asked the New York Jets for a trade, because he's frustrated with how little he's being used. And the NFL trade deadline is today, Tuesday, at 4 p.m. Eastern. Out here on the West Coast, the San Francisco 49ers are 7 and 1, two and a half games clear in the NFC West, coming off a 38 to 17 win over the Raiders. They just lost a young defensive lineman for the season. They're still looking for receiver help. And on Monday, the NFL officially reinstated Brandon Aiyuk, handing the 49ers yet another decision. So the question today is simple: does John Lynch make one more call before the clock hits zero? And who is the huge weapon this team could still add?

Let's start with the name that just landed on the market. On Monday, ESPN reported that Jets defensive end Will McDonald IV has requested a trade. NFL Network's Ian Rapoport also reported that McDonald told the team he'd prefer to be traded. The reason is frustration with his role. Through nine games this season, McDonald has three sacks, a forced fumble and 13 tackles. He's played 263 defensive snaps, and he's topped 30 snaps only once in the last five games. In Sunday's 35 to 12 loss to Kansas City, he played just 23 defensive snaps.

That's a big drop for a player who, in 2024, had ten and a half sacks. He started for head coach Aaron Glenn in 2025 before gradually becoming more of a situational player. And the Jets, who have now lost four straight, are using him less and less. So you can understand why he wants a change.

Now, here's what the 49ers would actually be getting. McDonald isn't an every-down defensive end. His game is speed off the edge, and his biggest question mark has always been his strength against the run. So realistically, you're not getting a three-down starter. You're getting a pass-rush specialist, a player who comes in on obvious passing downs and gets after the quarterback. And the 49ers could give him exactly that role, coming off the edge opposite Nick Bosa on third down, with DeForest Buckner pushing the pocket from the inside.

Why does that matter so much for this team? Because pressure has been a real issue. In the loss in Atlanta, the 49ers pressured Michael Penix Jr. on just three of his 34 dropbacks. According to ESPN Research, their pressure rate when rushing four or fewer players was 23 percent at that point, the second worst in the entire league. That's a big reason they traded for Buckner. And on Sunday against the Raiders, Bosa was back for the first time since September and Buckner played his first game back in red and gold. Buckner had two quarterback hits. But here's a number that might surprise you: Kirk Cousins wasn't sacked a single time. The 49ers forced five turnovers, which was fantastic, but the pass rush still didn't finish plays the way you'd want.

And there's a bigger picture here, too. Over all eight games this season, the 49ers have scored 235 points and allowed 156. That's a plus-79 point differential, roughly 29 points scored and 20 allowed per game. That's the profile of a very good team. But in three of those eight games, the opponent scored 29 or more: 30 for Arizona, 29 for Seattle, and 29 for Atlanta. The 49ers went 2 and 1 in those three games. So there have been weeks where the offense has had to cover for the defense. A third-down pass rusher is exactly how defenses close out games. That's what makes a move like this more than a luxury.

Now, here's the catch. The Jets say they're not trading him. Aaron Glenn was asked about the request and said, "Will's going to be here." He also added, "Never say never," about trades in general. ESPN reported that teams have called about McDonald, and the Jets haven't engaged in trade talks. They also turned down inquiries about him at last year's deadline, including reported interest from Tampa Bay. And after last year's deadline, the Jets exercised his fifth-year option, which means he's under contract through 2027, with about 13.8 million dollars guaranteed next season.

It's also interesting to think about the Jets' history here. Last year, New York traded away Sauce Gardner and Quinnen Williams for future draft capital. They've shown they're willing to move big names. But this year, they've been taking a different approach with some of their young players. When Garrett Wilson's name came up in trade rumors a couple of weeks ago, Adam Schefter reported that a source told him there was no way the Jets would trade him, and that ownership loves him. Wilson said publicly that he wanted to stay. So the Jets have been willing to dig in. With McDonald, the difference is that the player himself is asking to leave. That changes the dynamic. A team can keep a player who wants to stay. Keeping a player who wants out, and who's barely playing, is a lot harder to justify.

So the 49ers can't count on this happening. But I'll say this: all of the Jets' public statements came before the request went public. Once a player asks out, every week he sits unhappily on the roster cuts into his value. And the deadline is today. That's the leverage the buying teams have. Rapoport also listed teams like the Bears, Lions, Patriots and Giants as possible fits, so San Francisco wouldn't be the only team calling.

Let's look at it from the Jets' side, too. McDonald isn't their best pass rusher. David Bailey has been their top sack producer. But behind those two, their edge group isn't deep. That's a fair reason to say no. Still, a frustrated player who's playing less than half the snaps isn't helping a defense on a four-game losing streak.

Let's picture how McDonald would actually fit on this defense. On first and second down, the 49ers could keep their base front, with Bosa on one edge, Buckner and Osa Odighizuwa inside, and their run-stopping ends setting the edge. Then, on third down, McDonald comes in. Now you've got Bosa on one side, McDonald's speed on the other, and Buckner collapsing the middle. Offensive lines can't slide protection toward Bosa without leaving McDonald one-on-one, and they can't double McDonald without giving Bosa or Buckner a free lane. That's how a specialist makes everyone around him better. And it would also let the staff manage Bosa's snaps as he works back from that calf injury, without sacrificing pressure on the most important downs.

Mykel Williams, last year's first-round pick, is part of that rotation too, and he's healthy again. But young pass rushers develop at their own pace. Adding a player who has already had a ten-and-a-half-sack season gives this defense a proven option for the stretch run while Williams keeps growing.

So what would it cost? 49ers analyst Brian Peacock floated a modest Day 3 pick as the price that would make McDonald appealing. The Jets would almost certainly want more, maybe a second-round pick. They probably won't get that. The 49ers still hold their own first, second and third-round picks in 2027, so they have the ammunition. But Peacock also pointed out that a receiver is still the more likely trade target for San Francisco, with an edge rusher second. And it's important to say clearly: there's no confirmed report that the 49ers have contacted the Jets about McDonald.

What about the money? McDonald's 2027 salary of about 13.8 million guaranteed is the real cost here. And next year's cap sheet matters for the 49ers. Bosa, Purdy, Warner and the rest of the core all carry big numbers. And Aiyuk's contract could leave the team with more than 19 million dollars in dead money next year if he's released. So the 49ers have room, but they don't have endless room. On the other hand, McDonald's deal ends after 2027. There's no long-term commitment. If he's worth it, great. If not, he walks after next season. For a team chasing a Super Bowl, that's a reasonable bet.

Let's go back to Sunday's game for a moment, because the defensive performance tells two stories at once. The first story is the takeaways. Rookie linebacker Jaden Dugger picked off Cousins on the first play of the second half and took it 35 yards for a touchdown. Fred Warner punched the ball out of Brock Bowers' hands, and Dre Greenlaw recovered. Upton Stout forced a fumble from Michael Mayer near the goal line, and Warner recovered it in the end zone. Renardo Green intercepted Cousins in the end zone. And Malik Mustapha picked him off on the Raiders' final possession. That's the kind of opportunistic defense that wins playoff games. It was the first time the 49ers had five second-half takeaways against the Raiders since their days at Candlestick Park, 20 years ago, according to the Associated Press.

The second story is the pass rush. Bosa had a tackle for loss in his first game since September. Buckner had two quarterback hits in his return. Those are good signs for two players getting back up to speed. But zero sacks against a pocket passer like Cousins is a reminder that the front still has another level to reach. Some of that will come naturally as Bosa builds his workload back up. Some of it might come from a deadline addition. Either way, it's the one part of this team that still feels unfinished.

Now let's talk about the injury news that makes all of this even more urgent. On Monday, Kyle Shanahan confirmed that rookie defensive lineman Gracen Halton fractured and dislocated his ankle against the Raiders. He'll have surgery this week and goes on injured reserve, and Shanahan said he doubts Halton could return even for a deep playoff run. That's another blow to a defensive line that's already lost Alfred Collins to a patellar tendon injury and has been without Sam Okuayinonu, who's out until January.

There was some good news, though. Shanahan said defensive tackle C.J. West is expected to return to the practice field on Wednesday. And on the cornerback injuries from Sunday, Renardo Green, with his back, and Deommodore Lenoir, with his foot, are both considered day-to-day. That's a big relief, and it could change the urgency of adding another defensive back. When both starting outside corners went down on Sunday, it looked like it could be a serious problem. Now there's a real chance both are available for Dallas.

And there's another interesting name connected to the defensive line. Former 49ers defensive tackle Jordan Elliott is a free agent and has been working out for teams, including the Chicago Bears. Shanahan was asked about him on Monday and said the building loved Jordan, and that the 49ers would be interested in him as a person and a player. That doesn't mean a signing is coming. But with Halton out, it's a name worth keeping an eye on.

And this front office has a history of making exactly these kinds of deadline moves. In 2019, the 49ers traded for receiver Emmanuel Sanders from Denver, and he helped them reach the Super Bowl. In 2022, they made a blockbuster for Christian McCaffrey, sending second, third and fourth-round picks in 2023, plus a fifth-rounder in 2024, to Carolina. In 2023, they added Chase Young from Washington for a third-round compensatory pick to boost the pass rush. And this year, they've already added Buckner for a fourth. When John Lynch sees a chance to strengthen a contender, he usually takes it. The question is always the price.

Now, the other side of the trade conversation: the receivers. The 49ers' receiver room has been hit hard. Mike Evans is still dealing with Achilles tendinitis, and Shanahan said on Monday that he's not expected to be ready soon. Christian Kirk is on injured reserve with a knee injury that could end his season. Ricky Pearsall is out for the year after PCL surgery. Rookie De'Zhaun Stribling is on injured reserve too. The good news is that Deebo Samuel is expected to return to practice on Wednesday. And Demarcus Robinson came back on Sunday and caught three passes.

The young players have stepped up, too. Jordan Watkins scored his first career touchdown on Sunday, a 37-yard catch, and led the team in receiving. Shanahan said Watkins has made a huge step forward. And Brandin Cooks, Shanahan said, is adjusting well and making plays when he's targeted. So the room isn't empty. But one more injury, and it could be.

So who are the realistic receiver targets? Josh Downs requested a trade from the Colts. The Colts originally wanted a third-round pick, but Adam Schefter said nobody was at a three, and it could end up at a fourth. Calvin Ridley, who's about to turn 32 and has just six catches this season, is reportedly available from Tennessee for a Day 3 pick, maybe as low as a sixth. Adam Schefter and Ian Rapoport also reported that the 49ers have looked at pass-catching tight ends as a backup option if a receiver deal doesn't come together. There's no confirmed report that the 49ers are close to any of these deals.

And Shanahan has been very honest about the challenge. He's said it's very difficult for a new receiver to learn this offense in the middle of the season. On Monday, he said he's focused on preparing for the Cowboys, and that Lynch brings him names and he evaluates them. He also pointed to Emmanuel Sanders, the receiver the 49ers traded for in 2019, saying Sanders adapted quickly because of where he was in his career, not just because he happened to be available. In other words: if the 49ers add a receiver, it needs to be a player who can pick things up fast.

So here's my take. A deadline doesn't force you to pick just one position. A player like McDonald would probably cost a Day 2 or Day 3 pick. A receiver like Downs or Ridley would probably cost a Day 3 pick. Technically, the 49ers could do both. If Lynch is serious about going all in on this group around Brock Purdy, the ideal deadline adds a pass rusher and a receiver. But it has to be at the right price.

Now let's talk about Brandon Aiyuk, because this story took another turn on Monday. The NFL officially reinstated Aiyuk from the reserve/left squad list, after the request filed by his new agent, Drew Rosenhaus. That means he's technically back under contract with the 49ers. But keeping him in San Francisco isn't believed to be on the table. Team management has made it clear he's not welcome back. Shanahan's reaction on Monday was as blunt as ever. He said the reinstatement means absolutely nothing to the team, and that the 49ers get a two-week roster exemption, which he called administrative stuff.

That two-week exemption is important. It means Aiyuk doesn't count against the 53-man roster while the team decides what to do. And it means the 49ers don't have to make a decision before today's deadline. They could try to trade him today. But with the 13-million-dollar bonus dispute still unresolved, according to reports, and so many questions about his knee and his time away, a trade looks unlikely. The more likely path is a release later, once the money question is sorted out, or at least protected.

And it's worth remembering the Commanders angle. Washington lost Terry McLaurin to a season-ending torn patellar tendon. Adam Schefter and Ian Rapoport reported over the weekend that the Commanders haven't completely abandoned the idea of adding Aiyuk. It's not a no, but it's not a yes just yet. Earlier in the week, ESPN's Dan Graziano reported he didn't expect Washington to pursue him. So that door is cracked, but barely.

Let's put the Aiyuk money in perspective, too, because it affects how the 49ers approach everything else. According to cap analysis from SB Nation, if the 49ers release Aiyuk this season, they'd take about 12.35 million dollars in dead money this year and about 19.3 million in 2027. That's roughly 31.6 million in total. And the team is projected to be over next year's cap. So the 13-million-dollar bonus dispute isn't just about principle. It's real money that could help the 49ers manage next year's cap. That's why the team is in no rush. The roster exemption gives them two weeks. The bonus dispute could take longer. And none of it affects the football team on Sunday.

On Monday, the Jacksonville Jaguars traded for safety Jessie Bates III from the Falcons, sending Atlanta a 2027 second-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick. Earlier this deadline season, the Bears added Jeffery Simmons and the Ravens traded for Jacoby Brissett after Lamar Jackson's thumb injury. Contenders are making moves. The 49ers already made one of the best ones, getting Buckner for a fourth-round pick. Now we find out if they make another.

Let's recap Sunday, too, because that win set the stage for today. The 49ers beat the Raiders 38 to 17. They scored touchdowns on their first three possessions. Brock Purdy threw his 100th career touchdown pass, in just his 57th regular season game, becoming only the 10th quarterback since the merger to do it that fast. Christian McCaffrey scored twice and passed Marshall Faulk for the most receiving touchdowns by a running back in the Super Bowl era. Rookie linebacker Jaden Dugger returned an interception 35 yards for a touchdown on the first play of the second half. And the defense forced five takeaways, all in the second half. Before that, their only loss of the season was 29 to 20 in Atlanta on October 25th. They beat Washington 23 to 17 on October 19th, and Seattle 32 to 29 on the road on October 11th.

In the NFC West, Seattle beat Arizona 17 to 3 and the Rams beat Washington 20 to 13, so both the Seahawks and the Rams are 5 and 4. That two-and-a-half-game lead isn't going to disappear overnight. But a loss or two in the next few weeks would shrink it. And the schedule gets much tougher: at Dallas this Sunday, November 15th, at 1:25 Pacific on FOX, then the Vikings in Mexico City, then Seattle at home on November 30th. A fresh pass rusher who gets to the quarterback before he can set his feet is how the 49ers protect that cushion in November and December. And if a trade gets done today, the new player could be on the field in Dallas.

Let's also take a closer look at Dallas, since that's where any new acquisition could make his debut. The Cowboys came into last weekend at 3 and 5, after blowing a big lead and losing to Arizona 33 to 30 in overtime two weeks ago. Their defense has ranked near the bottom of the league in DVOA, which is good news for an offense that leads the NFL with a 52.7 percent success rate and is the only team with a rushing success rate above 50 percent. But Dallas still has dangerous weapons, including George Pickens, the receiver playing on the franchise tag whose name has popped up in trade rumors. If Green and Lenoir are both back, the 49ers' secondary should be in good shape. If either one sits, Pickens becomes a big problem.

And here's why the pass rush matters so much in a game like that. The best way to slow down a receiver like Pickens isn't always coverage. It's pressure. If the quarterback doesn't have time to wait for a deep route to develop, the receiver doesn't get the ball. That's exactly why a third-down rusher like McDonald could pay off immediately. Five takeaways against the Raiders were incredible. But the defense can't count on five takeaways every week. It needs to finish sacks.

The offense, meanwhile, should keep doing what it does. Purdy is completing about 69 percent of his passes. McCaffrey has scored a rushing touchdown in seven straight games. George Kittle remains the security blanket. And if Deebo returns to practice on Wednesday and gets cleared for Sunday, the passing game gets a lot more dangerous. Deebo had 16 catches for 206 yards and three touchdowns, plus 78 rushing yards, in the five games before his hamstring injury.

And it's worth thinking about who else in the NFC is getting stronger. Seattle won on Sunday even without Sam Darnold, with Drew Lock starting. The Rams have won two straight. The Bears added Jeffery Simmons and are among the teams Rapoport listed as possible fits for McDonald. The Lions are on that list too, and they beat Minnesota two weeks ago. And the Vikings, the team the 49ers face in Mexico City after Dallas, have one of the best defenses in football, ranking second in DVOA. If McDonald ends up in Chicago or Detroit, that's a contender in the NFC getting a pass rusher the 49ers could have had. That's part of the calculation too. Sometimes a deadline move is about what you add, and sometimes it's about who you keep away from your rivals.

The Mexico City game deserves special attention. Both teams came into last weekend at 6 and 1, and the winner will have a big edge in the race for the number one seed, which comes with the only first-round bye in the conference and home-field advantage through the NFC Championship game. Against a defense that good, every pass rusher and every receiver matters. That's what makes today so important.

McCaffrey, Kittle, Warner, Bosa and now Buckner are all in the later stages of their primes. This window won't stay open forever. When you're 7 and 1, first in the NFC, and two and a half games up in your division, the deadline is the moment to take one more smart swing. Not a reckless one. A smart one.
