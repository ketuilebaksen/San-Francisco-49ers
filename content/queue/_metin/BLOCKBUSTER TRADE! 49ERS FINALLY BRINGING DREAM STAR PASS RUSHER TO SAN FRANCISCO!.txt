Okay, now let's dive into the first big story. Why isn't Nick Bosa on injured reserve?

When Bosa pulled his calf during warmups at practice last week, the natural move would've been to put him on IR. He's expected to miss a few weeks, and IR would open up a roster spot. But the 49ers decided not to do it, and NBC Sports Bay Area's Matt Maiocco explained why. It's all about that magic number: eight.

Teams can activate a maximum of eight players from injured reserve during the regular season. And according to Maiocco, the 49ers already expect eight specific players on IR to come back at some point this year. Let me run through them, because this list tells you just how brutal this start has been.

Wide receiver Christian Kirk, with a calf injury. Wide receiver Demarcus Robinson, with a high ankle sprain from the Miami game. Rookie receiver De'Zhaun Stribling, with his ankle. Offensive lineman Austen Pleasants, with a knee injury. Defensive tackle C.J. West, also with a knee. Defensive end Sam Okuayinonu, with a foot injury. Linebacker Nick Martin, with a hamstring. And cornerback Nate Hobbs, with a groin injury.

That's eight. Every single return slot is already spoken for. And that's only three games into a 17-game season.

So think about what putting Bosa on IR would actually mean. Eventually, when he's healthy, the Niners would have to burn one of those eight activations to bring him back. And that means one of the players on that list, maybe Kirk, maybe Hobbs, maybe West, would have to stay on IR for the rest of the year. Same problem with James Thompson Jr., the rookie defensive tackle who has a high ankle sprain. Put him on IR, and someone else loses their way back.

So the 49ers are doing the smart thing, even if it's uncomfortable. They're keeping Bosa and Thompson on the active roster and simply listing them as inactive on game days while they heal. It costs them a roster spot each week. But it protects the long-term plan, and it keeps those precious IR activations available for later in the season.

And honestly, that tells you everything about how serious this injury situation has become. The Niners aren't dealing with one or two bumps and bruises. They're managing a whole roster's worth of players who could all matter in November and December. Every new injury now forces a decision. Every IR move has to be weighed against the bigger picture. There's almost no room for error left. And remember how IR works. Once a player goes on it, he has to miss at least four games. So if the Niners had put Bosa on IR last week, he'd be locked out through Denver, Seattle, and Washington, no matter how fast his calf heals. By keeping him on the active roster, they keep the option of getting him back the moment he's ready, even if that's just two or three weeks from now. For a player who changes every game he plays in, that flexibility matters.

Alright, story number two. And this is the one that's got the fan base talking. Joey Bosa.

With Nick hurt and the defensive line falling apart, his older brother has suddenly become a name worth discussing again. Joey Bosa is a free agent. His contract with the Buffalo Bills ran out after last season. He has not officially retired. And he hasn't signed with another team. So in theory, the door is still open.

And just imagine the storyline. Joey Bosa walks into Levi's Stadium, puts on the red and gold, and lines up on the same defensive line as his little brother. Their mom, Cheryl, gets to watch both of her sons play for the same NFL team. Kyle Shanahan has made no secret over the years of how much he'd love to have both Bosas on his defense, and there were reports this offseason connecting Joey to San Francisco. And this isn't just about family. Look at Joey's résumé. He was the third overall pick in the 2016 draft. He won Defensive Rookie of the Year that season. He's a multiple-time Pro Bowler. Over 122 career games with the Chargers and the Bills, he's piled up 77 sacks, 372 tackles, and 22 forced fumbles. That's a serious body of work. And last season in Buffalo, at 30 years old, he still had five sacks and kept punching the ball out. One of the highlights of his year was a strip-sack of Aaron Rodgers against the Steelers. The guy has always had a knack for taking the football away. And think about the family numbers for a second. Nick Bosa is a former Defensive Player of the Year with more than 64 career sacks of his own. Put the two brothers together, and you're talking about well over 140 career sacks on the same defensive line. Two former top-three picks, both from Ohio State, both with the same relentless motor. On paper, that's a nightmare for any offensive tackle.

During the offseason, Joey was linked to a whole bunch of teams: Dallas, Green Bay, New England, Chicago, Baltimore, and yes, San Francisco.
