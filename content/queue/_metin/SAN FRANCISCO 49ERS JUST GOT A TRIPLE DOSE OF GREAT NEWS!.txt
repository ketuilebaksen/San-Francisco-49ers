For five straight games, the San Francisco 49ers have played without the most feared pass rusher on their roster. For two months, one of their most reliable veteran receivers has been stuck on injured reserve. And for their entire season, a starting-caliber cornerback has been sitting on the sideline, waiting for his shot. On Wednesday, all three of those stories changed on the same afternoon. Nick Bosa walked back onto the practice field. Demarcus Robinson's practice window was officially opened. And so was Nate Hobbs's. That's a triple dose of good news for a 6 and 1 team that's already leading its division, and it came just four days before the Raiders come to town and six days before the trade deadline. But there's a lot more to unpack here, because not every update on Wednesday was good, and the trade market just gave us a fascinating clue about what John Lynch is really going to do before Tuesday. So let's get into all of it.

Let's start with the headliner. Nick Bosa is back at practice. After missing the last five games with a calf injury, Bosa was a limited participant on Wednesday, the team's first practice after the bye week. Kyle Shanahan said Bosa has made progress, and the plan was for him to work in a limited role. Now, a limited practice on Wednesday doesn't guarantee he plays on Sunday. We still need to see Thursday's and Friday's reports, and the team will make the final call based on how that calf responds to real football movements. But this is the first concrete step we've seen in weeks, and it lines up with what Matt Zenitz reported before the bye, when he said there was a high level of optimism that Bosa could return for Week 9.

Let's remember how this started. Bosa hurt his left calf during warmups before a practice back on September 24th. Before that, he had played two games, with seven tackles and no sacks. Not his usual production, but you could tell offenses were already game planning around him. Since then, the 49ers have gone 4 and 1 without him. Think about how impressive that is. They beat Arizona, Denver, Seattle and Washington while their best defensive player watched from the sideline. The only loss came in Atlanta, in a game where the offense turned the ball over four times.

Now imagine what the defense looks like with Bosa back. He changes everything before the ball is even snapped. Offenses have to slide their protection toward him. They have to keep a tight end or a running back in to chip him. Every one of those adjustments creates a one-on-one somewhere else on the line. And now there's someone waiting to take advantage of those one-on-ones: DeForest Buckner. Bosa on the edge, Buckner pushing the pocket from the inside, Osa Odighizuwa next to him. Quarterbacks don't have a clean place to step up, and they don't have a clean place to escape. That's the kind of front that wins in January.

But I'll say what I've said before: a calf injury is all about explosion. Bosa's game is built on that first step. If the calf isn't fully ready, he isn't fully Bosa. The 49ers have a two-game lead in the NFC West. They don't need to rush him. If he needs one more week, I'd rather see that than watch him aggravate it and miss another month. So this is great news, but it's the beginning of a return, not the end of one.

Good news number two: Demarcus Robinson. The 49ers opened his practice window off injured reserve on Wednesday, and he was a limited participant. Robinson suffered a high ankle sprain back in Week 2, and he's been out ever since. Now that the window is open, the team has 21 days to activate him. Shanahan said they want to see him in full action before making that decision, which makes sense. High ankle sprains are tricky, and receivers need to be able to cut hard and explode out of breaks.

Why does Robinson matter so much right now? Because this receiver room is thin. Deebo Samuel and Mike Evans both missed Wednesday's practice. Christian Kirk was limited with a knee issue. Ricky Pearsall is out for the season after PCL surgery. So a veteran like Robinson, who knows how to run routes, knows how to block in the run game, and has been in big games before, is more than just depth. He might be a starter on Sunday if things break a certain way.

Good news number three: Nate Hobbs. The 49ers also opened Hobbs's practice window. He's been out since an adductor injury on August 11th, before the season even started. He was limited on Wednesday, and like Robinson, the team now has a 21-day window to activate him. Hobbs is a physical, aggressive cornerback who can play inside or outside, and he's strong against the run. With injuries piling up in the secondary this season, getting him back adds real depth to a group that's been stretched thin.

And there's a bonus piece of good news that didn't get as much attention. Mykel Williams, last year's first-round pick, was a full participant despite a knee issue. That's another young pass rusher available to rotate with Bosa, Buckner and the rest of the defensive line. The defense is getting healthier fast.

Now, here's the part that's less fun, and I want to be honest about it. Not everybody was on the field on Wednesday. Christian McCaffrey didn't practice. He's dealing with a mild hamstring strain from the Atlanta game. But Shanahan said there was a chance McCaffrey could return to practice on a limited basis Thursday. That's the next thing to watch. If McCaffrey is on the field Thursday, his chances of playing Sunday go way up. If he's not, the 49ers might lean on their depth at running back against the Raiders.

Mike Evans also didn't practice. He aggravated his Achilles tendinitis right before halftime against Washington on October 19th, and he was inactive for the Atlanta game. He's also listed with a rib issue. Shanahan said Evans has made a lot of progress in physical therapy, but that they're not going to overdo it. That tells me the team is being cautious, which is exactly what you want with a veteran receiver and an Achilles. Evans is a big part of what this offense wants to be in the red zone, but you don't risk an Achilles in November.

And then there's Deebo Samuel. Deebo injured his left hamstring against Seattle on October 11th. Before that, he had 16 catches for 206 yards and three touchdowns, plus 78 rushing yards on nine carries, in five games. He did not practice Wednesday, and Shanahan said he'd be surprised if Deebo plays this week. So realistically, plan on the 49ers being without Deebo against the Raiders.

A few other names to know. Safety Siran Neal didn't practice. Trent Williams got a rest day, which is normal for a veteran left tackle. Linebackers Garret Wallow and Luke Gifford, safety Marques Sigle and cornerback Upton Stout were all limited. Defensive tackle C.J. West will likely sit this week, with his status reassessed next week. And rookie receiver De'Zhaun Stribling remains on injured reserve, with Shanahan saying he's probably more than a month away.

So let's put that into perspective. The defense is getting healthier fast. Bosa, Hobbs, Mykel Williams, and a handful of linebackers and safeties are trending in the right direction. The offense is still waiting. McCaffrey is the big question for Sunday. Evans is a maybe. Deebo is unlikely. And that's exactly why the front office is still shopping for a receiver.

Alright, now let's talk about the trade deadline, because it's getting closer and the picture is getting clearer. Jordan Schultz reported that the 49ers are still trying to make trades, and that they're looking for help at two spots: edge rusher and wide receiver. We also saw the Jauan Jennings situation, where the 49ers had a trade in place with Minnesota before Jennings declined to get on a Zoom call with John Lynch and Kyle Shanahan. That told us San Francisco is active. Now we know it's still active, at two positions.

So here's the question I want to ask you, and it's a different one than usual. Not who do you want. Who do you think this front office is actually going to get? Because answering that question means thinking like John Lynch, not like a fan.

Let's start with the receiver side, and the name everyone keeps connecting to San Francisco: Josh Downs. Downs asked the Colts for a trade last week, according to ESPN's Jeremy Fowler. Ian Rapoport of NFL Network reported on November 1st that the Colts want a third-round pick for him. And then Adam Schefter added something really interesting. He said the Colts want a three, but nobody's at a three. Maybe they'll get a team to a four. He even said it wouldn't surprise him if it ended up being a four, and that teams want to give up a five.

Here's why that's so fascinating. If any team in the league were willing to pay a third-round pick for Josh Downs, he'd already be on that team's roster. The Colts put a price on him, and nobody has met it. That tells you the real market is lower. Probably a fourth-round pick. Maybe a fifth, or a swap of late-round picks. And that's a price range the 49ers can actually work with.

Now, one important note. There's no confirmed report that the 49ers have made an offer for Downs. He's being linked to them because of the fit and the need, but he's a name in the rumor mill, not a done deal. Still, the logic is easy to follow. Downs is 25 years old, on the last year of his rookie contract, and through seven games he had 26 catches on 46 targets for a team-high 307 yards. He's only five-foot-nine, and he ran a 4.48 forty, which isn't blazing. But he separates well. He's quick in and out of breaks, he wins in tight windows, and he moves well after the catch. In Shanahan's offense, getting open on time matters more than raw speed. He could also add value in the return game, which matters for a special teams unit that has struggled.

And here's the bigger picture that I think matters most. The 49ers are shopping for two players, not one. If they were going all in on a superstar, they probably wouldn't be looking at a second position at the same time. Think about it like a grocery bill. You don't put a thousand dollars of steak in your cart if you still need to buy the rest of the week's groceries. Shopping at two spots suggests a value approach: a couple of smart, efficient moves instead of one blockbuster. That fits with everything we've seen. The Buckner trade cost a fourth-round pick. Matt Maiocco suggested a Khalil Mack deal might cost something like a late-round pick or a swap of late picks. And Schefter thinks a Downs deal lands around a fourth. Do you see the pattern? This front office is hunting for value, not fireworks.

Of course, that could change. There's no rule that says Lynch can't make a big swing. The only thing holding him back is his own long-term plan, and the cap situation down the road. But the shape of the shopping list tells me what kind of week this is going to be.

Now let's talk about the edge rusher side, where Khalil Mack keeps coming up. Mack signed a one-year, 18-million-dollar deal with the Chargers in March, so his contract expires after this season. Los Angeles has had a brutal year and is still searching for its first win, which is exactly the kind of situation where a veteran on an expiring deal gets moved. Mack has three sacks, 21 tackles, five tackles for loss and six quarterback hits in seven games. Jordan Schultz said back in October that everybody wants a rusher, and Mack is still disruptive. He's not the player he was at his peak, but he's still a smart, physical edge setter who can get home.

Here's how the fit would work. With Bosa returning, Mack wouldn't need to carry the pass rush. He'd be the third option, the guy who makes offenses pay for sliding toward Bosa. And with Sam Okuayinonu out until January, the edge rotation could use another proven veteran. The counterargument is that if Bosa comes back fully healthy and Mykel Williams keeps developing, the need for Mack shrinks. And the 49ers may not want to give up more 2027 picks after the Buckner trade. So it's a real possibility, but I wouldn't call it likely yet.

Now let's talk about a couple of roster moves that slipped under the radar. On October 30th, the Indianapolis Colts signed defensive tackle Kevin Givens off the 49ers' practice squad and onto their 53-man roster. Givens was a true homegrown 49er. He signed as an undrafted free agent in 2019 and spent seven seasons with San Francisco, playing in 71 regular season games with 14 starts, 95 tackles and eight sacks, plus nine playoff games. He was released on October 24th and re-signed to the practice squad that same day. Then, just days after the Buckner trade, the Colts came calling. It makes sense for Indianapolis. They'd just traded away a starting defensive tackle, and Givens gives them a reliable rotational body. He's a professional, he's been through big games, and every Niner fan should wish him the best.

A couple of days later, defensive tackle Naquan Jones left the practice squad for the Houston Texans. That opened up another spot, and the 49ers filled one of them with a familiar face: KhaDarel Hodge.

Let's talk about Hodge for a second, because I think he's a bigger addition than people realize. Hodge is a former special teams Pro Bowler from his five seasons in Atlanta. He's played in 118 career games. He's a gunner, the guy who sprints down the field on punts and makes the tackle before the returner can get started. And that's huge for a special teams unit that has slipped badly. You saw it in Atlanta. The 49ers had real breakdowns on special teams in that game, and in a league where so many games are decided by a field goal, those breakdowns cost you.

As a receiver, Hodge isn't going to blow the doors off a defense. He's not going to show up on highlight reels every week. But he's a physical, reliable body who knows how to block, runs the routes he's asked to run, and makes the routine plays. Kind of a steady presence. The Athletic's Vic Tafur described him as very good on special teams and a physical receiver. When your receiver room is this banged up, that has real value.

Now, let's get to the emotional centerpiece of this week: DeForest Buckner. On Monday, he spoke to reporters for the first time since the trade. And if you haven't heard what he said, it's worth knowing. Buckner said that when he learned the trade was done, he fell to his knees and started praying. He described how unexpected everything was, and how doors you think are closed can open back up.

You don't have to share his faith to appreciate that moment. Every fan wants players who want to be here. And here's a three-time Pro Bowler, a man who's made his money and built his career somewhere else, and his first reaction to coming back to San Francisco was to drop to his knees.

He also told the story of how the reunion happened. He made it clear to the Colts, all the way up to owner Carlie Irsay-Gordon, that if they moved him, San Francisco was where he wanted to be. And they honored it. In his words, at the end of the day, he wanted to be here, and they made it happen. He'd stayed close with George Kittle, Kyle Juszczyk, Nick Bosa, Deebo Samuel, Fred Warner and Dre Greenlaw over the years. And through texts and Pro Bowl trips, they kept telling him the same thing: come back home, bro.

And that brings us to the contrast. Because the same week Buckner talked about falling to his knees with joy, Jauan Jennings turned down a chance to come back. I want to be fair to Jennings. He gave this team a lot. He was a huge part of the 2023 run, including a touchdown pass in the Super Bowl. He stepped up in 2024 when injuries hit the receiver room, becoming the guy almost out of nowhere. And his blocking in the run game was some of the best of any receiver in football, which almost never gets the credit it deserves. Players have reasons we don't always know.

But I'll be honest. When you see a guy like Buckner so excited to come home, and then you see another former 49er refuse even to take a Zoom call, it makes the Jennings story feel a little more bitter. I think it'll take a small bite out of his legacy here. Not a huge one, because what he did on the field still matters. But a little. And here's the twist: the 49ers play the Vikings in Mexico City later this month. There's a decent chance Jennings will be on the other sideline. That's going to be a storyline.

Now back to Buckner, because two more things he said stood out. First, he said he knows the standard here, and it's Super Bowl or bust. He talked about unfinished business, pointing back to the 2023 season, when the 49ers reached the Super Bowl and couldn't finish. He wants to help bring a sixth Lombardi Trophy to this franchise.

Now, a word of caution. Phrases like "Super Bowl or bust" come with risk. This team still has a big chunk of the regular season left, starting with the Raiders on Sunday, and nobody should be planning a parade in November. But it tells you what's on the players' minds. They believe this can be a special season, and they're not hiding from it.

Second, and I think this one matters more than people realize, Buckner talked about the young players in the defensive line room. He said he's still playing at a high level, but he wants to be an open book. If the younger guys want to pick his brain, he's there. He believes the defensive line is the engine of the team, and he wants to pour everything he knows into the young players so they can get better. Think about who that helps. Mykel Williams, a first-round pick in his second season. The young interior linemen. Every rotational player looking to earn more snaps. They now have a 75-and-a-half-sack veteran sitting in the same meeting room every day.

And from what I've seen of his comments, he wasn't specifically asked to talk about being a mentor. He brought it up himself. That tells you a lot about his character, and about how he's thinking about the final years of his career. That's a player who wants to win now and leave the room better than he found it.

Let's quickly touch on one more story: Brandon Aiyuk. Aiyuk has hired Drew Rosenhaus, one of the most powerful agents in football, and reports say he wants to play in the NFL again. But the path is complicated. Aiyuk is still on the 49ers' reserve/left squad list, and the first step in any return would be a formal request to be reinstated. The 49ers voided the remaining guarantees on his contract in 2025, and he hasn't played in an NFL game in more than two years.

Let me be very clear: Aiyuk is not coming back to play for the 49ers. The team has said he'll never play for them again. If he completes the reinstatement process, San Francisco could try to trade him, but the most likely outcome is a release. A lot of fans said all offseason that the team should have just cut ties, and this could stay a headache for a little while longer. But it has nothing to do with this week's game, and nothing to do with this season's title chances.

So let's look at the Raiders. Las Vegas comes into Levi's Stadium at 4 and 4 after beating the Jets 38 to 33 to snap a four-game losing streak. Brock Bowers had seven catches for 92 yards and two touchdowns in that win, and rookie quarterback Fernando Mendoza, the first overall pick, is getting more comfortable every week. The Raiders' Wednesday injury report was light: cornerback Darien Porter missed practice with a groin injury, and defensive tackles Thomas Booker and Folorunso Fatukasi, along with tight end Ian Thomas, were limited.

But the matchup that jumps off the page is the 49ers' run game against the Raiders' run defense. San Francisco has the best offensive success rate in the league at 52.7 percent, and it's the only team with a rushing success rate above 50 percent. The Raiders rank 27th in defensive DVOA, and they've struggled against physical rushing attacks. In that win over the Jets, Braelon Allen ran for 121 yards on 18 carries in the first half alone. Whether McCaffrey plays or not, Shanahan will want to test that front early.

On the other side, Maxx Crosby is still one of the most dangerous defensive players in football. The 49ers will need to give him extra attention, with chips from tight ends and backs, quick throws, and runs aimed away from him. And if Bosa does play, the 49ers will have their own game-wrecker on the other side, which makes this matchup feel a lot more balanced.

Let's also zoom out and look at the rest of November, because it explains why these returns matter so much. After the Raiders, the 49ers go on the road to Dallas, whose defense ranks 31st in DVOA. Then they face the Minnesota Vikings in Mexico City, and the Vikings have the number two defense in the league by that same measure. And then Seattle comes to Levi's Stadium on November 30th, with the division on the line. Two softer defenses, followed by two of the toughest tests on the schedule. If Bosa, Robinson and Hobbs are back up to speed by the time the Vikings and Seahawks games arrive, this team will be in a much stronger position than it was in Atlanta.

And remember what this offense has done even with its depth chart in shambles. Brock Purdy threw 15 touchdowns and only four interceptions in the first seven games. On Monday Night Football against Washington, his career passer rating of 105.6 became the highest in NFL history among quarterbacks with at least 1,500 attempts. Christian McCaffrey leads every running back in the league in EPA per rush. The 49ers rank first in DVOA. They've done all of that while juggling a receiver room that has looked different almost every week. So when people ask whether the 49ers need a big trade, I think the honest answer is that they need the right trade. A receiver who can separate and help in the return game. Maybe a veteran edge rusher for depth. Not a blockbuster that empties the draft cupboard.

There's also the Garrett Wilson question, which many fans are still asking about. Adam Schefter reported last week that a source told him there was no way the Jets would trade Wilson, and that ownership loves him. Wilson also said publicly that he wants to stay in New York. So as of right now, Wilson isn't a realistic target, no matter how many trade machine screenshots you see on social media. That's another reason a player like Downs, with a clear trade request and a falling price tag, makes so much more sense for San Francisco.

And here's one more thing to watch: the Thursday and Friday injury reports. On Thursday, the coordinators, Morris and Kubiak, along with Brock Purdy, all speak to the media, and the practice report comes out in the afternoon. That's when we'll know whether McCaffrey was able to return in a limited role. Friday, Shanahan speaks again and the final game statuses are released. If Bosa goes from limited to full this week, that's a strong sign he's playing. If McCaffrey practices at all, that's a strong sign he has a chance. Those reports will tell us more than any headline. And if you see a headline before then claiming everyone is officially back, take a breath and wait for the report.
