Let's start with the weekend that happened while the 49ers were on the couch. A bye week has a strange effect on the standings. A team that doesn't play can still gain ground because its rivals lose. It's one of the few situations in football where doing nothing is the best possible move. But it's also a reminder that the standings are relative. The 49ers didn't get better or worse because other teams lost. But their path to the top seed got easier, and that has real value over a long season.

The result that matters most for San Francisco came on Monday Night Football. The Chicago Bears beat the Seattle Seahawks 34 to 17. D'Andre Swift ran for three touchdowns. Caleb Williams threw a short touchdown pass to Luther Burden. And Jeffery Simmons, the defensive tackle the Bears just traded for, made an immediate impact up front. Seattle's run game managed only 81 yards, and Sam Darnold threw for 188 yards and two touchdowns, but it was nowhere near enough. The defending Super Bowl champions are now 4 and 4.

What does that mean for the NFC West? It means the 49ers, at 6 and 1, now lead Seattle by two and a half games. The Rams also sit at 4 and 4 after beating the Chargers 24 to 9 on Sunday. So the 49ers are two and a half games clear of both teams chasing them. That's a real cushion at the midpoint of the season.

Now, I want to be careful not to dismiss Seattle. This is a talented team, and they're the defending champions for a reason. But right now they don't look like a team playing like a contender. The offense leans very heavily on Jaxon Smith-Njigba. And when a team relies that much on one receiver, it can become easier to defend. An opponent can bracket the star, bring pressure, and force the rest of the offense to prove it can win. When the run game isn't strong enough to punish that approach, the whole offense becomes predictable. That's not a knock on Smith-Njigba, who is excellent. It's an observation about balance. And balance is what separates teams that win in January from teams that don't.

There's also a running back story here. Kenneth Walker, who was a key part of Seattle's offense, is now in Kansas City. He had a monster debut for the Chiefs back in Week 1, and he's become a big part of their offense. And watching Seattle's run game this season, you can see how much they miss him. That's a big change from the team that won it all last year.

Speaking of the Chiefs, they lost too. Denver beat Kansas City 28 to 24 on Sunday, sealing the win with a goal-line stand on fourth down when Patrick Mahomes was brought down short of the end zone. I'm still not ready to count the Chiefs out of the contender conversation. You never sleep on Mahomes. But their offense has looked very dependent on Walker, and their receiver group has struggled badly with injuries. And here's why that game matters to the Faithful: the 49ers beat that same Denver team 24 to 14 back in Week 4. Every time the Broncos win a game like that, the 49ers' win looks better.

Then there's Minnesota. The Vikings came into the weekend at 6 and 0, the last unbeaten team in the NFC. They lost 19 to 15 in Detroit. And with that loss, the 49ers moved into the number one seed in the NFC. That matters for two reasons. First, the top seed is the only one that gets a bye in the playoffs and home field all the way through the NFC Championship game. Second, the 49ers play the Vikings later this month in Mexico City. That game could end up deciding who holds the top seed.

And then there's Dallas. The Cowboys lost to the Arizona Cardinals 33 to 30 in overtime after an incredible comeback by Arizona. Now, that was one of those funny weekends where somebody you don't love was going to lose either way. From an NFC West standpoint, a Cardinals loss would have been better for the 49ers. But I'll admit, I don't mind watching Dallas lose. And it's a team the 49ers face on the road in just over a week.

So, let's put it together. A rested 49ers team, no new injuries from a game, a new veteran defensive tackle in the building, and results across the league that mostly went San Francisco's way. The only thing that would have made it perfect is a Rams loss, and that didn't happen. But it was a great weekend.

Now, let's look at the NFC playoff picture if the season ended today. The 49ers are the number one seed at 6 and 1. The Vikings are second at 6 and 1. The New York Giants lead the NFC East at 4, 2 and 1, and the Carolina Panthers lead the NFC South at 3 and 4. The wild card spots would go to the Chicago Bears at 6 and 2, the Detroit Lions at 5 and 2, and the Green Bay Packers at 5 and 3. Look at that again. All four NFC North teams would be in the playoffs. That would be a wild outcome. A crowded wild card race means tiebreakers start to matter, head-to-head results, conference record, strength of victory, and a single upset can reorder everything. For the 49ers, the goal is simple: win the division, avoid the tiebreaker chaos, and keep stacking wins toward that top seed.

And here's the beautiful part. While all that chaos was happening, the 49ers stayed healthy. A bye week is valuable for any team, but for a roster that has dealt with this many injuries, it's even more important. A team that arrives rested has an advantage over a team that's been playing every Sunday. And Shanahan himself pointed out that the 49ers have historically played well after the bye, while acknowledging that rest doesn't solve every problem.

Now let's talk about the huge weapon. Because the biggest news of the week isn't a trade. It's Christian McCaffrey. After missing Wednesday's practice with a mild hamstring strain, McCaffrey was back on the field Thursday as a limited participant. That's exactly what Shanahan said he hoped would happen. And it puts McCaffrey on track to potentially play against the Raiders on Sunday. Nothing is official until Friday's final injury report and game statuses, but the trend is good.

Why does that matter so much? Because McCaffrey is the engine of this offense. The 49ers have the best offensive success rate in the NFL at 52.7 percent. They're the only team with a rushing success rate above 50 percent. And McCaffrey leads every running back in the league in EPA per rush. Those numbers tell you he's not just a name on the depth chart. He's the reason this offense moves the chains more consistently than anybody in football. When he's on the field, defenses have to account for him on every snap, as a runner, as a receiver out of the backfield, and as a decoy.

And the matchup is perfect. The Raiders rank 27th in defensive DVOA, and they've struggled against physical run games. In their win over the Jets, Braelon Allen ran for 121 yards on 18 carries in the first half alone. If McCaffrey plays, I'd expect Shanahan to test that front from the opening drive.

Nick Bosa is also trending in the right direction. He was limited again on Thursday, his second straight practice after missing five games with a calf injury. Shanahan said Bosa has made progress. If he plays Sunday, even on a snap count, the defensive front with Bosa, DeForest Buckner and Osa Odighizuwa is going to look very different from the one that played in Atlanta.

There's more good news on defense. Nate Hobbs, who's been out since an adductor injury in August, practiced in a limited role again and could make his season debut. Upton Stout is trending toward returning to his starting nickel spot. Mykel Williams was a full participant. And Demarcus Robinson, back from a high ankle sprain, was limited again on Thursday. The team opened his practice window this week, and it has 21 days to activate him.

But here's the bad news, and I want to be honest about it. The receiver room took another hit. Christian Kirk didn't practice Thursday because of a knee injury. That makes three top receivers missing from practice: Kirk, Mike Evans and Deebo Samuel. And general manager John Lynch said it's probably likely that Evans won't be out there Sunday, and that neither Evans nor Deebo is likely to play this weekend. The team is being cautious with Evans' Achilles so he can be healthy for the stretch run. And Deebo is still working back from a significant hamstring strain he suffered in the win over Seattle.

So let's think about what that leaves. Jordan Watkins. Jacob Cowing. Brandin Cooks. Possibly Demarcus Robinson if he's activated. George Kittle at tight end. And McCaffrey out of the backfield. That's a thin group to throw to against a defense with Maxx Crosby. And it explains exactly why the front office keeps calling around before the deadline.

Alright, now let's talk about the trade deadline, because it's Tuesday at 4 p.m. Eastern, and the 49ers are clearly still active. Jordan Schultz reported that San Francisco is still looking for a receiver and a pass rusher, and that he wouldn't rule out another move. And Kyle Shanahan confirmed this week that the team is looking at receivers and, in his words, every other position too.

But Shanahan also said something important about receivers, and I think it explains why the 49ers haven't made a big move yet. He said it's difficult to add a player in the middle of the season who can learn the offense quickly, especially at receiver. This offense asks a lot of its receivers. They have to know the timing, the motions, the blocking responsibilities, the option routes. You can't just plug anyone in. And Shanahan said that adding another player would be great, but the team is not counting on it. He also said that adding Buckner was really awesome, and that he believes this team is better than it was last year.

Now, this is where the Jauan Jennings story gets an important correction. Earlier this week, the report was that Jennings declined to get on a Zoom call with Lynch and Shanahan, and that killed the trade. Shanahan said that report was accurate but incomplete. He said he and Jennings actually spoke over FaceTime, and Jennings told him he was pretty happy where he is. The 49ers respected that decision. So it wasn't quite the cold shoulder some people made it out to be. It was a player who's settled in Minnesota and wanted to stay. You can still be disappointed, and I understand why a lot of fans are. But it's fair to give Jennings that context.

So, who could the 49ers actually get? Let's go through the names.

First, Josh Downs. He requested a trade from the Colts, and Ian Rapoport reported the Colts want a third-round pick. But Adam Schefter said on Wednesday that nobody is at a three, that teams want to give up a five, and that it wouldn't surprise him if the deal ends up at a four. That's interesting information. If any team had been willing to pay a third, Downs would already be gone. So the real market seems to be a fourth or fifth-round pick. Downs is 25, on the final year of his rookie contract, and had 26 catches for a team-high 307 yards through seven games. He's a quick separator who wins in tight windows, which fits what Shanahan wants. There's no confirmed report that the 49ers have made an offer, but the price is moving into a range that would make sense for them.

Second, a much bigger name: George Pickens. Jordan Schultz said Pickens is a major trade name and that teams are getting curious. Pickens is playing on the franchise tag in Dallas, and he's been used less this season. Last year, he had 93 catches for 1,429 yards and nine touchdowns and earned his first Pro Bowl. This year, he has 39 catches for just over 500 yards and two touchdowns. Now, I want to be clear. There's no report that the 49ers are in talks for Pickens. Schultz mentioned the 49ers are still looking for a receiver, and he mentioned Pickens is available, but those are two separate points. And a player on the franchise tag comes with a big salary and a big question about next year's contract. But if you're asking who the true "huge weapon" on the market might be, Pickens is the name that would change the conversation. And remember, the 49ers face Dallas on the road next week. If the Cowboys do decide to sell, it would be fascinating timing.

Third, Garrett Wilson. Let's put that one to rest. Adam Schefter reported last week that a source told him there's no way the Jets trade Wilson, and that ownership loves him. Wilson also said publicly that he wants to stay. So unless something dramatic changes, Wilson isn't a realistic target.

And on the pass rush side, Khalil Mack remains the name to watch. He's on a one-year, 18-million-dollar deal with the Chargers that expires after the season. He has three sacks, 21 tackles and six quarterback hits in seven games. And Matt Maiocco of NBC Sports Bay Area said he wouldn't rule out a deal, possibly for a late-round pick or a swap of late picks.

Here's my read on all of this. The 49ers are shopping at two positions, which tells me they're looking for value, not a blockbuster. Think about it like a grocery bill. If you have to buy everything for the week, you're not putting a thousand dollars of steak in your cart. The Buckner trade cost a fourth-round pick. Downs might cost a fourth. Mack might cost a late pick. That's the pattern. Smart, efficient moves to raise the ceiling without sacrificing the future. A Pickens trade would break that pattern, which is exactly why I'd call it unlikely, even if it's fun to imagine.

Now, let's talk about the analytics, because they tell you this team doesn't need to panic. The 49ers are ranked first in DVOA, which measures efficiency adjusted for opponent. They're near the top of the league in EPA per play on offense, and they're also strong in defensive EPA per play allowed. Special teams is the one area where they haven't been near the top, and that's why bringing back KhaDarel Hodge, a former special teams Pro Bowler, to the practice squad matters more than it looks.

They're also near the top of the league in point differential. I'd put a small asterisk on that, though. The Jacksonville Jaguars, who are just ahead of them in that category, got a big boost by beating the Colts 42 to 7 over the weekend. Point differential can be inflated by one or two blowouts. That's why I like EPA and success rate more. They measure performance on every snap, not just the final score. And when the efficiency numbers and the point differential tell the same story, it's a good sign the record isn't a fluke.

Some people will say the 49ers have had an easier schedule. I don't buy that framing. They beat the Rams 27 to 7. They went into Seattle and won 32 to 29. When they beat Seattle, the Seahawks were the defending champs and looked like a real threat. Now Seattle looks beatable, but you can't go back and discount a win after the fact. You don't know who a team will be at the moment you play them. A win is a win, and road wins over division rivals count double in my book.

And here's the scary part for the rest of the league. The 49ers built all of this while missing a huge chunk of their roster. Bosa missed five games. Evans and Deebo have been in and out. McCaffrey hurt his hamstring in Atlanta. Ricky Pearsall is out for the season after PCL surgery. And still, they're 6 and 1, first in DVOA, and first in success rate. Brock Purdy has 15 touchdowns and just four interceptions. And on Monday Night Football against Washington, his career passer rating of 105.6 became the highest in NFL history among quarterbacks with at least 1,500 attempts.

Now imagine the second half of the season with the reinforcements. Evans changes how defenses cover the field. Deebo adds a completely different kind of weapon. Robinson gives Purdy another veteran option. And Bosa restores the pass rush to full strength next to Buckner. A team that was already first in DVOA while missing those players could be even more dangerous once they're healthy.

But I'll add one word of caution. Players coming back from injury don't always make an immediate impact. They need to rebuild rhythm and conditioning. A receiver who's been out for weeks might need a game or two to get back up to speed. A pass rusher coming off a calf injury might be limited in snaps. The staff will probably ease some of them in, which is the right approach if the goal is to be healthy in January. So the benefits might show up gradually, not all at once.

And let's not forget the move the 49ers already made, because it might end up being the most important one of the deadline. DeForest Buckner is back in red and gold, wearing number 99 again, after San Francisco sent a 2027 fourth-round pick to Indianapolis. He's 32, a three-time Pro Bowler, and he had 47 sacks during his time with the Colts, including four already this season. He's on an expiring deal worth about 7.9 million dollars.

When he spoke to reporters on Monday, Buckner said that when he learned the deal was done, he fell to his knees and started praying. He said he'd made it clear to the Colts that if they were going to move him, San Francisco was where he wanted to be. He called this season Super Bowl or bust, pointing back to the unfinished business of the 2023 season. And he said he wants to be an open book for the young players in the defensive line room, because he believes the defensive line is the engine of the team. Over the years, his old teammates, Kittle, Juszczyk, Bosa, Deebo, Warner and Greenlaw, kept telling him the same thing: come back home, bro. Now he's home.

For anyone newer to the Faithful, here's why that reunion means so much. Buckner was the seventh overall pick in 2016 and a cornerstone of the defensive line that reached the Super Bowl after the 2019 season. In 2020, the 49ers traded him to Indianapolis for the 13th overall pick, which became Javon Kinlaw. John Lynch later called that trade probably the toughest thing he'd done as general manager. Six years later, he gets to undo it for a fourth-round pick. And if you're wondering whether the Buckner trade had ripple effects, it did. A few days after the deal, the Colts signed defensive tackle Kevin Givens off the 49ers' practice squad. Givens had spent seven seasons in San Francisco after signing as an undrafted free agent in 2019, with 71 games and eight sacks. Another practice squad defensive tackle, Naquan Jones, left for the Houston Texans. And one of those open spots went to KhaDarel Hodge.

There was also one more update on Brandon Aiyuk this week. Aiyuk has hired Drew Rosenhaus and reportedly wants to return to the NFL, but he's still on the 49ers' reserve/left squad list and would need to be reinstated first. Asked about it, Shanahan said he has no expectation that Aiyuk will be back, and that mentally he has moved on. When pressed, he added that there are no absolutes in life. But the takeaway is clear: the 49ers aren't counting on Aiyuk, and he isn't part of their plans for this season. The most likely outcome, if he's reinstated, is a trade or a release.

Now, let's zoom in on the Vikings game, because after this weekend, it might be the most important regular season game on the 49ers' schedule. Both teams are 6 and 1. The 49ers hold the top seed right now, and the Vikings sit right behind them. Minnesota has one of the best defenses in the league, ranking second in defensive DVOA. And it's being played in Mexico City, a neutral site, so there's no home-field edge. Head-to-head results are one of the first tiebreakers between teams with the same record. If the 49ers win that game, they'd hold the inside track to the top seed for the rest of the season. And yes, there's a good chance Jauan Jennings will be on the other sideline. That's going to add some extra drama.

And then, on November 30th, Seattle comes to Levi's Stadium. The Seahawks are 4 and 4 and fading right now, but they're still the defending champions with a lot of talent, and nothing would help them more than a win in Santa Clara. The 49ers already beat them once in Seattle. A sweep would essentially put the NFC West out of reach before December even starts.

Let's take a quick look at the Raiders before we wrap up. Las Vegas comes in at 4 and 4 after snapping a four-game losing streak with a 38 to 33 win over the Jets. Brock Bowers had seven catches for 92 yards and two touchdowns in that game, and rookie quarterback Fernando Mendoza, the first overall pick, is growing week by week. On their Thursday injury report, defensive tackle Thomas Booker didn't practice with an ankle injury, cornerback Darien Porter was limited with a groin injury, and receiver Dont'e Thornton was limited with a back issue.

The keys for the 49ers are simple. Run the ball, especially if McCaffrey plays. Keep Maxx Crosby from wrecking the game, with chips from tight ends and backs, quick throws, and runs aimed away from him. Contain Brock Bowers, which will likely fall on Fred Warner and the linebackers. And, most importantly, protect the football. Four turnovers lost the Atlanta game. A clean game against a team that just gave up 33 points to the Jets should be enough.

And then look at what comes next. After the Raiders, it's a trip to Dallas, a Cowboys team that just lost in overtime and ranks near the bottom in defensive DVOA. Then the Vikings in Mexico City, with the top seed potentially on the line. Then Seattle at home on November 30th. If the 49ers take care of business against the Raiders and the Cowboys, they could be 8 and 1 heading into the biggest stretch of their season.

Let me give you the other side of the argument too, because a good debate goes both ways. Some fans will say the 49ers shouldn't trade anything else. The roster is already the best in the NFC by record and by DVOA. Buckner was the big move. Players are coming back from injury. Draft picks matter for the future, especially with so many core players getting older. And as Shanahan said himself, it's hard for a new receiver to learn this offense in the middle of the season. Even if everyone comes back eventually, one more injury puts this team right back where it was in Atlanta, throwing to its fourth and fifth options in a game that matters. A reasonable price for a quick separator like Downs is insurance for a Super Bowl run. And insurance is exactly what contenders buy at the deadline.

And that's what makes George Kittle so important this week. With the receiver room this thin, Kittle becomes even more central to everything this offense does. He's the security blanket on third down, the matchup problem in the red zone, and one of the best blocking tight ends in football. Against Atlanta, he had 88 receiving yards when the receivers around him were banged up. Expect Shanahan to lean on him again Sunday.
