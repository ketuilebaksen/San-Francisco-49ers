To understand why everybody is suddenly talking about a backup quarterback, you have to look at what just happened around the league. Giants quarterback Jaxson Dart sprained his MCL on the opening drive of Monday Night Football. The early timeline is four to six weeks, and it could be longer, which leaves New York with Jameis Winston under center. In Washington, Jayden Daniels dislocated his left elbow. That's the second time in two seasons, and Dan Quinn says there's no timetable for his return. Marcus Mariota is their guy for now. In Chicago, Caleb Williams has a hamstring injury and has been ruled out, with head coach Ben Johnson calling it week to week. And in Atlanta, the Falcons are dealing with injuries to both Michael Penix and Tua Tagovailoa, and they've been forced to lean on Cooper Rush. Even in Minnesota, Kyler Murray is hurt and the Vikings are riding with Carson Wentz. That's at least five teams with a starting quarterback problem, and we're only in Week 3.

When starters go down like that, the market for experienced backups explodes. Contenders panic. Desperate teams start picking up the phone. And one name sits at the top of almost every list: Mac Jones.

Let me remind you why. Last season, Brock Purdy missed eight games with a toe injury. Mac Jones stepped in and played in 11 games, including eight starts, and he went 5 and 3 as a starter. He threw for 2,151 yards and 13 touchdowns, completed 69.6 percent of his passes, and kept the Niners' season alive until Purdy came back. That's not a backup who just survives. That's a backup who wins football games. And remember who we're talking about. Mac Jones was a first-round pick of the New England Patriots back in 2021. He made the Pro Bowl as a rookie. Things fell apart in New England, he spent time in Jacksonville, and he came to San Francisco to rebuild his career in Shanahan's system. He did exactly that. Now he's the kind of quarterback other teams can actually build a season around for a few months.

And the league has noticed. Bleacher Report's Kristopher Knox ranked Jones number one on his trade block big board heading into Week 3. Not number five. Number one. He projected Jones as worth a conditional second-round pick, and he named the Falcons and the Commanders as potential landing spots because of their quarterback situations. The Giants are an obvious fit as well now that Dart is down. And this isn't a brand new idea, either. Back in the spring, reports said San Francisco was asking for a second-round pick for Jones and nobody met the price. Now, with quarterbacks dropping all over the league, that price suddenly looks a lot more realistic.

Now, let's make the case for trading him, because it's stronger than you might think.

Number one: the contract. Mac Jones is set to become a free agent after this season. If he plays well, even as a backup, there's a very good chance some team out there offers him a chance to start next year, and he walks away. San Francisco gets nothing. So the question becomes: would you rather have Mac Jones for the rest of 2026, or a second-round pick that helps you for the next four or five years?

Number two: timing. His value will never be higher than it is right now. Three starters just went down in one week. The trade deadline is November 10th. Desperate teams overpay. That's how the NFL works. Some voices in the Bay Area media have already said the Niners should at least be willing to listen if someone comes with a big offer, maybe even a pick plus a player.

Number three: Purdy is rolling. Through two games, he's completing 80.4 percent of his passes, with a 125.6 passer rating, 492 yards, and five touchdowns. If your starter is playing at an MVP level, the backup matters a little less every week.

That's the case for trading. And honestly, if you only look at it from a business perspective, it makes a lot of sense.

But this is Ketuil NFL Stories, and we look at the whole picture. So here's the case for keeping him, and to me, it's the stronger one.

Number one: this franchise's injury luck. You've heard the list today. Six defensive linemen down. Three receivers out. And it's only September. Purdy himself played just nine games last season because of toe and shoulder problems. We have seen what happens to this team when the starting quarterback goes down with nobody reliable behind him. It turns a Super Bowl contender into a team fighting for a wild card spot.

Number two: the window. This roster is built to win right now. Trent Williams is 38 years old. Kittle is 32. McCaffrey is 30. This core isn't going to be together forever. When your Super Bowl window is open, you don't trade away the insurance policy that keeps it open. A second-round pick in April doesn't help you in January.

Number three: Mac Jones has already proven himself here. He knows the system. He knows the receivers. He knows the protections. If something happens to Purdy in November, you're not asking a stranger to learn Shanahan's playbook on the fly. You're handing the ball to a guy who went 5 and 3 in this exact offense last year. That kind of certainty is incredibly hard to find.

And if you need a history lesson on why quarterback depth matters in San Francisco, just look at how Brock Purdy got here in the first place. Back in 2022, Trey Lance broke his ankle in Week 2. Then Jimmy Garoppolo broke his foot in December, ironically against the Miami Dolphins. The Niners were down to their third-string quarterback, a rookie named Brock Purdy, the very last pick of the draft. And he took that team all the way to the NFC Championship Game. This franchise knows better than anyone that you're always one hit away from your backup becoming your season. Sometimes you get lucky and find a Purdy. You can't count on getting lucky twice.

And then there's the trade idea that really got people talking. A fan proposal that's been floating around social media suggested sending Jones to the Giants for defensive end Kayvon Thibodeaux. And with Bosa hurt, I understand why that idea sounds tempting. You need a pass rusher, the Giants need a quarterback. Easy, right?

Not so fast. Look at Thibodeaux's trend. He had 11 and a half sacks in 2023. That dropped to five and a half in 2024. Then just two and a half last season. And availability has been an issue too. He played only 12 games in 2024 and 10 games last season. So you'd be trading your quarterback insurance for a pass rusher whose production has fallen off a cliff and who has struggled to stay on the field. For a team whose biggest weakness is injuries, adding another injury question mark doesn't solve anything.

So here's my take. San Francisco should answer the phone. You always listen. But unless someone makes a truly overwhelming offer, a clean second-round pick or better with no strings attached, Mac Jones stays in red and gold. And the smart play might be to wait. Let's see where this team is in early November. By then, Bosa should be back or close to it. Mykel Williams could be off the PUP list. Kirk should be back at receiver. If the roster is healthy and Purdy is still rolling heading into the deadline, maybe you think about it then. But trading your backup quarterback in September, while half of your roster is in the training room? That's how Super Bowl seasons slip away.
