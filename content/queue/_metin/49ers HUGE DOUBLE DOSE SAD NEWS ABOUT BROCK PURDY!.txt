Let's start with Purdy, because this is the news that changes the week. According to the 49ers' official website, Purdy has been ruled out for Sunday's game. He hurt his ankle in the second quarter of the 35 to 10 win over the Vikings in Mexico City. He stayed in that game and played well enough to be named NFC Offensive Player of the Week, completing 21 of 26 passes for 255 yards, three touchdowns and no interceptions. According to ESPN, he had his ankles taped for the final two quarters. That tells you everything you need to know about his toughness.

And that performance was special. According to ESPN, Minnesota blitzed him on 20 of his 26 dropbacks, the highest rate he's ever faced, and he completed 17 of 19 of those throws for 208 yards and two touchdowns. The 49ers scored 28 points in the first half, more than the Vikings had allowed in any full game this season. According to FOX Sports, it was the first time a Shanahan offense scored more than 17 points against a defense with Brian Flores calling the plays. The crowd in Mexico City chanted "M-V-P." And now we know he did a lot of that on an injured ankle. George Kittle said this week, according to ESPN, that Purdy is going to play through things that none of us know about. Mexico City proved it.

But the injury didn't respond the way the team hoped. Purdy missed practice on Wednesday, Thursday and Friday. On Friday, Shanahan gave the update. According to 49ers Webzone, Shanahan said the sprain was more mid than low, which is a little bit worse. He also made it clear that surgery is not being considered. That's important. A mid-ankle sprain is more serious than a typical low ankle sprain, but it's still not the high ankle sprain that can keep players out for several weeks.

Let me quickly explain the difference, because it helps make sense of the timeline. A typical low ankle sprain affects the ligaments on the outside of the ankle, and players often return within a week or two. A high ankle sprain affects the ligaments above the ankle that connect the two lower leg bones, and those can take much longer, sometimes four to six weeks or more. Shanahan's description of this one as more mid than low suggests it falls somewhere in between: worse than a routine sprain, but not the kind of injury that threatens the rest of the season. Every injury is different, of course, and only the medical staff knows the full details. But the fact that surgery isn't being considered, and that Shanahan is optimistic about next week, points to a short-term absence rather than a long one.

Purdy also has a long history of fighting through injuries. As a rookie, he tore the UCL in his throwing elbow early in the NFC Championship Game in Philadelphia and stayed in that game as long as he physically could. He needed surgery that offseason and came back to lead the 49ers to the Super Bowl the following year. In 2025, he dealt with a lingering toe injury that cost him several games. If the team is holding him out now, it's because the medical staff believes rest is the fastest path back, not because Purdy wanted to sit.

So when will Purdy be back? According to the 49ers' website, Shanahan said he's pretty optimistic about Purdy for next week, but that it's too early to tell. That's the honest answer, and it's probably the right approach. The 49ers are 9 and 1. They lead the NFC West by three games. The real prize is in January and February, not one game in late November. Forcing a quarterback back on an ankle that isn't ready can turn a short absence into a long one. Protecting Purdy now could be the smartest decision the team makes all season. And by ruling him out on Friday, instead of waiting until game day, the 49ers also gave Mac Jones a full weekend to prepare as the starter, with no uncertainty hanging over the offense.

And let's be clear about how much the 49ers are losing for this one game. Purdy leads the NFL with a 118.2 passer rating, according to the Associated Press. Through ten games, he has 23 touchdown passes and just five interceptions. He's thrown three touchdowns with no interceptions in each of his last two games. He's at or near the top of the MVP race. You don't replace that. You survive without it.

There's also an MVP angle here. According to 49ers Webzone, through ten games, Purdy has 2,624 passing yards, 23 touchdowns and five interceptions. Joe Burrow has been the betting favorite, with Purdy right behind him. Missing a game won't end Purdy's case, but it does give the rest of the field a chance to catch up. If Purdy comes back next week and keeps playing at this level, he'll stay in the conversation. And honestly, if the 49ers struggle without him on Sunday, that might only strengthen the argument for how valuable he is.

Now let's talk about the man who has to step in: Mac Jones. This will be his first start of the season. And Shanahan sounded completely confident in him. According to the 49ers' website, he said, "We went through this a lot with him last year, so everyone's got a ton of confidence in Mac." He's right. Last season, with Purdy dealing with a toe injury, Jones made eight starts. According to CBS Sports, he completed about 70% of his passes for 2,151 yards, with 13 touchdowns and six interceptions, and according to Heavy, he went five and three in those starts. That's not a panic-button backup. That's a quarterback who can manage a game with this supporting cast around him.

Jones has been through a lot in his career. He was a first-round pick of the New England Patriots in 2021 and made the Pro Bowl as a rookie. After some tough seasons in New England, he spent time in Jacksonville before landing in San Francisco, where he rebuilt his reputation. Before this season's trade deadline, several teams called the 49ers about him, and Shanahan said at the time that he was very glad Jones was still on the roster. This is exactly why. Rookie Kurtis Rourke will be the backup on Sunday. Rourke has spent the season learning the system, and the hope is that he won't be needed.

And Jones won't be doing this alone. He'll have Mike Evans, who's questionable but practiced on a limited basis Thursday and Friday. He'll have Keenan Allen, George Kittle, Deebo Samuel and Christian McCaffrey. And he'll have a defense that just held a playoff-caliber Vikings team to 10 points. If there's ever a time for a backup quarterback to step in, it's with this group around him.

Mike Evans, in particular, could be a huge help. In the first meeting against Seattle, he caught seven passes for 113 yards and a touchdown, plus the go-ahead two-point conversion. In his return from the Achilles injury in Mexico City, he caught six passes for 109 yards and a touchdown. Shanahan called him a first-ballot Hall of Famer after that game. A big, physical receiver who wins contested catches is exactly the kind of target a backup quarterback wants. Even when he's covered, he's open. And Keenan Allen, whom Shanahan said people don't talk about enough, has the veteran savvy to find soft spots in zone coverage, which is exactly what Seattle's two-deep defense tends to leave open underneath.

According to the 49ers' website, Hobbs signed with San Francisco on March 17th, then landed on injured reserve on September 1st, before the regular season even started. He has one playoff game on his résumé, with five tackles and a pass defensed. He's 27 years old, from Louisville, Kentucky, and he played his college football at Illinois, where he started 35 games over four seasons. With Stout questionable and Lenoir coming off a foot injury, getting a physical, experienced cornerback like Hobbs back isn't a small thing. He may not start on Sunday, but the depth just got a lot better.

Second, the 49ers brought back a name every NFL fan knows: Brandin Cooks. They signed him to the practice squad just days after waiving him on November 21st to make room for defensive tackle C.J. West. According to the 49ers' website, Cooks is 33, a native of Stockton, California, and an Oregon State product who was a first-team All-American in 2013. The Saints drafted him 20th overall in 2014, and he's played for the Saints, Patriots, Rams, Texans, Cowboys, Bills and now the 49ers. Third, and this is the move that hurts, the 49ers placed right tackle Colton McKivitz on injured reserve to make room for Hobbs. McKivitz had started all ten games this season before fracturing and dislocating his left ankle in Mexico City. According to ESPN, he'd started 61 straight games and was leading all qualified tackles in pass block win rate. That's a starter on a 9 and 1 team gone for the year.

So what happens on the right side? Vederian Lowe takes over at right tackle. According to PFF, Lowe didn't allow a single pressure in his 38 snaps against the Vikings, the most aggressive blitzing defense in the league. And there's another name to watch: Austen Pleasants. A decision on activating him was expected Saturday. If he's activated, he would serve as the backup tackle, which would give the 49ers some security behind Lowe and Trent Williams.

Shanahan talked this week about how valuable it had been to have the same five starting offensive linemen through the first ten games. He said the team had been very fortunate, and that this was the first big loss. He also explained that Lowe takes tackle reps every Wednesday and rotates at other positions on other days, so he's been preparing for this all season. Now, with a backup quarterback under center and a new starter at right tackle, that preparation will be put to the test against one of the best defenses in the league.

The Seahawks have struggled with turnovers while trying to defend their title, and those turnovers cost them the first meeting with San Francisco. Mike Macdonald, one of the sharpest defensive minds in the league, has had two weeks to prepare. Expect him to disguise his coverages and test Mac Jones and Vederian Lowe right away.

So how do the 49ers win this game with Mac Jones? Let's go through the keys.

First, run the football. Seattle's defense is elite. According to Sports Illustrated, it allows just 4.0 yards per play, the lowest in the league. But Shanahan explained this week that Seattle plays a heavy two-shell defense, with two deep safeties most of the time, and doesn't blitz nearly as much as Minnesota. Against a two-deep shell, there are often fewer defenders near the line of scrimmage, which can open running lanes. Christian McCaffrey has scored a rushing touchdown in eight straight games. Rookie Kaelon Black has given the 49ers a physical second option. A run-heavy game plan protects Jones, keeps Seattle's pass rush in check and controls the clock.

There's a reason this approach makes so much sense. Last season, when Jones was starting, the 49ers leaned on their running game and their playmakers to take pressure off him, and it worked well enough to win five of his eight starts. This season, the 49ers lead the NFL in scoring at 31.6 points per game and in yards per play, according to the Associated Press. That's not all Purdy. It's also Shanahan's scheme, an elite group of skill players and an offensive line that, until Mexico City, had started the same five players all season. A lot of that remains in place for Jones.

Second, keep it simple for Jones. Quick throws, high-percentage passes, play-action and getting the ball to playmakers in space. Jones doesn't need to be Purdy, and nobody is asking him to be. He needs to stay on schedule, avoid negative plays and let Evans, Allen, Kittle and Deebo make plays after the catch.

Third, protect him. Lowe will be making his first full start at right tackle. Expect chip blocks from Kittle and the running backs, especially early. The good news is that Seattle is without Reed, and several of its defensive linemen were dealing with injuries during the week.

Fourth, win the turnover battle. In the first meeting, on October 11th in Seattle, the 49ers won 32 to 29 in a game with seven combined turnovers, according to the Associated Press. Seattle committed four. Rookie Gracen Halton stripped George Holani and recovered the fumble in the end zone for a touchdown, and Malik Mustapha sealed the win with a fourth-down stop on Cooper Kupp. With a backup quarterback starting, turnovers become even more important. The 49ers can't give Seattle short fields, and their defense needs to take the ball away.

And fifth, let the defense lead. After Mexico City, the 49ers' defense is playing with confidence. According to PFF, Nick Bosa led all players with five pressures against the Vikings and recorded his first sack of the season. Osa Odighizuwa and rookie Romello Height each had four pressures. C.J. West earned the highest pass-rush grade in the game after being activated from injured reserve. And the secondary created both turnovers, with Ji'Ayir Brown's pick-six and Lenoir's forced fumble. Against Darnold, who's returning from nearly a month away, and a Seattle offensive line missing two starters, the 49ers' pass rush has a real opportunity to take over the game. According to Sports Illustrated, Darnold ranks 25th in adjusted EPA per play among starting quarterbacks this season, and he threw two interceptions in the first meeting. Shanahan said all along that he wasn't worried about Bosa, and John Lynch predicted Bosa would start impacting games in a big way. If Bosa builds on what he did in Mexico City, this could be the week the 49ers' defense carries the team.

And here's what to watch on game day. The 49ers will announce their final inactives 90 minutes before kickoff, which is at 1:25 p.m. Pacific on FOX. Keep an eye on Evans and Greenlaw, both questionable. Watch whether Pleasants is activated from injured reserve, giving the team another tackle. And watch how the 49ers use their running game early. If McCaffrey and Black get going in the first quarter, it'll be a sign that Shanahan's plan is working and that Jones will be able to play from a comfortable position. If the 49ers fall behind early and have to throw, it becomes a much tougher afternoon.

Here's where I land on all of it. If Purdy were playing, I'd feel very good about this game at home. Without him, it becomes a closer, tougher matchup. But I trust Shanahan to build a plan around Jones, McCaffrey and Kittle that keeps things simple and takes pressure off the quarterback. And I trust this defense to make life difficult for Darnold. The roster moves this week, from Hobbs to Cooks to Pleasants, were all about protecting the team's depth, and they did exactly that. What worries me most isn't one single injury. It's the pile of small ones adding up right before December. Purdy, McKivitz, Watkins, Stout, Greenlaw, Neal and Stribling are all dealing with something. That's the reality of an NFL season in late November. The teams that win in January are usually the ones that survive this stretch with their core intact.

And let's keep the big picture in mind. Even if the 49ers lose on Sunday, they'd still be 9 and 2, leading the NFC West, with their franchise quarterback expected back soon. If they win, they'll have survived a week without Purdy and McKivitz, swept the defending champions and taken another huge step toward the NFC's top seed. Either way, sitting Purdy now, so he's healthy for the stretch run, is the right call.

The race for the top seed matters a lot here. After the Mexico City win, one FTN Fantasy projection cited by Niner Noise put the 49ers' chances of earning the NFC's number one seed at about 81%. That seed comes with the conference's only first-round bye and home-field advantage throughout the playoffs. For a veteran-heavy team that's already been hit by injuries, that bye week could be the difference between a deep run and an early exit. And a healthy Purdy in January is worth far more than a hobbled Purdy in November.
