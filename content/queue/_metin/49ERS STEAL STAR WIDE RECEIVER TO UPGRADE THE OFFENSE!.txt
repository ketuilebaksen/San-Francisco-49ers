Let's start where this whole chain of events began: the trade that rewrote the defensive line. Rewind to Tuesday, October 27th. The Indianapolis Colts, after firing general manager Chris Ballard and losing three straight games, were in the middle of a fire sale. And they agreed to send DeForest Buckner back to San Francisco. The price? A 2027 fourth-round pick.

Think about that for a second. In 2020, the 49ers traded Buckner to Indianapolis for the 13th overall pick, which became defensive tackle Javon Kinlaw. John Lynch has called that trade probably the toughest decision of his tenure. Six years later, Buckner is back for a fourth-rounder. And the timing was no accident.

Look at what the defensive line was dealing with. Alfred Collins suffered a patellar tendon injury. C.J. West has been dealing with a knee injury. James Thompson Jr. has been banged up with an ankle injury. Nick Bosa missed five games with his calf. And Sam Okuayinonu is out until January. That injury list reads like a medical report.

And the results showed up on the field. In the loss in Atlanta, the 49ers pressured Falcons quarterback Michael Penix Jr. on just three of his 34 dropbacks. Three. According to ESPN Research, San Francisco's pressure rate when rushing four or fewer players was 23 percent, the second worst in the entire league. For a defense that's always been built on winning up front with four rushers, that's a real problem.

Now look at who they brought in. Buckner is 32. Through seven games, he had four sacks for Indianapolis, which tied for the Colts' team lead and was more than any 49ers player had at that point. He's a three-time Pro Bowler, a first-team All-Pro back in 2020, and he has 75 and a half career sacks. ESPN noted that since 2016, he's one of only four players with at least 75 sacks and 25 batted passes. The other three? T.J. Watt, Cameron Jordan and Chris Jones. That's elite company.

And you could feel what it meant inside the building. Buckner said that when he learned the trade was done, he fell to his knees and started praying. He said his old teammates, guys like George Kittle, Kyle Juszczyk, Fred Warner and Nick Bosa, spent years telling him to come back home. And now he's home.

Let's also think about how Buckner changes the math for everybody else up front. When an interior lineman can collapse the pocket, edge rushers suddenly win more often, because the quarterback can't step up to escape them. That's the hidden value of a player like Buckner. His own sack numbers are great, but his real impact shows up in the stat sheets of the players around him. Osa Odighizuwa gets more one-on-ones. Mykel Williams, last year's first-round pick, gets cleaner rush lanes. And now that Bosa is back, offenses will have to decide who gets the double team: Bosa off the edge, or Buckner in the middle. Whichever one they pick, the other one is going to eat.

But here's where it gets interesting. A team that just solved its biggest problem should be done shopping, right? Apparently not. Because one week later, the front office was back at it.

On Tuesday, November 3rd, the 49ers brought in eight players for workouts. All eight were defenders. Five were defensive linemen, and three were defensive backs. I want you to sit with that split for a moment, because it says more than any press release.

Let's start with the linemen. Four of the five were defensive tackles: Matthew Butler, Aaron Graves, Chris Williams and Kristian Williams. The fifth was defensive end Dominic Bailey.

Now, I'll be honest with you. These were workouts, not signings. Nothing has been announced, and anyone telling you a deal is done is guessing. Teams bring players in for workouts all the time to keep an emergency list ready. But a team that felt completely comfortable with its interior depth probably wouldn't bring in four defensive tackles a week after trading for a Pro Bowler.

And it makes sense when you think about what Buckner can and can't do. He can transform a defensive front. He cannot play every single snap. Someone has to handle the rest of the rotation, especially with Collins, West and Thompson all dealing with injuries. And the depth took another hit when Kevin Givens, who'd spent seven seasons in San Francisco, was signed off the practice squad by the Colts on October 30th. Another practice squad defensive tackle, Naquan Jones, left for the Houston Texans a couple of days later. So this is a group still being stitched together, and the front office is clearly making sure it has options.

Now, here's the detail that jumped out at me. Of those four tackles, Matthew Butler is the name some fans might recognize. He played at Tennessee, and the Las Vegas Raiders drafted him in the fifth round in 2022. He later spent time with them again on their practice squad. And who do the 49ers host today? The Las Vegas Raiders.

Is that a coincidence? Possibly. Teams don't usually bring in a player just to pick his brain about an opponent's playbook, and nobody has reported that that's the reason here. But when a former Raider walks through the door a few days before you face the Raiders, you at least raise an eyebrow. And Butler wasn't the only one.

Let's move to the secondary. When the 49ers acquired Buckner, they needed to open a roster spot. The player who made way was cornerback Jack Jones. He had appeared in seven games this season with 11 tackles and three passes defensed. But his performance in Atlanta was rough, and it seemed to be the final straw.

The three defensive backs who worked out on Tuesday were Christian Braswell, David Long and Kyu Blu Kelly. And Kelly is the one to remember. He's a Stanford product, so he has Bay Area roots. And he also spent time with the Raiders. They signed him to their active roster in 2024 after he started on their practice squad. So that's two former Raiders in one workout group, the week the 49ers play the Raiders.

Now, who's already in the cornerback room? According to the team's depth chart, Renardo Green and Deommodore Lenoir are the outside starters. Upton Stout plays nickel. Ephesians Prysock sits behind Lenoir. And Nate Hobbs, who's been out since August with an adductor injury, has his practice window open and is close to returning. That's a solid top group. But the question is always the next man up. One injury, one tough matchup against a deep receiving corps, and the whole picture changes.

So here's what I think these workouts are really saying. San Francisco isn't panicking. It's doing something smarter. It's planning for the problem before the problem arrives. And after all the injuries this team has absorbed already, I'd much rather see that than a front office hoping for good luck.

Now, I promised you a second story, and this one has a twist a lot of people missed: the receiver who came back.

On Monday, November 2nd, the 49ers signed wide receiver KhaDarel Hodge to their practice squad. Then, on Saturday, they elevated him to the active roster for today's game. But here's what makes this interesting. This isn't his first stint with San Francisco this season. Not even close.

The 49ers originally signed Hodge on August 5th. They released him at the end of August as part of final cuts, and then brought him back to the practice squad. He was elevated for the season opener against the Rams in Melbourne, Australia, then promoted to the active roster in September. Then, on October 1st, a knee injury led to his release with an injury settlement. He spent October without a team. And now, in the first days of November, he's back.

So what changed? The simple answer is injuries, and the receiver room keeps getting thinner. On Saturday, the 49ers placed Christian Kirk on injured reserve with a knee injury that could end his season. Mike Evans and Deebo Samuel were already ruled out. And when the room gets short, you call the guy who already knows the playbook.

So who is KhaDarel Hodge? Let's get the facts right, because some of the numbers floating around online about him are way off. Hodge is 31 years old. He's from D'Lo, Mississippi. He started at Hinds Community College before transferring to Prairie View A&M. In three seasons there, he caught 104 passes for 1,797 yards and 21 touchdowns, including 48 catches, 844 yards and 12 touchdowns as a senior. Those are not the numbers of a guy who got lucky. Even so, nobody drafted him.

The Rams signed him as an undrafted free agent in 2018. He moved on to Cleveland in 2019, then briefly to Detroit off waivers in 2021, and then to Atlanta in 2022. And that's where he found his niche. With the Falcons, he became one of the best special teams players in the NFC. After the 2024 season, he was named to the Pro Bowl as the NFC's special teamer. Atlanta re-signed him on a two-year deal in March 2025.

Why does that matter? Because special teams has been one of the few weak spots on this 49ers team. They had real breakdowns in Atlanta. And Hodge is a gunner, the guy who sprints down the field on punts and makes the tackle before the returner can get started. That's why the team elevated him for today: mainly for special teams. And against a Raiders team whose returner, Dylan Laube, just took a kickoff back 95 yards against the Jets, that's no small thing.

As a receiver, Hodge isn't going to blow the doors off a defense. He's not going to show up on highlight reels every week. But he's a physical, reliable veteran who knows the system, blocks hard and makes the routine catch. On a day when the 49ers are missing three of their top receivers, that has real value.

The other big move on Saturday: Demarcus Robinson was activated from injured reserve. He's been out since a high ankle sprain in Week 2, and his questionable tag was removed. Robinson wears number 11 this year, Brandon Aiyuk's old number. And he gives Brock Purdy a veteran who knows how to run Shanahan's routes and block in the run game.

So let's look at the receivers available today. Demarcus Robinson. Jordan Watkins. Jacob Cowing. Brandin Cooks. And KhaDarel Hodge. Plus George Kittle at tight end and Christian McCaffrey, who's back after a full practice on Friday. It's not the dream group. But with McCaffrey and Kittle healthy, it's workable.

I'd compare Hodge to a player like Trent Taylor in a previous era of this team. Not flashy, not a star, but a steady presence who knows exactly what he's supposed to do on every play. Coaches love players like that, because they never have to wonder whether he'll be in the right spot. And in a season where so many receivers have been in and out of the lineup, reliability matters more than ever. Now, we haven't seen him get significant offensive snaps for San Francisco yet, so we'll have to see how he's used today. But if I had to guess, his main job will be on punt and kickoff coverage, with a handful of offensive snaps if the 49ers need a sixth receiver.

And I think the Hodge story says something about how this front office operates. They didn't forget about him when they let him go in October. They kept him in mind, waited until he was healthy, and brought him right back when the need showed up. That's what a well-run organization does. It keeps a list. It keeps relationships. And when the time comes, it doesn't have to start from scratch.

Now let's get to the part of today's headline everyone wants to talk about. Can the 49ers steal a wide receiver before the deadline? Let me be completely honest: as of right now, they haven't made a trade for a receiver. Hodge is the receiver who came back, and Robinson is the receiver who got healthy. But the trade market is moving, and one name just became very interesting.

That name is Calvin Ridley. On Saturday, reports said the Tennessee Titans are open to trading Ridley before Tuesday's deadline. ESPN's Jeremy Fowler reported that Tennessee is looking for a "quality Day 3 pick," and Sports Illustrated's Albert Breer suggested a sixth-round pick or so could get it done. JP Acosta of CBS Sports specifically suggested Ridley as an intriguing target for the 49ers. Now, I want to be clear. That's an analyst's idea, not a report that the 49ers are in talks. The teams that have been connected to Ridley are the Steelers, and Ridley's been called a backup plan for contenders looking for receivers.

But let's talk about why the idea is intriguing. Ridley turns 32 in December. He's had two 1,000-yard seasons in recent years: 76 catches for 1,016 yards and eight touchdowns in 2023, and 64 catches for 1,017 yards and four touchdowns in 2024. But 2025 was cut short by a broken leg after just seven games, and this season, on a 1 and 7 Titans team, he's fallen down the depth chart. He has just six catches for 91 yards in seven games.

So here's the honest picture. Ridley is not the player he once was. But he can still win underneath, and he has the veteran savvy to get open. The bigger issue is the money. He's under contract through 2027, with a 29.75-million-dollar cap hit next season, and about 13 million dollars in cash still owed. None of next year's money becomes guaranteed until a two-million-dollar roster bonus in March. That's a hurdle. It means a team could take him as a rental and potentially release him before next season. But it would still need to fit his remaining salary this year.

Would I call that stealing a star receiver? Not quite. It would be more like buying a lottery ticket at a cheap price. A sixth-round pick for a former 1,000-yard receiver who might have something left is low risk. But it's not the same as trading for a true number one.

And let's look at the rest of the market. Josh Downs requested a trade from the Colts. Ian Rapoport reported that the Colts want a third-round pick, but Adam Schefter said nobody is at a three, and it could end up at a fourth. Downs is 25, a quick separator, and on the final year of his rookie contract. He might be the best fit for what Shanahan wants. George Pickens is the bigger name. Jordan Schultz said teams are getting curious about the Cowboys receiver, who's playing on the franchise tag. But there's no report linking him to San Francisco, and the price would be a lot steeper. Garrett Wilson is off the table, with Schefter reporting there's no way the Jets trade him. And Jauan Jennings stayed in Minnesota. Shanahan said he and Jennings spoke on FaceTime, Jennings told him he was happy where he is, and the team respected that.

Let me compare Downs and Ridley directly, because they're the two most realistic names, and they're very different players. Downs is 25. He's quick, he separates in short areas, and he'd be under team control only through the end of this season, so he'd likely need a new contract after that. Ridley is about to turn 32. He's a bigger name with a longer track record, but he's coming off a broken leg, and he's barely been used in Tennessee this year. Downs is the better bet to help immediately in Shanahan's timing-based offense. Ridley is the cheaper flier with more risk and more upside if he still has something in the tank. If I'm John Lynch, I'd take Downs at a fourth-round price over Ridley at a sixth. But if the Colts won't come down, Ridley at a late-round pick is a reasonable fallback.

There's also a counterargument worth making: maybe the 49ers shouldn't add any receiver at all. Evans and Deebo are both expected to return at some point. Robinson is back today. Pearsall is out for the season, but McCaffrey and Kittle are healthy. And Shanahan himself has said how hard it is for a new receiver to learn his offense in the middle of the season. If the 49ers think Evans and Deebo will be healthy for the stretch run, they might decide that the receiver they need is already in the building. That's a fair way to look at it. But with Kirk now on injured reserve, the margin for error at receiver is very thin. One more injury and the 49ers would be back to throwing to their fifth and sixth options.

So what will the 49ers actually do? Shanahan has said it's hard to add a receiver midseason who can learn this offense quickly, and the team isn't counting on it. But he also said they're looking at receivers and every other position. My read is that if the 49ers make a receiver move, it will be a value move. A Day 3 pick for someone like Downs or Ridley, not a blockbuster. That fits the pattern of everything this front office has done: Buckner for a fourth, workouts to build emergency depth, Hodge back for special teams. Smart, efficient, low-risk moves.

And then there's the pass rush. Khalil Mack is still the name to watch, on an expiring one-year deal with the Chargers. Matt Maiocco said he wouldn't rule out a trade, possibly for a late-round pick. With Bosa back and Buckner in the building, Mack would be a luxury. But with that 23 percent pressure rate fresh in everybody's mind, adding another edge rusher wouldn't be crazy.
