Before we get to Cousins, let's start with the news that every 49ers fan was waiting for. Mike Evans is back on the practice field. On Wednesday, the first full practice day in Colorado Springs, Evans didn't participate. He's been dealing with Achilles tendinitis and has missed the last three games, going back to the Washington game in October. When reporters asked him whether there was still hope he could go on Thursday, Evans said he hoped so, but that he was taking it day by day. Shanahan was asked an interesting question too: if Evans had no chance to play on Sunday, would he even be on this trip? Shanahan admitted he hadn't thought about it, and said that maybe Evans does have a chance, but that the team probably still would have brought him anyway. That's classic Shanahan. He won't give you a headline until he's sure.

But on Thursday, the headline came anyway. Evans returned to practice as a limited participant. Cam Inman of the Bay Area News Group wrote that Evans looked unhindered in his first practice since the Achilles issue. That's a very good sign. It doesn't mean he's guaranteed to play on Sunday. Friday's practice and the final injury report will tell us more. But for the first time in weeks, there's a real chance that Evans is on the field for a game, and a real chance that we finally get to see what this offense looks like with Evans, Keenan Allen, Deebo Samuel, George Kittle and Christian McCaffrey all on the field together.

Think about what that means. In Dallas, without Evans, the 49ers put up 584 yards and 46 points. Evans was leading all 49ers wide receivers in receiving yards when he got hurt, and before his Achilles started bothering him, he had a 113-yard game in Seattle and 94 yards in barely a half against Washington. He's one of the best contested-catch receivers of his generation. Against a Minnesota defense that blitzes constantly and leaves receivers in one-on-one coverage, a big, physical target who can win on the outside could be exactly the weapon the 49ers need.

And there was more good news on Thursday's injury report. Renardo Green, who left the Dallas game with a back injury, was a full participant. Nate Hobbs was full again too. That's important, because Hobbs is still on injured reserve, and the 49ers have until November 25th to activate him. Shanahan has said the team has to cut someone to make room for him, but with Green back and Hobbs healthy, the cornerback room suddenly looks a lot stronger than it did in Arlington. Dre Greenlaw, who missed Wednesday with a neck issue, returned as a limited participant. Defensive tackle C.J. West and defensive end Mykel Williams practiced fully. Christian McCaffrey and Trent Williams were full participants after getting a scheduled rest day on Wednesday. The one new concern is backup quarterback Mac Jones, who missed Thursday's practice with an illness. It's worth keeping an eye on, but it doesn't change anything about the starting lineup.

On the Minnesota side, receiver Jordan Addison missed practice again with a thumb injury, and quarterback Kyler Murray remains out with a hamstring injury. Tight end T.J. Hockenson, edge rusher Andrew Van Ginkel and cornerback Isaiah Rodgers were limited. And linebacker Blake Cashman, who's been in the concussion protocol, practiced fully, with Kevin O'Connell saying he's likely to play. With Addison potentially out, former 49ers receiver Jauan Jennings could become an even bigger part of Minnesota's game plan. He practiced fully on Thursday.

There's also a roster deadline approaching that's worth keeping in mind. Brandon Aiyuk's unpaid roster exemption runs through November 23rd, the day after the Vikings game. The 49ers will have to make a decision on him by then, and a release is widely expected after everything that's happened this year. So the next few days could bring a couple of important roster moves. Whatever happens, the timing makes sense. The 49ers want their best possible roster on the field for a game that could decide the top seed in the NFC, and Hobbs, who can play both inside and outside, gives them valuable flexibility at a position where injuries have already caused real problems this season.

Now let's talk about the man at the center of the title of this video: Kirk Cousins. Let's be clear about one thing first, because I want to keep this honest. There's no big official announcement from the 49ers about Cousins. What we got this week was Shanahan's honest evaluation of him, and a reminder of just how deep the connection between these two runs. And honestly, that story is more interesting than any announcement.

When Shanahan was asked what he saw from Cousins in his first game back with the Vikings, his answer was very revealing. He said it was the same thing you always see when you watch Kirk play. Then he pointed out something important: Minnesota's defense created a lot of turnovers against Green Bay and gave Cousins a bunch of short fields. Shanahan estimated that the Vikings' defense earned him about 17 points, and compared it to what the 49ers' own defense did for them when they played Seattle. In other words, Shanahan wasn't dismissing Cousins, but he wasn't overreacting to that 30 to 10 win either. He said Cousins just looked like Kirk with the Vikings: running the same scheme they've always run and connecting with the same guys he's always connected with. And he added that the 49ers played Kirk just a couple of weeks ago, so they're very familiar with him, while admitting that it definitely helps Minnesota to have him there.

Let's look at the facts behind that. In Cousins's return at Lambeau Field, he completed 21 of 31 passes for 207 yards, with two touchdowns and an interception. Justin Jefferson caught nine passes for 74 yards, his season high in catches. But Shanahan's point was correct. Green Bay lost three fumbles, and each one led to a Minnesota field goal. Harrison Smith also intercepted Jordan Love in the end zone. The Vikings' defense sacked Love eight times. That's a defense carrying an offense, with a steady quarterback making sure he doesn't give it away. That's exactly what Minnesota wants from Cousins.

And here's where the history gets fascinating. Shanahan and Cousins go way back. When Washington drafted Cousins in the fourth round in 2012, Kyle Shanahan was the team's offensive coordinator, working under his father, Mike Shanahan. Cousins spent his first seasons learning in Kyle's offense. Years later, when Shanahan took over in San Francisco, he and John Lynch had a plan for the 2018 offseason. According to the San Francisco Standard, Shanahan has said that signing Cousins in free agency was, in his words, a done deal, at least as far as he saw it. He believed the 49ers were going to land a franchise-type quarterback in his second year as head coach.

So what happened? During the 2017 season, with the 49ers at 0 and 9, the Patriots offered Jimmy Garoppolo for a second-round pick. San Francisco said yes. Garoppolo won the last five games of the season, the 49ers signed him to a five-year, $137.5 million contract in February 2018, and the Cousins plan disappeared, as Shanahan put it, kind of out of sight, out of mind. Cousins signed with Minnesota instead. Garoppolo led the 49ers to a Super Bowl two years later. And eventually, the 49ers found Brock Purdy with the last pick of the 2022 draft.

But Shanahan has never stopped respecting Cousins. He's said that Kirk's talent has always been underrated. And it's not hard to see why. Cousins has had a remarkable career: years as the Vikings' starter, a stint in Atlanta, a stop in Las Vegas this season, where he threw more touchdown passes than anyone in the league through the first seven weeks, and now a return to Minnesota at 38 years old. He knows Shanahan's system, the outside zone, the play-action, the boot concepts, because he grew up in it. And Minnesota head coach Kevin O'Connell runs an offense built on very similar principles, which is a big reason Cousins looks so comfortable there.

Now, the 49ers just faced Cousins two weeks ago. In that game, with the Raiders, San Francisco won 38 to 17 at Levi's Stadium. The defense forced five takeaways in the second half alone. Rookie linebacker Jaden Dugger returned an interception 35 yards for a touchdown on the very first play after halftime. Cousins threw three interceptions, according to the team's official count. That's the version of the matchup the Faithful want to see again. But Shanahan is right to be careful. Cousins now has Justin Jefferson, a familiar offensive system and a much better supporting cast. And the 49ers' secondary is coming off a game in which Dak Prescott threw five touchdown passes against it. So this is not the same challenge as it was two weeks ago.

And this isn't the first time Cousins and Flores have teamed up against the 49ers. Back in October 2023, on Monday Night Football in Minneapolis, Cousins and the Vikings beat San Francisco 22 to 17. That was Flores's first season as Minnesota's defensive coordinator, and his defense made life miserable for the 49ers' offense, while Cousins made the throws he needed to make. It was part of a three-game losing streak for a 49ers team that eventually reached the Super Bowl. Then, in Week 2 of 2024, with Sam Darnold at quarterback, Minnesota beat the 49ers again, 23 to 17. According to Sharp Football Analysis, the 49ers turned the ball over five times in those two games, and Purdy was responsible for four of them. That's the pattern the 49ers have to break. Pressure leads to rushed decisions, rushed decisions lead to turnovers, and turnovers decide close games.

But there's another chapter of the Cousins story that 49ers fans love to remember. In January 2020, in the divisional round of the playoffs, Cousins brought the Vikings to Levi's Stadium, fresh off an overtime upset win in New Orleans. The 49ers' defense dominated that day, and San Francisco won 27 to 10 on the way to the Super Bowl. So Cousins has both beaten Shanahan and been crushed by him on the biggest stage. That's what makes this rivalry so fascinating. These two know each other's tendencies better than almost anyone else in the league.

There's also a simple football reason why the 49ers' defense should feel confident. Cousins is a pocket passer. He's never been a quarterback who beats you by scrambling. If the 49ers can get pressure up the middle with DeForest Buckner and Osa Odighizuwa, and force him off his spot, the mistakes can come. That's exactly what happened two weeks ago. The challenge is that the 49ers' pass rush struggled to finish in Dallas, with only one shared sack in the entire game. Nick Bosa had three offside penalties. Shanahan has said he's not worried about Bosa and that he'll keep getting better each week. Sunday night would be a great time to prove that.

Now let's talk about the bigger threat, the one Shanahan spent the most time on: Brian Flores. When a reporter asked what makes Flores's defenses so unique, Shanahan didn't hold back. He said Flores is more unique than all the other 31 defensive play callers in the league. He said Flores is the one guy who truly commits to this style, and that it's not just a fad for a few games. He's been doing it his whole time in Minnesota, and he did the same thing in Miami. Shanahan called him as good as it gets at that stuff.

And then he said something really interesting about how offensive coaches used to view this kind of defense. He suggested that, early on, offensive coaches liked to believe that a defense that lives in that world, blitzing and taking risks on nearly every play, would eventually get burned. It was just a matter of time. But, as Shanahan explained, it's been too many years and too many games this season for that idea to hold up, because Flores's defenses are sound. Then he described what it's like to face them. He said they put so much pressure on the quarterback, the play caller, the offensive line, the receivers, on everybody. In a typical NFL game, Shanahan said, that kind of pressure mostly shows up on third down. But when you play Brian, it's 70 straight plays of that.

That's the most honest description of this matchup I've heard. And the numbers back it up. Through ten weeks, the Vikings have blitzed on 72.6% of opponents' dropbacks, the highest rate any team has posted through ten weeks since at least 2010. They lead the league with 37 sacks. They allow just 14 points per game, the best mark in the NFL. They've allowed touchdowns on just 25% of opponents' red zone trips, the lowest rate of any defense since at least 2000. And according to Pro Football Talk, Flores is five and oh in games where his defense has faced a Shanahan-coached offense, including two wins over the 49ers as Minnesota's defensive coordinator, in 2023 and 2024.

So how does Shanahan plan to handle it? He didn't give away his game plan, of course. But one of his comments this week tells you a lot about how much he trusts his quarterback. When he was asked how much it helps to have Brock Purdy playing at this level with so many moving parts at receiver, Shanahan said it helps a ton. And then he gave one of the best descriptions of Purdy I've ever heard. He said Purdy plays the right way. He truly plays the position. He never says, "This guy's better than that guy, so I'm going to force the ball to him," or, "That guy didn't make the play last time, so I'm going to ignore him." Shanahan said Purdy keeps a clear mind, plays one play at a time and does what he's supposed to do. And if nothing is there, he competes and tries to make a play on his own. He added that Purdy's plan is the same every week, regardless of who's on the field. It can be harder when some players aren't available, but the plan never changes for him.

That's exactly the kind of quarterback you want against Flores. A blitz-heavy defense is designed to confuse you, to rush your decisions and to make you guess. A quarterback who stays disciplined, takes what the defense gives him and doesn't panic can turn that aggression into big plays. And remember what Purdy did against the blitz in Dallas: according to Next Gen Stats, he completed all six of his passes against the blitz for 197 yards and two touchdowns. Against Flores, he'll need more of that.

And it's not just the press conference. Shanahan's praise of Purdy this season has been remarkable. In an interview with the San Francisco Standard, he said Purdy is giving him the best quarterback play he's ever coached, even better than Matt Ryan's MVP season in Atlanta in 2016. He even asked why Purdy wouldn't be a Hall of Fame-type player if he keeps doing what he's doing. Purdy's career passer rating is the best among active quarterbacks. That's not a coach hyping up his player. That's a coach who's watched every snap.

Now let's talk about the altitude, because it's the other big story of the week. The 49ers flew straight from Dallas to Colorado Springs after Sunday's game and are practicing all week at the Air Force Academy before traveling to Mexico City on Saturday. Mexico City sits roughly 7,350 feet above sea level, more than 2,000 feet higher than Denver. When Shanahan was asked how the players have been adjusting, he said it's been good, but admitted the coaches hadn't seen the players much, because the players were off on Monday and Tuesday after reviewing the Dallas game. The coaches, he joked, have just been working in their hotel rooms and offices. And then he admitted he was out of breath just walking over to the press conference. Everyone feels it, he said, just like you always do when you come to Colorado. Wednesday's practice was at half speed, and the team decided to stay outside instead of moving into the indoor bubble, despite the wind.

Shanahan also explained his view on how much the altitude really matters, and his answer was balanced. He said he thinks it's just science, which he admitted he doesn't know much about, but that he knows from personal experience growing up in Colorado, when his father, Mike Shanahan, coached the Denver Broncos. He compared it to playing in Denver. He said he always thought the Broncos have about a 2% advantage at home because they're used to the altitude, then admitted with a laugh that he was making up that number. But his point was clear: a small edge doesn't win games by itself, but it's definitely nicer to play when you're acclimated and not as out of breath. It's rarely the difference in winning and losing, he said, but if it helps, they'll do it. And when asked whether the altitude would change how he calls the game, for example by trying to tire out the defense with tempo, he said probably not, calling it something like number 20 on his list of priorities. Football reasons come first.

The Vikings, by contrast, are staying home in Minnesota and flying in on Saturday. They've been using a high-altitude room at their facility. According to the team's head of health and performance, Tyler Williams, they've logged close to 450 sessions in that room, with nearly the entire roster doing at least five sessions and experiencing simulated altitude as high as 12,000 feet. Flores said his defense will rotate heavily to deal with the thin air. So it's a genuine contrast in philosophy, and it'll be fascinating to see if it makes any difference late in the game.

And then there's the story that made everyone laugh. Shanahan was asked about the pros and cons of the 49ers' last trip to Mexico City, in 2022, when they beat the Arizona Cardinals 38 to 10 on Monday Night Football. He said the stadium was awesome and the atmosphere was great, but the travel was chaotic. The team arrived so late on Saturday night that it took a long time to get through immigration, and the staff had to rearrange all of their night meetings. And then came the part his staff didn't tell him about until later, because they thought he would have freaked out. Usually, Shanahan said, the last bus of players arrives at the stadium about three hours before kickoff. That day, the bus got lost on the way to the stadium. It didn't arrive until about an hour and 15 minutes before the game, and it had about 12 starters on it. Shanahan admitted that he probably would have freaked out. Luckily, it didn't affect them. Then, after the game, it took the team about 10 hours to get out of Mexico, because they had to wait for the Cardinals to clear customs first. They got back to the Bay Area at around 9 a.m. on Tuesday.

That's a great behind-the-scenes story, and it explains why this trip has been planned so carefully. This time, the 49ers are leaving nothing to chance. Shanahan explained that when the team travels, it brings its own cooks, its own equipment for strength, conditioning and rehab, and even sets up its offices to look the same in the hotel. When he was asked whether cost has ever been an issue, whether anyone in the organization has ever pushed back on the expense of a week-long trip like this, Shanahan's answer was simple: not once. He said the question is always just what helps the team play better football. That's a big compliment to the York family and the 49ers' front office. Not every organization does it this way.

Shanahan was also asked about team bonding, because this is the second long trip of the season, after the opener in Australia. His answer was refreshingly honest. He said bonding can be cool, but it can also be overrated. The real advantage of being on the road for a week, he said, is that players' families aren't there, so nobody is rushing home after work. Players hang around, do extra rehab, watch extra film, or just sit around and play cards. That extra time together lets them focus more on the game. But, he added with a laugh, if you did it every week, it would eventually get depressing, because people want to be with their families and get away from each other too. There's a very fine line in that balance.

And remember how well this approach has worked before. In September, the 49ers arrived in Melbourne early for the season opener against the Rams, the first NFL regular season game ever played in Australia. The Rams arrived much later. San Francisco won 27 to 7, with Purdy throwing three touchdown passes. In 2022, they prepared in Colorado Springs before beating Arizona 38 to 10 in Mexico City. Two big trips, two convincing wins. The 49ers will be hoping for a third.

And this time, the 49ers know exactly what can go wrong. They'll travel to Mexico City on Saturday after a full week of altitude work, with their routines, staff and equipment already in place, and with the memory of that lost bus and that ten-hour wait at customs still fresh in the minds of the staff. You can be sure every lesson from the last trip has been built into this one, right down to the bus route to the stadium.

Then there's the crowd. Shanahan remembered the 2022 game in Mexico City as a true home game for the 49ers. He joked that he goes to Cabo a lot and that the 49ers' Mexican fan base is, in his words, off the chain. He said they show up wherever the 49ers go, and that last time, it felt like almost the whole stadium was cheering for San Francisco. The 49ers are the designated home team again on Sunday, and with tens of thousands of fans expected at the renovated stadium now known as Estadio Banorte, the formerly named Estadio Azteca, they could have a real home-field advantage even far away from Levi's Stadium.

So let's put everything together. Here's the state of the 49ers heading into the biggest game of the season so far.

On offense, they're the best unit in football right now. They lead the league in total offense and yards per play, and they're scoring touchdowns on more than 82% of their red zone trips, the best rate in the league. Purdy is in the middle of the MVP race. McCaffrey has a rushing touchdown in eight straight games. And Mike Evans is back at practice.

On defense, there are real questions after Dallas, but help is arriving. Green is back at full participation. Hobbs is healthy and waiting to be activated. And the 49ers have fresh film on Cousins from a game in which they intercepted him three times.

And then there's the matchup itself. Shanahan against Flores. The best red zone offense against the best red zone defense. The 49ers' week at altitude against Minnesota's altitude room. Cousins, the quarterback Shanahan once planned to bring to San Francisco, against the quarterback Shanahan now calls the best he's ever coached.

It's the kind of game that tells you who you really are. A win would move the 49ers to 9 and 1, give them a two-game lead over Minnesota and a head-to-head tiebreaker in the race for the NFC's top seed. A loss would make the top of the conference a crowded fight.
