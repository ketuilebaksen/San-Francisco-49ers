Let’s start with a quick catch-up, because it’s been a busy two weeks.

Two Sundays ago, the Denver Broncos came to Levi’s Stadium, and a lot of people thought this was the game that would finally slow the Niners down. Denver came in at 2-1 with a defense that ranked in the top ten against the pass and Pat Surtain the second in the secondary. And for a while, it looked like the doubters were right. The Niners’ offense was sluggish in the first half. Drives stalled. Purdy didn’t have much room to work. It was the kind of game where you start checking the clock and wondering if this is the week the streak ends.

And then special teams flipped everything. In the third quarter, Denver returner Marvin Mims Jr. fumbled a punt, and San Francisco took over on a short field. That one mistake cracked the game wide open. The Niners scored on three straight second-half possessions. Purdy threw two touchdown passes. Christian McCaffrey punched in a rushing score. And with about two minutes left, George Kittle put the cherry on top, taking a screen pass 56 yards to the end zone to ice it. Final score: 49ers 24, Broncos 14.

Purdy finished 19 of 30 for 218 yards, two touchdowns, and most importantly, zero turnovers. On a day when the offense wasn’t at its best, he didn’t give Denver a single gift. Sean Payton summed it up after the game: his team just didn’t have enough offense to get a lead. And that’s the thing about this 49ers team. Even on an off day, they find a way. Denver also lost Surtain to an injury in that game, while San Francisco lost fullback Kyle Juszczyk to a knee injury. But the bottom line was 4-0, and a date in Seattle.

Now, let’s get into Seattle. And let’s start with the headline everyone is talking about. Brock Purdy is the MVP favorite.

After the win at Lumen Field, BetMGM moved Purdy to the top of its MVP odds at plus 275. Josh Allen was right behind him at plus 300. Then Patrick Mahomes at plus 600, Trevor Lawrence at plus 900, and Lamar Jackson at plus 1100. Think about that for a second. The last pick of the 2022 draft. Mr. Irrelevant. The guy people spent years calling a system quarterback, a game manager, a product of Kyle Shanahan’s genius. He is now the betting favorite for the biggest individual award in the entire sport.

And the numbers back it up. Through five games, Purdy has thrown for 1,196 yards, 13 touchdowns, and just two interceptions, completing 69 percent of his passes. Let’s put that touchdown-to-interception ratio in perspective. Thirteen to two. Remember all the talk before the season about his double-digit interception years? Eleven in 2023, twelve in 2024, ten in 2025? Right now, he’s on pace for the cleanest season of his career. And he’s doing it as the quarterback of the only undefeated team in the NFC West.

Against Seattle, it wasn’t perfect. He finished 19 of 29 with two touchdowns, and he threw an interception to Nehemiah Pritchett in the second half that helped the Seahawks climb back into the game. But here’s what separates good quarterbacks from great ones. Great ones don’t let one mistake become two. When Seattle took the lead late, Purdy had to answer on the road, in one of the toughest environments in football, with his receiver room falling apart around him. And he did. That’s what MVPs do.

Now, I’m going to be fair, because that’s what we do here on Ketuil NFL Stories. Betting odds in October don’t hand anyone a trophy. They move with results, with perception, and with where the money is going. And Josh Allen is not going anywhere. When those odds came out, Allen had played one fewer game and already had seven rushing touchdowns. Then on Monday night, he went out and beat the Rams 37 to 34 in an instant classic. He threw for 210 yards and a touchdown, and he ran for 121 yards and another score on just 10 carries. That’s Josh Allen being Josh Allen. So don’t be surprised if those odds tighten up again this week. Comparing passing touchdowns alone leaves out a huge part of what Allen brings.

But here’s what matters for Niners fans. Purdy is producing at a level where this undefeated start has his fingerprints all over it. It’s not just McCaffrey. It’s not just Kittle. It’s not just Shanahan. It’s not just the talent around him, especially since so much of that talent has been in the training room. When the game gets tight, the ball is in his hands, and he keeps finding the right answer.

So what’s the next test for Purdy’s MVP case? Consistency. If the protection holds up and he keeps punishing different coverages, his case gets stronger every single week. If defenses start taking away his first read and the production dips, that’s a different challenge, and we’ll see how he responds. But for now? This is real, and it deserves to be celebrated. A quarterback who was once an afterthought is now the front-runner. Let that sink in.

And honestly, the best explanation for why Purdy jumped in those odds was running routes in Seattle with sore ribs. Let’s talk about Mike Evans.

Seven catches. 113 yards. A touchdown. And a two-point conversion. That was Mike Evans’s stat line on Sunday. And that touchdown wasn’t just any touchdown. It was the 111th receiving touchdown of his career, which tied him with Tony Gonzalez for ninth most in NFL history. Tony Gonzalez! One of the greatest pass catchers to ever live. And Evans matched him in a 49ers uniform, in Seattle, while playing through a rib injury.

At 33 years old, coming off an injury-shortened final season in Tampa Bay, a lot of people wondered whether Evans still had it. Could he still be the guy who decides a hard game? Could he still win one-on-one when a defense knows the ball is coming his way? Sunday gave us a loud answer.

Let’s go back to the beginning of that game, because Evans set the tone early. In the first quarter, cornerback Renardo Green picked off Sam Darnold at the Seattle 18-yard line. Short field. And Purdy went right to his big target, a 2-yard touchdown pass to Evans. 7 to 0 Niners.

Then came the chaos. Jacob Cowing muffed a punt, Seattle got a short field of its own, and running back Emanuel Wilson scored to tie the game. Then Fred Warner did what Fred Warner does. He punched the ball out, safety Malik Mustapha recovered it, and Christian McCaffrey finished the drive with an 11-yard touchdown run. And right before the half, the rookie made his play, which we’ll get to in a few minutes. 21 to 13 at the break.

The second half started well too. Renardo Green intercepted Darnold for the second time. But this time, the Niners couldn’t cash in, and they missed a field goal. And that’s when Seattle started to fight back. Jaxon Smith-Njigba caught a touchdown. Pritchett picked off Purdy. Deebo Samuel lost a fumble that led to another Seattle field goal. And suddenly, the Seahawks were up 26 to 21, and the crowd was shaking the building.

And this is where Mike Evans took over. Let me walk you through the drive that won this game, because it’s one of the best drives of the entire season.

The Niners got the ball back down five. And right away, disaster. An offensive pass interference call on Brandin Cooks put them in a first and 20. That’s a drive in serious trouble. What happens on the very next snap? Purdy to Evans for 15. Back on schedule. A few plays later, it’s third and 10. Purdy to Evans for 23. First down. Then an intentional grounding penalty pushes the offense all the way back to second and 21. Second and 21, on the road, in Seattle, down five in the fourth quarter. That’s usually where drives go to die.

And what happens? Purdy looks to the outside, sees Evans isolated in single coverage, and throws a back-shoulder ball. Evans turns, goes up, and takes it away for 28 yards. Drive alive. First down near the goal line.

Purdy explained it after the game. He saw Evans one-on-one and decided to give him a chance. That’s it. That simple. And that’s exactly what a receiver like Mike Evans does to a defense. Leave a defensive back alone with someone that big, that strong, and that good at fighting for the football, and a play that looks dead can turn into a huge gain in a heartbeat. Defenses know it. And yet, they still can’t stop it.

Brandin Cooks, the same guy who got flagged earlier in the drive, caught the touchdown from seven yards out. Redemption. Twelve plays, 74 yards. And then the two-point try. The first attempt to Evans fell incomplete. But a penalty gave the Niners another shot, and Purdy went right back to the same guy. Evans in the end zone. Two points. 29 to 26, Niners.

That’s trust. After a failed first try, a lot of quarterbacks would look somewhere else. They’d overthink it. Purdy didn’t. He knew exactly where he wanted to go, and Evans rewarded him.

But Seattle didn’t go away. Jason Myers kicked a field goal to tie it at 29. And now it’s a heavyweight fight in the final minutes. The Niners went right back down the field, and Eddy Pineiro drilled a 44-yarder with about two minutes left. 32 to 29. And the defense had to hold one more time. Smith-Njigba dropped a pass on third down. On fourth and 10, Darnold hit Cooper Kupp, but Malik Mustapha was there to make the stop, one yard short of the sticks. The officials reviewed it. Short. Ballgame.

And remember, Evans was doing all of this hurt. He said after the game that he actually felt better against Seattle than he had the week before. Christian McCaffrey called him a difference maker. Fred Warner praised the quiet way Evans goes about his business all week, never complaining, never making it about himself, and then shows up on Sunday when it matters most. Through five games, he now has 24 catches for 326 yards and three touchdowns. And honestly? It feels like more, because so many of those catches have come at the biggest moments.

Now look at what that win means in the standings. The Niners are 5-0. Seattle drops to 3-2. That’s a two-game lead in the NFC West, plus the head-to-head tiebreaker for now. Winning in that building is never easy, and doing it with this many injuries is a statement to the rest of the division. The Rams and the Seahawks were supposed to be the teams that pushed San Francisco all year long. Right now, the Niners have already beaten both of them. NBC Sports Bay Area has San Francisco at number one in its power rankings for a reason. They called them the best team in football through five weeks.

Now, here’s the fair objection, and I want to address it head on. One great game doesn’t erase Evans’s age, and it doesn’t make those ribs heal any faster. Can he handle this kind of workload every single week? Because right now, the 49ers are more dependent on him than ever. Ricky Pearsall is out for the season after knee surgery. Christian Kirk still hasn’t played because of his calf. Demarcus Robinson is on injured reserve with a high ankle sprain. De’Zhaun Stribling is still at least a month away after ankle surgery. Evans became the main answer just as the receiver room got thinner and thinner. And then on Sunday, it got even thinner.

This is Ketuil NFL Stories, and here’s the bad news.

Deebo Samuel left the Seattle game with a hamstring injury. And on Monday, Kyle Shanahan confirmed what everyone feared. Deebo got it pretty good, and he’ll miss at least a couple of weeks. That’s a big loss. Deebo had 16 catches for 206 yards and three touchdowns this season, and his ability to line up anywhere, wide, in the slot, even in the backfield, is a huge part of what makes this offense so hard to defend. Remember that hook and ladder against Arizona? Zero catches, 80 yards, a touchdown? That’s Deebo. There’s nobody else like him on this roster.

And it was a rough afternoon for him overall in Seattle. Before the injury, he also lost a fumble that set up a Seahawks field goal. So it’s a frustrating stretch for a player who’s meant so much to this franchise.

Now look at who’s left at receiver for Monday night. Mike Evans, with sore ribs. Brandin Cooks, who just scored his first touchdown as a 49er. Second-year receiver Jordan Watkins, who made some big catches against Arizona. Jacob Cowing, who had that costly muffed punt in Seattle. And on the practice squad, guys like Malik Turner and Xavier Smith. That’s it. That’s a thin, thin group behind a 33-year-old who’s playing through a rib injury.

Of course, the Niners still have George Kittle, who’s been fantastic since coming back from his Achilles. They still have McCaffrey, who can line up as a receiver whenever Shanahan wants. And rookie running back Kaelon Black has shown he can handle touches too. But when it comes to true wide receivers who can stretch the field and win on the outside, the cupboard is getting bare.

And that’s exactly why one name has started popping up: Calvin Ridley.

The Tennessee Titans are 0-5. Ridley is a former first-round pick with three 1,000-yard seasons on his résumé. Earlier this month, a Sports Illustrated piece listed him among five former first-round receivers the 49ers could consider before the November 10th trade deadline. The other names on that list were Xavier Legette, Jerry Jeudy, Quentin Johnston, and Brian Thomas Jr. And you can see the appeal with Ridley. If he can still threaten a defense deep and win his one-on-one matchups, he’d stop teams from loading up on Evans, and he’d give Purdy another answer when the first read gets taken away.

But let’s slow down, because I don’t want to sell you a trade headline. There’s no reported offer from the 49ers. There’s no asking price from Tennessee. There’s no sign the two teams have even talked. Right now, this is a writer’s idea, not a negotiation.

And the counterarguments are strong. Ridley will turn 33 soon. According to that same SI piece, he only had 303 receiving yards last season and hasn’t produced at a high level since 2024. He’s reportedly being phased out of the Titans’ offense under a new coaching staff. And then there’s the money. Taking on Ridley means taking on a cap hit of around 29.7 million dollars. That’s a massive number for a player whose best years might be behind him, especially for a team that’s already paying Purdy, McCaffrey, Kittle, Warner, and Bosa.

There’s also a fit question. Deebo’s injury might only be a couple of weeks. Stribling has a return timeline. Kirk has a return timeline. How many snaps would Ridley actually play once everyone gets healthy? If the only answer is “he’s talented,” that’s not enough to separate a good idea from a good investment. And the 49ers have already used a lot of resources this season patching holes. Remember, they only have eight IR return slots, and they’re already managing that list very carefully.

So here’s how I see it. If Deebo misses significant time and the guys on the roster can’t threaten defenses downfield, an outside option becomes a lot more valuable. If the current group spreads the ball around and keeps winning, the urgency fades. John Lynch has said he always keeps his eyes open for ways to improve this roster, but nothing points specifically to Ridley. I want to see the next injury report and how the receivers get used on Monday night before I get excited about any trade.

And there’s one more thing to keep in mind. The 49ers have shown all season that their offense doesn’t depend on one receiver. Through the first three weeks, seven different players scored touchdowns. When Evans went down against Arizona, Kittle stepped up. When Deebo was quiet, McCaffrey carried the load. Shanahan has always been at his best when he has to get creative. So maybe the answer isn’t a trade. Maybe the answer is more Kittle, more McCaffrey in the passing game, more creative designs, and more trust in the young guys.

Now, while that receiver question is still hypothetical, another roster concern already got a real answer on defense. Let’s talk about Gracen Halton.

If you’ve been following this channel since the start of the season, you know we’ve been talking about Halton for weeks. In Week 1 in Melbourne, he stopped Matthew Stafford on a goal-line quarterback sneak, and that stand turned into a 99-yard touchdown drive the other way. Against Miami, he set the tone on the very first snap and posted a 21.4 percent pressure rate, one of the best among all defensive tackles that week. And now, in Seattle, he had his breakout moment on a national stage.

Here’s the situation. With 18 seconds left in the first half, Seattle had the ball deep in its own territory, backed up near its own end zone. The 49ers still had all three timeouts. Seahawks head coach Mike Macdonald wanted to run out the clock without giving San Francisco one more possession, so he called a run from deep in his own territory. Safe call, right? Wrong. Halton got there immediately, knocked the ball loose, and then had the awareness to fall on it in the end zone. Touchdown. The first of his NFL career. And instead of going into halftime in a close game, the Niners went into the locker room up 21 to 13.

Think about how much that play changed the game. A bad decision only becomes a defensive touchdown when somebody makes the play at the exact moment the chance appears. Halton had the strength to knock the ball loose and the awareness to finish the job. That’s not luck. That’s a football player.

And that wasn’t his only big play. Earlier in the second quarter, Halton picked up his first career sack, dropping Sam Darnold for a 9-yard loss. He also blew up a run for no gain. A rookie affecting the pass and the run in the same afternoon, on the road, in Seattle, with Nick Bosa still out. That’s exactly the kind of growth you want to see.

And the whole defense showed up. Five sacks, including the first career sacks for Jaden Dugger and Sebastian Valdez. Remember Valdez? He was the practice squad guy who got elevated before the Arizona game when the defensive line was falling apart. Now he’s got an NFL sack. Halton got his. Osa Odighizuwa got one. And Keion White, back from his hamstring injury, came up with a huge third-down sack in the fourth quarter.

Four takeaways, too. Renardo Green intercepted Darnold twice, and the first one set up Purdy’s touchdown pass to Evans. Fred Warner forced a fumble that Mustapha recovered, which set up McCaffrey’s touchdown run. And Halton’s forced fumble and recovery for the score. Darnold finished just 18 of 29 for 195 yards, one touchdown, and two interceptions. That’s a defense that bent at times but made the plays that mattered.

And here’s another piece of good news. Mykel Williams made his season debut after coming back from his torn ACL. Last year’s first-round pick is finally back on the field. The cavalry is arriving.

Now, let’s be realistic about Halton, because I don’t want to overhype one game. A fumble recovery for a touchdown is rare. By itself, it doesn’t tell us what a defensive tackle can do over four quarters, on the boring snaps that never make the highlight reel. The real test is repeatable. Can he beat blocks on ordinary downs? Can he hold his ground against the run when there’s no dramatic moment waiting? Can he keep his energy as the rotation changes and the veterans come back? Those answers will tell us whether he can take on a bigger role. But the early signs are really, really encouraging. This is a rookie who keeps showing up in big moments.

There’s also some discipline news you need to know. Dre Greenlaw was ejected in Seattle. After a late hit on Darnold near the Seattle sideline drew a personal foul, things got heated, and Greenlaw headbutted Seahawks linebacker Ernest Jones, who was bleeding after the incident. On Monday, the NFL suspended Greenlaw for one game, calling the headbutt contrary to the principles of sportsmanship. He’s appealing, and Shanahan said he hopes the appeal goes well. But if the suspension stands, Greenlaw will miss Monday night against Washington.

And honestly, Faithful, this one is frustrating. Greenlaw is one of the toughest players on this team, and we love that edge. But he was also fined for taunting the week before, and this isn’t the first time he’s been ejected in his career. The Niners need him on the field, not in the locker room. With so many injuries on defense already, they can’t afford to lose healthy players to discipline.

But there’s good news on the injury front, too. Shanahan said Nick Bosa, fullback Kyle Juszczyk, and James Thompson Jr. all have a chance to return for the Washington game. Getting Bosa back right as Halton is breaking out and Mykel Williams is returning? That could change the whole look of this defensive front. Imagine a pass rush with Bosa on one side, Mykel Williams developing on the other, and Halton and Odighizuwa collapsing the pocket from the inside. That’s when this defense could go from good to scary.

Now let’s look ahead to Monday Night Football. The Washington Commanders come to Levi’s Stadium, and this is a big one.

Remember, Washington is the team that beat Seattle 33 to 31 back in Week 3, and that loss is part of the reason the Niners sit two games up in the division right now. And now, Jayden Daniels is back. He missed two games with a dislocated left elbow, his second elbow dislocation in two seasons. But he returned against the Giants this past weekend, wearing a brace on that arm, and it didn’t slow him down at all. He ran nine times for 103 yards and two touchdowns. Nine carries, 103 yards. That’s a quarterback who can break a game open with his legs at any moment.

So here’s what I’ll be watching on Monday night.

First, Fred Warner and the plan for Daniels. If Greenlaw’s suspension stands, Warner is going to have even more on his shoulders. Daniels is the kind of quarterback who makes you pay for every mistake in your rush lanes. Raheem Morris has to find a way to keep him in the pocket without giving up big plays downfield. We’ve seen Morris adjust his game plan every single week this season. Two-high safeties against the Rams, heavy blitzing against Miami. Expect another new wrinkle on Monday.

Second, the receiver room. Without Deebo, who steps up? Does Brandin Cooks become the clear number two? Does Jordan Watkins get more snaps? Does Shanahan lean even harder on Kittle and McCaffrey in the passing game? And how much can Evans handle with those ribs?

Third, Nick Bosa. If he’s back, how much does he play? Do they ease him in, or is he ready for a full workload? Even a limited Bosa changes how an offense has to protect.

And fourth, Purdy under the bright lights. Monday Night Football. The whole country watching. The MVP odds on the line. This is a chance for him to make a statement on the biggest regular-season stage there is.

So let’s bring it all home. Purdy leading the MVP odds gets the headlines, and it should. But this 5-0 start was built by a lot of players outside the award conversation. On offense, a veteran known for deciding games answered the pressure in the biggest moment. On defense, a rookie changed the scoreboard before most fans even knew his name. And a defense full of backups, practice squad call-ups, and young players keeps making the plays that matter. Purdy trusts the answers he finds, and his teammates keep rewarding that trust when the game gets tight.

What’s still open is the health of this receiver room, especially with Deebo out, and whether the defense can keep producing without a loose ball bouncing its way in the end zone. Monday night against Washington is going to tell us a lot more than any trade headline.

Five games. Five wins. A two-game lead in the division. The MVP favorite at quarterback. And reinforcements on the way. Faithful, it doesn’t get much better than this in October.
