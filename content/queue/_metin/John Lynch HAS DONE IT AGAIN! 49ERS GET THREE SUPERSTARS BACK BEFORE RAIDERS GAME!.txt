Let’s start with the catch-up, because a lot has happened since our last video.

Week 6. Monday Night Football against the Washington Commanders at Levi’s Stadium. And the Niners got off to a fast start. George Kittle opened the scoring with an 11-yard touchdown. Christian McCaffrey punched in two 1-yard touchdown runs, the second one in the final seconds of the first half, and San Francisco went into the locker room up 20 to 3. Washington made it interesting late, rallying to make it a one-score game in the fourth quarter. But the defense held on, and the 49ers won 23 to 17 to move to 6-0.

And there were some big milestones in that game. Fred Warner reached 1,000 career tackles. A thousand! The heart of this defense keeps building that Hall of Fame résumé. Brock Purdy reached 1,500 career pass attempts, which officially qualified him for the career passer rating leaderboard. And his 105.6 career rating is now the highest in NFL history. Let me say that again. The highest career passer rating in the history of the National Football League belongs to the last pick of the 2022 draft. And that night also gave the franchise its 56th win on Monday Night Football, the most of any team in league history.

The young defense was fantastic too. Rookie linebacker Jaden Dugger led the team with 11 tackles, which was huge with Dre Greenlaw serving his suspension. The defense held Washington to just 53 rushing yards, and that’s against Jayden Daniels, one of the most dangerous running quarterbacks in the league. Malik Mustapha broke up a fourth-down pass in the end zone. Mykel Williams got his first sack of the season in just his second game back from a torn ACL. And on the final play of the game, Romello Height and Keion White sacked Daniels as time expired. Ballgame.

The one concern from that night? Mike Evans had 94 receiving yards and was on his way to a monster game, but he had to leave before halftime. And that set up a very difficult trip to Atlanta.

Week 7. Falcons 29, 49ers 20. The first loss of the season. And honestly, Faithful, this one hurt because it felt like the Niners beat themselves.

It actually started well. Rookie running back Kaelon Black broke off a 30-yard touchdown run, and San Francisco jumped out to a 14 to 3 lead. But then the wheels came off. In the second quarter, with the Niners leading 14 to 9 and in scoring range, Black fumbled. And that turnover started a stretch where Atlanta scored on four straight long drives. Purdy threw two interceptions. One came when he scrambled under pressure and didn’t throw the ball away, and Jessie Bates picked it off. The other came on a third and 9 in the fourth quarter, on what could have been a go-ahead drive, to rookie Kendal Daniels. And that third and 9 only happened because of a false start on Brandin Cooks that turned a manageable third and 4 into third and long. Then, in the final minute, Kittle made a long catch into the red zone, and Bates punched the ball out of his hands. Four turnovers. Seven penalties. You’re not beating anybody in this league like that.

Special teams were a problem too. Atlanta’s Zachariah Branch ripped off a 61-yard kick return, and Khalil Herbert added a 38-yarder, and both of those set up Falcons points. Eddy Pineiro missed an extra point, his fourth miss of the season. And Jordan Watkins had a touchdown catch overturned on replay. One analyst put it perfectly: the 49ers spun out of control in all three phases of the game.

And look at who wasn’t on the field. San Francisco was missing its top five receivers. No Evans. No Deebo. No Robinson. No Stribling. No Pearsall. Then McCaffrey left in the second quarter with what was first called a thigh injury. He came back in the second half, but the offense was running on fumes. Michael Penix Jr. didn’t do anything spectacular for Atlanta, but he didn’t have to. The Falcons leaned on their checkdowns and their run game, and at one point they ran 36 plays to San Francisco’s seven. Kyle Pitts and Drake London caught touchdowns, and Nick Folk iced it with a field goal with under two minutes left.

So yes, it was ugly. But let’s keep perspective. The 49ers are 6-1. They still have a two-game lead in the NFC West. This isn’t a team that needs rescuing. It’s a team that needs its best players back. And that’s exactly what could happen against the Raiders.

But before we get to the injury list, let’s talk about the trade.

During the bye week, the 49ers acquired defensive tackle DeForest Buckner from the Indianapolis Colts. The price? A 2027 fourth-round pick. That’s it. Ian Rapoport broke the news, the team made it official the next day, and Niner Faithful everywhere lost their minds.

Because this isn’t just any trade. This is a homecoming. San Francisco drafted Buckner seventh overall in 2016 out of Oregon. He spent his first four seasons here and was a huge part of that 2019 defense that made it all the way to Super Bowl LIV. Then, in March of 2020, the 49ers traded him to Indianapolis for the 13th overall pick. And Lynch has never hidden how hard that was. He once called it probably the toughest thing he’d done since becoming the general manager. The team used that pick on Javon Kinlaw, and later signed Javon Hargrave, and honestly, neither of them ever fully filled the hole Buckner left behind.

And Buckner didn’t slow down in Indianapolis. He earned All-Pro honors in his very first season with the Colts. He’s now a three-time Pro Bowler. And he leaves Indy with 47 sacks, the most ever by a Colts defensive tackle. He’s 32 years old now, but he’s still producing. Through seven games this season, he had four sacks, eight quarterback hits, four tackles for loss, three passes defensed, and a fumble recovery. He started every game and played 82 percent of Indianapolis’s defensive snaps. Those four sacks would lead the 49ers. Think about that. A fourth-round pick just bought you the team’s sack leader.

And the money works too. The 49ers take on the rest of his 7.9 million dollar salary for this season, and his contract runs out after 2026. No long-term commitment. No risk to future cap plans. If it works out, maybe you bring him back. If not, you shake hands in March. It’s the perfect deadline rental.

According to reports, Buckner had grown tired of the same frustrating routine in Indianapolis every year. Now he gets a shot at a Super Bowl with the team that drafted him. And George Kittle wasted no time welcoming him back on social media.

Remember a few weeks ago, when we went through five potential trade targets, and Khalil Mack was at the top of the list for the defensive line? Lynch went a different direction, but the logic was exactly the same. Find a proven veteran on a short contract, pay a mid-round pick, and strengthen the front without hurting the future. And honestly, Buckner might be an even better fit, because he already knows the organization and the culture. He just has to learn Raheem Morris’s system, and the bye week gave him two weeks to do exactly that.

Now, let’s go through the players who could return against Las Vegas. And I want to be careful here, because none of these returns are confirmed. Kyle Shanahan has said several of these players have a chance to come back after the bye, but he also made it clear that nothing is guaranteed. The practice reports this week will tell us far more than anyone can say right now. So treat all of this as possibilities, not promises.

Let’s start with Christian McCaffrey. After the Atlanta game, the injury was first described as a thigh issue, but Shanahan later clarified that it’s a mild hamstring strain. When he was asked whether McCaffrey would play against the Raiders, his answer was: yeah, we think so, with it just being that. He added that you never know for sure, but the team is pretty optimistic, and so is McCaffrey.

That’s great news, because this offense runs through Christian McCaffrey in a way few offenses run through any one player. He can run between the tackles. He can catch out of the backfield. He can line up wide. He can turn a routine touch into a huge play. Defenses have to know where he is on every single snap. When he’s on the field, Purdy has a safety valve and a weapon in the same player. Against Washington, he scored twice. That’s what he means to this team.

But here’s my concern. Hamstrings are tricky. Come back a week too early, and a two-week injury turns into a two-month problem. We’ve seen it happen to players all over the league. I’d much rather see McCaffrey sit one more game and be fully healthy for the second half of the season than rush him back for the Raiders and lose him again in November. The 49ers have a cushion in the division. They don’t have to take that risk. And with Kaelon Black and Jordan James both getting real carries, there’s a decent backup plan.

Next up: Mike Evans. Evans has been dealing with sore ribs and a flared-up Achilles, and he eventually had to sit out the Atlanta game. The good news is that Shanahan said no further testing is needed, and the plan is to have him ready for the Raiders. If Evans is out there, it changes the shape of the passing game immediately. He brings size, physicality, and the ability to win contested catches, especially in the red zone, where this offense has really needed someone who can simply go up and take the ball away. Remember that drive in Seattle? That’s what Evans gives you.

But again, Achilles issues aren’t something you want to play through carelessly, especially with a 33-year-old receiver. If he’s not right, he’s not right. The medical staff will have the final word.

And on defense, the biggest name of all: Nick Bosa.

Bosa has been out since straining his calf during warmups before Week 3. He’s missed five straight games. And there was a moment before the Atlanta game when it looked like he might come back early. But Shanahan said Bosa was a little too optimistic about his return. After one workout, he was sore the next day, and Shanahan called that a bad sign, especially with a game on turf coming up. So they pumped the brakes, and the bye week gave him two extra weeks of rehab. CBS Sports’ Matt Zenitz reported there was a high level of optimism that Bosa would return for the Raiders game. And Shanahan has said Bosa has a chance after the bye.

If he’s cleared, this pass rush could look completely different. Bosa forces offenses to change their protection plans before they even break the huddle. He draws the chip help. He draws the slide protection. He draws the tight end staying in to block. Every one of those adjustments opens something up for the rest of the defensive front. And now, with DeForest Buckner and Osa Odighizuwa on the inside, Mykel Williams getting healthy, and young guys like Gracen Halton, Romello Height, and Keion White all getting home, this front could suddenly look loaded.

That said, a calf injury is exactly the kind of thing you don’t want to push with a player like Bosa. So much of what makes him dangerous is that first explosive step off the snap. If the calf isn’t right, he isn’t really Bosa. And putting a limited version of him on the field doesn’t help anyone. Remember, Buckner and Bosa only played one season together, back in 2019, Bosa’s rookie year, and that defense ranked among the best in the league in sacks. The Niners want the real Bosa next to Buckner, not a hobbled one.

There’s more on the list. Deebo Samuel has been recovering from the hamstring injury he suffered in Week 5 in Seattle. He’s missed two games, and a Week 9 return fits the expected timeline, but nothing is guaranteed. Deebo plays such a physical style, with so much contact after the catch, that the staff might decide he needs a little more time.

Demarcus Robinson has been on injured reserve since his high ankle sprain back in Week 2 against Miami. He’s been eligible to return since Week 7, and his recovery timeline points right at the Raiders game or shortly after. If Deebo isn’t ready, Robinson’s return would add some badly needed depth at receiver. And remember, before he got hurt, Robinson had five catches for 84 yards and a touchdown in the first two games.

A few other players could benefit from the extra rest too. Safety Marques Sigle has been out since an ankle injury in Week 4 and is likely to play after the bye. Linebackers Luke Gifford and Garret Wallow, and defensive tackle C.J. West, also have potential paths back. And Upton Stout, who’s been managing a shoulder issue, was expected to be full-go when the team returned to practice.

On the flip side, not everyone is coming back soon. Rookie receiver De’Zhaun Stribling is on track for a possible return in December. And defensive end Sam Okuayinonu, who’s recovering from a foot injury, is tentatively targeting January. Shanahan admitted that receiver and the defensive line are still the team’s most obvious injury concerns.

Imagine the version of this team where McCaffrey, Evans, and Bosa are all cleared. The run game is dangerous again. Purdy gets his big target back. Kittle gets more one-on-one looks. The defensive front gets its best pass rusher alongside Buckner and Odighizuwa. That’s a massive upgrade from the team that lost in Atlanta, and the front office wouldn’t even have to make another phone call. The healthiest version of this roster might be the best trade of all.

And the Raiders are a perfect test for that kind of return.

Las Vegas comes in at 4-4. They just snapped a four-game losing streak by beating the Jets 38 to 33 on the road, coming back from a nine-point second-half deficit. The headline name on that team is rookie quarterback Fernando Mendoza, the first overall pick in the draft. He’s got some real weapons around him too. Tight end Brock Bowers caught seven passes for 92 yards and two touchdowns against the Jets. Ashton Jeanty is a dangerous running back who can also catch out of the backfield, and he broke a 45-yard catch-and-run in that game. And on special teams, Dylan Laube returned a kickoff 95 yards. After what happened in Atlanta, that’s something the 49ers’ coverage units have to take very seriously.

And while we’re on special teams, let’s talk about the kicking game, because it’s quietly become a concern. Eddy Pineiro’s missed extra point in Atlanta was his fourth miss of the season. Yes, he hit the game-winning 44-yarder in Seattle, and we love him for that. But in a close game, an extra point here and a kickoff return there can be the difference between a win and a loss. You can bet the coaching staff spent a big part of the bye week on that phase, because the Atlanta tape on special teams was not pretty, and those are the kinds of mistakes that come back to bite you in January. Against a Raiders team that just took a kickoff back 95 yards, the coverage units need to be locked in from the opening kick.

But here’s where it gets interesting. The Raiders’ run defense has been a real problem. Against the Jets, Braelon Allen ran for 121 yards on 18 carries in the first half alone, and New York scored on every possession before halftime. If McCaffrey is healthy, that’s a matchup Shanahan will want to attack from the very first series. Run the ball, control the clock, keep Mendoza and Bowers on the sideline.

On the other side, the Raiders still have Maxx Crosby. And Crosby can wreck a game plan all by himself. He’s been vocal during the losing streak, calling on his teammates to bring more energy and play tougher. The 49ers’ offensive line will have its hands full. And that’s why getting Bosa back matters so much. If both teams have an elite edge rusher on the field, the matchup feels a lot more balanced. So this isn’t a throwaway game against a struggling opponent. It’s a real chance to see how all the returning pieces fit together under pressure.

And that brings us back to the clock. The Raiders game is on Sunday, November 8th. The trade deadline is Tuesday, November 10th, at 4 p.m. Eastern. That’s roughly 48 hours. By the time that game ends, Lynch and Shanahan should finally know which of their injured stars are really back, and which holes on this roster still need outside help. If McCaffrey, Evans, Deebo, and Bosa all look healthy, maybe they stand pat. If the receiver room still looks thin, the phones in Santa Clara start ringing.

And one name that has started floating around the 49ers, at least in the hypothetical sense, is Garrett Wilson.

On October 26th, CBS Sports writer Tyler Sullivan put together a list of three potential landing spots for Wilson if the Jets ever decided to move him. The 49ers were on that list, along with the Chiefs and the Raiders. Former Seahawks quarterback Russell Wilson even named San Francisco as a possible destination.

But I want to be really clear about what that article is. It’s an analysis piece. A hypothetical exercise. Sullivan himself framed it as what would happen in the event the Jets decide to move him. There’s no indication that the Jets have put Wilson on the market, and there’s no report that San Francisco has made an offer. This is a conversation, not a negotiation. It’s the kind of list writers put together every year as the deadline approaches, and most of those ideas never turn into anything real.

So why is Wilson’s name even coming up? Part of it is a moment from the Jets’ loss to Miami. Wilson was seen yelling during a sideline huddle that included quarterback Geno Smith and passing game coordinator Seth Ryan. He later explained that his frustration wasn’t aimed at Smith, and that he was upset about the red-zone play calling. That doesn’t mean he wants out of New York. Players get heated in losing games all the time. But when it happens on a struggling team just a couple of weeks before the deadline, people start connecting dots that may not actually connect.

And Wilson addressed the noise himself. He said he sees his name flying around on social media because the Jets are losing, but he has no idea whether they’ll trade him. His focus, in his words, is to go out and run good routes, catch the ball when it’s thrown his way, and have a good impact on his teammates. That’s a professional answer.

There’s also the fact that the Jets have already shown they’re willing to move big names. Last year, they traded Sauce Gardner and Quinnen Williams for future draft capital. But here’s the important part. Wilson reportedly wasn’t among the players they were shopping back then, because the organization saw him as part of its future. So there’s no guarantee the Jets would even pick up the phone.

On the field, Wilson has 37 catches on 51 targets for 444 yards and three touchdowns this season. He’s 26 years old, and he’s under contract through 2030. That last detail is huge. He wouldn’t be a rental. Whoever gets him would be getting a top receiver for years, which is exactly why the price would be steep. Most people think it would take at least a first-round pick, and maybe more.

For San Francisco, the logic is easy to see. In the loss to Atlanta, with Deebo and Evans both sidelined, a lot of the receiving work fell to Jordan Watkins, Jacob Cowing, and Brandin Cooks. That’s not the kind of group you want carrying the passing game for a contender. And even if everyone comes back healthy, adding Wilson would give Purdy one more reliable target on the downs that decide games. Picture an offense with a healthy McCaffrey, Evans, Deebo, Kittle, and Garrett Wilson all on the field together. Good luck matching up with that.

Wilson also fits the way Shanahan likes to build a passing game. He’s a precise route runner who creates separation at every level of the field, and he’s dangerous after the catch. In this offense, receivers who can win quickly on timing routes and then turn short throws into long gains are gold. Purdy has always been at his best when he can get the ball out on rhythm to someone who’s already open. Wilson would give him another one of those players, and he’d do it without a long adjustment period. Shanahan’s offense asks a lot of its receivers as blockers, so there would be some learning there. But the route running and the hands would translate right away.

Sullivan pointed to the 49ers’ record and their division lead as the reason they belong in the conversation. A team sitting at 6-1 with a real shot at the top seed is exactly the kind of team that makes a big midseason swing.

But let’s look at the other side, because there are real reasons this might not make sense.

First, the cost. A first-round pick, maybe more, for a receiver who’d be stepping into a room that’s about to get healthier. Second, the money. Wilson’s contract runs through 2030, and the 49ers already have big commitments to Purdy, McCaffrey, Kittle, Warner, and Bosa. Adding another top-of-market receiver contract has consequences for years. Third, the timing. If Evans, Deebo, and Robinson all come back for the Raiders, and Stribling returns in December, the need gets smaller every week. And fourth, the Jets might simply say no. Every report says they view Wilson as part of their future.

And remember, the 49ers already made their deadline move with Buckner. They also got linked to Jauan Jennings a few weeks ago. Lynch has shown he prefers smart, short-term, mid-round deals in this situation. A Garrett Wilson trade would be a completely different kind of move. A long-term, franchise-level investment. That’s not impossible, Lynch traded for McCaffrey in the middle of a season, after all. But it would be a major departure from the approach he’s taken so far this year.

So here’s how I see it. Watch the Raiders game closely. If the returning receivers look healthy and the offense is humming, I don’t think the 49ers chase Wilson. If Evans can’t go, Deebo has a setback, and the passing game sputters again, then the conversation gets a lot more real in those 48 hours before the deadline. Either way, this is a fascinating moment for the franchise.

And one more thing on the standings. Even after the Atlanta loss, the Niners still own a two-game lead in the NFC West, and they’ve already beaten the Rams and the Seahawks. A win over the Raiders keeps the pressure on everyone chasing them for the top seed in the NFC.

So let’s bring it home. The 49ers are 6-1. They took their first loss in Atlanta, but it came with half the roster in street clothes and four turnovers. John Lynch went out and brought back DeForest Buckner for a fourth-round pick, a move that could transform the defensive interior for the rest of the season. Christian McCaffrey, Mike Evans, and Nick Bosa all have a real chance to return against the Raiders. Deebo, Robinson, and Sigle could be right behind them. And 48 hours after that game, the trade deadline arrives, with Garrett Wilson’s name hanging in the air.

This could be the moment the 49ers go from a great team surviving injuries to a complete team ready for a Super Bowl run. The best part? A lot of the reinforcements are already in the building.
