Before the game, the analysts were confident. Some predicted a double-digit 49ers win. The matchup looked like strength against weakness. The 49ers came in with the most efficient offense in the NFL, and Dallas came in with one of the worst defenses in the league, near the bottom in efficiency and against the run. The question was whether Dallas could pick its poison against Kyle Shanahan's shifts, motions and personnel groupings. The answer, it turned out, was no. But the other question, whether the 49ers' defense could slow down Dak Prescott, CeeDee Lamb and George Pickens, turned out to be just as important.

Let's start with the offense, because it was truly historic.

The 49ers gained 584 yards. That's the most they've gained in any game, regular season or playoffs, since Kyle Shanahan took over in 2017, and the fifth-most in franchise history. It was the most yards the Cowboys have ever allowed in a home game. The 49ers' 46 points were the most any 49ers team has ever scored against Dallas. And they scored six touchdowns, three by McCaffrey, two by Deebo and one by Kaelon Black.

And think about the setting. This was the 42nd meeting between these two historic rivals, in front of a sellout crowd of 93,715 at AT&T Stadium. This rivalry goes back to The Catch in January 1982, through three straight NFC Championship games in the early 1990s, to playoff meetings in recent years. And now the 49ers have won four straight against Dallas.

Brock Purdy was spectacular. He completed 22 of 28 passes for a career-high 389 yards, with three touchdowns and no interceptions, for a passer rating of 154.5. He was sacked five times, so it wasn't easy. But every time Dallas got pressure, Purdy seemed to find a way to make something happen. And this was his first career game in Dallas. He's now 4 and 0 against the Cowboys.

Then there's Christian McCaffrey. Faithful, this was one of the greatest all-around games of his career. He finished with 223 scrimmage yards and three touchdowns. He caught four passes for 161 yards and two touchdowns, his most receiving yards ever in a game, and the most by any running back since 2013. He also ran 13 times for 62 yards and a touchdown. And according to ESPN, he became the first player in NFL history with at least 160 receiving yards, two receiving touchdowns, 60 rushing yards and a rushing touchdown in the same game. The first. Ever.

His touchdowns told the story of this game. In the second quarter, Purdy escaped pressure and found McCaffrey, who turned it into a 74-yard catch-and-run touchdown, the longest touchdown of his career. Purdy said afterward that he wasn't planning to throw it, and then he just felt Christian pop open. McCaffrey credited Purdy for keeping the broken play alive. In the fourth quarter, McCaffrey added a 16-yard touchdown run. And then, with just over three minutes left, with Dallas surging, he caught a 66-yard touchdown that essentially sealed the win. He's now scored a rushing touchdown in eight straight games, which ties for the third-longest streak ever by a player 30 or older.

It's worth putting McCaffrey's day in the context of his season, too. He came into this game leading every running back in the NFL in EPA per rush. He'd scored a rushing touchdown in seven straight games. People spent the summer asking whether he'd lost a step at 30. On Sunday, he answered that question as loudly as possible. And he did it one week after passing Marshall Faulk for the most receiving touchdowns by a running back in the Super Bowl era. When McCaffrey is healthy and fresh, there's still nobody like him.

And then Deebo Samuel came back. After missing three games with a hamstring injury, Deebo made an immediate statement. On the 49ers' opening drive, he caught an 11-yard touchdown pass from Purdy. Then, in the third quarter, he took a reverse handoff from Kaelon Black and raced 70 yards for a touchdown, with Purdy and Trent Williams throwing blocks to spring him. He finished with 109 scrimmage yards and two touchdowns. Afterward, Deebo said the ceiling for this offense is as high as they set it. After a month on the sideline, he played like a man who had something to prove.

And Trent Williams played, which was huge news after he hurt his shoulder in practice on Thursday and was listed as questionable. Shanahan had admitted on Friday that he wasn't totally at ease about it, and Vederian Lowe was ready to start if needed. Not only did he play, but he was out there throwing a block on Deebo's long touchdown.

Kaelon Black also scored, on a 25-yard run to open the second half, the second touchdown of his young career. He finished with 53 rushing yards on 11 carries. George Kittle caught six passes for 74 yards. Jordan Watkins caught two passes for 49 yards.

And then there was Keenan Allen's debut. Remember, he had only three practices with the 49ers before this game. He caught his first pass on the opening touchdown drive, a six-yard gain. He finished with five catches for 61 yards, and four of those catches went for first downs. In the second quarter, Purdy targeted him three times in six plays, and every one of them moved the chains, including a diving 23-yard catch on third-and-12 that set up an Eddy Pineiro field goal. Shanahan said Allen made all the plays they went to him on. Purdy called him a true pro who does a good job of creating separation. Kittle said Allen made catches when it counted. And Allen himself kept it simple: "We were just playing ball." That's exactly what the 49ers traded for: a veteran who could walk into a new offense and convert third downs right away. For a sixth-round pick, that's an incredible return on investment, and he's only going to get more comfortable as he learns the playbook.

Kittle summed up the offense perfectly after the game. He said it makes the offense incredibly dangerous, but added that they need to stay on the field more. And that's the right attitude, because as great as the offense was, the other side of the ball is where the real story of this game is.

Now, let's talk about how this game went from a comfortable lead to a nail-biter. The 49ers led 17 to 16 at halftime. Dak Prescott had already thrown two touchdown passes to George Pickens, including a 35-yarder in the first quarter. In the second half, the 49ers pulled away. Black's 25-yard touchdown run made it 24 to 16. After a Dallas interception, Deebo's 70-yard reverse made it 31 to 19. And McCaffrey's touchdown run pushed the lead to 19 points.

But Dallas wouldn't go away. Prescott hit Pickens and Ryan Flournoy for touchdowns, and the Cowboys cut a 19-point deficit to six in the final four minutes. That's when McCaffrey's 66-yard touchdown came through. Then CeeDee Lamb scored again to make it a six-point game, and Dallas tried an onside kick. The 49ers recovered it, and then held off a final Hail Mary attempt. Game over. The 49ers escaped. But it was much closer than anyone expected. A game that looked like a blowout late in the third quarter turned into a test of nerves in the final minutes.

Let's also give Purdy's history against Dallas its due, because this game added another chapter. As a rookie, he beat the Cowboys 19 to 12 in a divisional round playoff game in January 2023. In October 2023, the 49ers won 42 to 10 on Sunday Night Football, with Purdy throwing four touchdown passes. In 2024, they won 30 to 24, again on Sunday Night Football. And now, in his first game at AT&T Stadium, he threw for a career-high 389 yards in a 46 to 40 win. Four games, four wins. Dallas simply hasn't found an answer for him.

The Cowboys also lost several players during the game. Cornerback DaRon Bland injured his ankle and didn't return. Defensive tackle Otito Ogbonnia hurt his finger in the second quarter. And right guard Tyler Booker was replaced by T.J. Bass after an ankle injury. Kicker Brandon Aubrey even missed an extra point, a rare miss for one of the league's best kickers, and in a six-point game, that mattered. Dallas fell to 4 and 6 and now hosts Tennessee next week.

So let's be honest about the defense, because it was a problem. Prescott completed 30 of 51 passes for 343 yards and five touchdown passes, tying his career high. Pickens had 11 catches for 145 yards and three touchdowns, his first career three-touchdown game. Lamb had seven catches for 72 yards and a touchdown. Flournoy added six catches for 71 yards and a touchdown.

Sports Illustrated's 49ers site graded the defensive backs with an F and the defensive line with a D. They pointed out that Pickens did most of his damage against Deommodore Lenoir. And when Renardo Green left the game in the second half with his back injury, rookie Ephesians Prysock had to step in. Before this game, Prysock had played only nine defensive snaps all season. He gave up a touchdown and several other catches. That's not a knock on the rookie, who was thrown into a very difficult spot. Facing a receiver like Pickens in a road game, in your first meaningful snaps of the season, is about as hard as it gets in this league. It's a sign of how thin the cornerback room is right now, with Nate Hobbs still not activated.

And the pass rush, again, didn't finish plays. The official stats credited Dre Greenlaw with a sack, but no defensive lineman got Prescott on the ground. Osa Odighizuwa, Mykel Williams and Keion White each hit Prescott, but DeForest Buckner and Nick Bosa didn't. And Bosa was flagged for being offside three times. That's now two straight weeks without a sack from the defensive line, and it's the clearest warning light for this team. Against elite quarterbacks in January, a pass rush that can't get home is a recipe for disaster.

Speaking of Odighizuwa, this game had an extra storyline for him. The 49ers acquired him from Dallas in March for a third-round pick, and he's been one of their best defensive linemen since Week 1. With Buckner and Bosa next to him, the hope is that he'll see more one-on-ones. He did generate pressure on Sunday. But the group as a whole needs to be much more disruptive.

There were some bright spots on defense. Fred Warner had 11 tackles, a quarterback hit, and a forced fumble that officials appeared to miss. Malik Mustapha had an interception, although it came on fourth down and actually cost the 49ers about 30 yards of field position. And the defense made the plays it needed at the very end, recovering the onside kick and stopping the final heave. But the 49ers have now allowed 29 points to Atlanta, 17 to the Raiders, a game that would have been much closer without five second-half takeaways, and 40 to Dallas. That's a trend worth watching.

Let me be fair to the defense, too. It was missing key pieces. Green left the game. Hobbs and C.J. West were inactive. Gracen Halton is out for the season with a fractured and dislocated ankle. And Dallas has two of the best outside receivers in the league in Lamb and Pickens. But when you're the number one seed in the NFC, and you're planning to win a Super Bowl, you can't give up 40 points to a 4 and 5 team.

So what can Raheem Morris do to fix it? First, the pass rush has to win with four. Before the Buckner trade, ESPN Research had the 49ers' pressure rate when rushing four or fewer at 23 percent, second worst in the league. The Buckner trade and Bosa's return were supposed to fix that. So far, the results have been mixed. Against the Raiders, there were pressures but no sack from the line. Against Dallas, there were hits from Odighizuwa, Williams and White, but the only official sack came from Greenlaw. Bosa, who missed five games with a calf injury, is still working his way back into game shape, and his three offside penalties suggest he's pressing. Buckner is 32 and may need a lighter workload. And Morris' blitzes didn't land against Prescott. Something has to change.

Second, the cornerback room needs bodies. Green's back is a concern. Lenoir struggled against Pickens. Upton Stout has been dealing with shoulder and neck issues. Prysock is a rookie with very little experience. Activating Hobbs isn't just helpful now. It's urgent.

Third, the safeties need to help more on deep shots. Prescott hit Pickens for touchdowns of 35 and 39 yards. The Vikings, with Jefferson, will test the 49ers downfield too. Safety help over the top, and better communication on the back end, will be critical.

And here's the encouraging part: this defense has shown it can be elite. It held the Rams to seven points in Week 1. It held the Raiders to 24 rushing yards and forced five takeaways two weeks ago. The talent is there. Warner is playing at an extremely high level. The front has Bosa, Buckner, Odighizuwa, White and Mykel Williams. The issue is execution, health and depth, and those are things that can improve quickly, especially with a full week of practice in Colorado Springs.

So, let's get to the injuries. After the game, Shanahan said Green was the only injury, and that he left in the second half with his back issue and didn't return. It may be a flare-up of the back problem from the Raiders game, which kept him out of two practices last week. Shanahan said, "That's it for now," and promised a fuller update when he speaks again. The team will likely be careful with Green this week. That makes the Hobbs decision even more important. His practice window opened on November 4th, so the 49ers have until the end of this week to activate him, or he stays on injured reserve for the season. With Green's back acting up again, activating Hobbs for Mexico City seems likely. C.J. West could also be activated, which would help a defensive line that needs more bodies after the Halton injury.

And then there's Mike Evans. Shanahan said last week that Evans is "real close" to returning, and that "it would be hard to keep him out if this was the last game." He's been resting his Achilles tendinitis since leaving the Washington game on October 19th. With the Vikings next, in a game that could decide the top seed, Mexico City might be when we see him back on the field.

Evans' return would also change how defenses play the 49ers. With Evans on the outside, Allen in the slot, Deebo moving around the formation, Kittle at tight end and McCaffrey out of the backfield, there's no way for a defense to cover everyone. On Sunday, the 49ers put up 584 yards without Evans. Imagine what that offense looks like with him. That's the vision Jerry Rice was talking about last week when he said the 49ers should win the whole thing if the receivers get healthy at the same time.

There's one more complication to keep in mind with Allen. He's facing league discipline after being charged with two misdemeanors related to drunk driving in Indianapolis before the season. Adam Schefter has reported a suspension of at least three games is likely, and Shanahan said the timing could go either way. If it comes soon, the 49ers would lose Allen right as the schedule gets toughest. That's another reason Evans' return matters so much.

And there's a deadline coming on Brandon Aiyuk, too. The NFL reinstated him last week, and the 49ers have been using an unpaid roster exemption that runs through November 23rd, the day after the Vikings game. So we could get a resolution on Aiyuk, most likely a release, very soon. Shanahan has said the reinstatement means absolutely nothing to the team. And after Sunday's offensive explosion, it's hard to imagine Aiyuk being part of the plan.
