Two of them are big names with Hall of Fame-caliber résumés. Two of them are under-the-radar depth moves that could quietly matter more than you think. So who are they? How do they fit? Did John Lynch do enough? And are the 49ers really the team to beat in the NFC, or did they miss their shot at a bigger splash? Today, I'm breaking down every addition, every grade, and every question mark, along with the latest injury news heading into Sunday's game in Dallas. Let's start with the facts, because there's been a lot of noise. John Lynch made exactly two trades before the deadline. That's it. And both came with the same trade partner: the Indianapolis Colts, who are 2 and 7, have lost five straight, fired general manager Chris Ballard last month, and are clearly in teardown mode. On top of those two trades, the 49ers added two depth pieces to the practice squad. So let's go through all four, one by one.

Move number one is the headliner: DeForest Buckner. On October 27th, the 49ers sent a 2027 fourth-round pick to the Colts for the three-time Pro Bowl defensive tackle. This is the man the 49ers drafted seventh overall in 2016. In 2020, they traded him to Indianapolis for the 13th overall pick, which became Javon Kinlaw. John Lynch has called that trade probably the toughest thing he's ever done as general manager. Now Buckner is back, and the timing couldn't be better.

Buckner had four sacks for the Colts this season, tied for their team lead. And here's what makes this move so interesting. He now plays alongside Osa Odighizuwa, one of the better interior pass rushers in football. On paper, that duo gives San Francisco an interior pass rush that could be sensational. Yahoo's grade on the 49ers' deadline was a B, and the writer made it clear that Buckner carries the grade. He called Buckner one of the best defensive tackles in the league, and said pairing him with Odighizuwa gives the 49ers a potentially elite interior rush.

Buckner also joins a defensive line that includes Nick Bosa, Mykel Williams and Keion White. That's a front with real talent, assuming everyone stays healthy, which has been the problem all season. And in his first game back, against the Raiders, his stat line was quiet: a tackle and two quarterback hits. But Kyle Shanahan said the pass rush had a good game and that it got Kirk Cousins on the ground a number of times. The official stat sheet credited no sacks, and the 49ers were credited with nine pressures. So there's room to grow. But the potential is obvious.

And let's not forget what this reunion means emotionally. When Buckner learned the trade was done, he said he fell to his knees and started praying. He called this season Super Bowl or bust. He said his old teammates had spent years telling him to come back home. And after the Raiders game, he said it felt like they never skipped a beat.

Move number two is the receiver everyone was waiting for: Keenan Allen. Just hours before the deadline on Tuesday, the 49ers traded a 2027 sixth-round pick to the Colts for Allen. He's a six-time Pro Bowler. He's 34 years old. And he played his college football at Cal, just across the Bay. He's also the fastest player in NFL history to reach 1,000 career catches, doing it in 159 games, and he now has 1,095 catches for his career, along with more than 12,400 receiving yards.

The reason for the trade is obvious. The receiver room has been decimated all season. Ricky Pearsall has been out since having knee surgery in August. Rookie De'Zhaun Stribling is on injured reserve. Christian Kirk landed on injured reserve with a knee injury. Mike Evans is dealing with Achilles tendinitis and a rib issue. And Deebo Samuel is working back from a hamstring injury. Allen brings stability. In eight games with the Colts, he caught 40 passes for 351 yards and a touchdown. Last year with the Chargers, he had 81 catches for 777 yards. He isn't a deep threat anymore, but he knows how to get open, he knows how to read coverages, and he can step into an offense without a long learning curve.

That matters, because Shanahan has said it's very difficult for a receiver to learn his system in the middle of the season. And on Wednesday, Allen got only about two hours of installation before his first practice. But Shanahan said Allen makes the team a lot better, praising his ability to separate, his hands, his blocking and his work ethic. The staff will decide on his role for Sunday based on how he looks by Saturday.

And here's the fun part. Allen played against the Cowboys last Sunday, catching four passes for 26 yards for the Colts. So if he's ready this Sunday, he'll face Dallas for the second straight week. That gives him fresh film, and fresh memories, of exactly what that defense does.

There's one complication, though. Allen faces league discipline after being charged with two misdemeanors related to drunk driving in Indianapolis before the season. Adam Schefter has reported he faces at least a three-game suspension. Shanahan said this week that the team is waiting on the league, and the timing could go either way, meaning it could still come this season. That's a real risk, and it's part of why the price was so low. The Colts were losing him at the end of the season anyway, and the 49ers were willing to accept the uncertainty in exchange for a proven veteran at almost no cost.

Move number three is a quieter one: KhaDarel Hodge. The veteran receiver signed with the practice squad on November 2nd. He was actually with the 49ers earlier this season, but a knee injury led to his release with an injury settlement on October 1st. So he already knows the system and the culture. And he's a former Pro Bowl special teamer from his days in Atlanta.

Why does this move matter? Because when you have as many injuries as the 49ers do, the players at the bottom of the depth chart matter. The team elevated Hodge for the Raiders game, mainly for special teams. And he played well, earning a 79.8 grade from Pro Football Focus. Kick coverage has been one of the few real weaknesses on this team, and Shanahan admitted this week that the coverage has to get better, with one player out of position on each bad play. A proven special teamer like Hodge can help fix that. It isn't glamorous, but it's the kind of depth that wins games in November. Special teams plays rarely make highlight shows, but a missed tackle on a kickoff return can flip a close game in an instant, and contenders can't afford those mistakes.

Move number four is another under-the-radar addition: Robert Beal Jr. And this is the one most fans probably missed completely. The 49ers signed the defensive lineman to their practice squad on November 3rd. Beal was a fifth-round pick of the 49ers in 2023, 173rd overall, and he spent his first three seasons with the team, including four playoff games. He started this year with the Miami Dolphins, played in two games, recorded one tackle, and was released on October 30th. The 49ers brought him back almost immediately.

Beal is a former Georgia Bulldog, part of those dominant Georgia defenses, and in college he had 10 and a half sacks and 12 tackles for loss in 54 games. His NFL numbers are modest: 27 games, one start, 25 tackles and a sack. But he knows this defensive scheme, and he can be elevated to the active roster if injuries pile up. And after what happened to Gracen Halton, that insurance matters even more. Halton, a key young defensive lineman, suffered a fractured and dislocated ankle against the Raiders. He needs surgery, and his season is over. The defensive line depth, which was already a concern, just got thinner. A player like Beal, who knows the system, can help fill the gap.

And notice something about the two practice squad moves. Both Hodge and Beal are familiar faces. Hodge was with the team in training camp and early in the season. Beal spent three seasons in this building. That's not an accident. In the middle of a season, the hardest thing for a new player is learning the playbook, the terminology and the expectations. Players who've already been through it can be ready to contribute almost immediately. It's the same logic that made Buckner such an appealing target: he already knew the culture, the coaches and many of the players. This front office values familiarity, especially when injuries force quick decisions.

So those are the four additions. Two big. Two small. All four with a clear purpose. And now, the question everyone is asking: did the 49ers do enough?

Let's look at the reaction. As I mentioned, Yahoo gave the 49ers' deadline a B. The writer said Buckner carries the whole grade and that Allen is useful depth but doesn't move the needle much. And he noted one blemish: the 49ers didn't add another premier edge rusher, which could have taken them over the top. Though he also pointed out that no other team landed a premier pass rusher either, because the market simply didn't offer one. Khalil Mack was the only logical target, and the Chargers chose to keep him.

Another Yahoo analysis also gave the 49ers a B, listing Buckner's return and Allen's addition as winners. On the other side, it listed two misses. First, the 49ers reportedly wanted Josh Downs, the 25-year-old slot receiver, but he went to Kansas City. And second, there was no trade for Brandon Aiyuk.

And fans have debated the bigger names the 49ers didn't land: Maxx Crosby, Garrett Wilson and Brian Thomas Jr. But the reality is that the top targets mostly weren't available. The Jets made it clear there was no way they'd trade Wilson. And the Raiders weren't moving Crosby. So I think the measured approach makes sense. You can't trade for players whose teams refuse to move them. The real priority for this team is getting healthy for January. Lynch improved the roster at two positions of need, and he did it without giving up a first, second or third-round pick. The trade-off is that the 49ers now have just five picks in the 2027 draft, their fewest since 2020. But they kept their premium picks, which means that if a big opportunity comes up in the offseason, or if injuries force another move, they still have flexibility.

It's also worth looking at how this deadline fits into John Lynch's track record. In 2019, he traded for receiver Emmanuel Sanders, who helped the 49ers reach the Super Bowl. In 2022, he made a blockbuster for Christian McCaffrey, sending second, third and fourth-round picks in 2023, plus a fifth-rounder in 2024, to Carolina. In 2023, he added pass rusher Chase Young from Washington for a third-round compensatory pick. Lynch has never been shy about making deadline moves when he believes the team can win. This year, he chose two smaller, targeted deals over one huge splash. That tells you how he views this roster: good enough to win, but needing a couple of specific pieces.

And here's something that doesn't show up in deadline grades: the internal additions. Over the last two weeks, the 49ers got Bosa back from a calf injury, Robinson back from injured reserve, Hobbs off injured reserve, C.J. West back to practice and, now, Deebo back to practice. In a way, those returns are as important as any trade. Nobody gave up a draft pick for them. They're simply the reward for patience. When you add Buckner and Allen on top of that, the 49ers' roster looks very different from the one that lost in Atlanta.

And let's not forget the young players who emerged while everyone was hurt. Jordan Watkins, a fourth-round pick in 2025, caught four passes for 86 yards and his first career touchdown against the Raiders, and Shanahan said he's taken a huge step forward. Rookie running back Kaelon Black ran for 70 yards on 11 carries, and Shanahan said he and Christian McCaffrey are basically interchangeable in the run game. Rookie linebacker Jaden Dugger played just 12 defensive snaps against Las Vegas and still returned an interception for a touchdown. Those players aren't trade acquisitions, but they've expanded what this roster can do. When the veterans are healthy and the young players are confident, you get the kind of depth that survives a long season.

Now think about how Allen fits with all of that. With Watkins stretching the field on the outside, Allen can work underneath and in the slot, where he's always been at his best. Kittle won't face as many double teams, because defenses will have to respect a veteran who catches everything on third down. McCaffrey should find more space on checkdowns. And when Deebo is fully back, Shanahan can move him all over the formation, as a receiver, a running back or a decoy. The puzzle pieces fit. The question is just how quickly they can come together.

And through all of it, Brock Purdy keeps producing. He's completing about 69 percent of his passes. His career passer rating of 105.6 became the highest in NFL history among quarterbacks with at least 1,500 attempts back in Week 6. He threw his 100th career touchdown pass against the Raiders, in just his 57th regular season game. And Prisco wrote that he's now in the MVP conversation. With more weapons coming back, that conversation could get a lot louder.

Now, let's talk about the NFC picture, because this is where it gets exciting. The 49ers are 7 and 1. ESPN and CBS Sports' Pete Prisco both have them second in their power rankings, behind only Buffalo. Pro Football Talk has them third. And Niner Noise, in a post-deadline ranking, called the 49ers the team to beat in a loaded NFC. Their take was that Allen and Buckner might not reshape the hierarchy of the conference, but they give the 49ers a baseline of competence at the two positions that threatened to hold them back. And even if both players are past their peaks, they can still give the team reliable options in the playoffs.

That's a fair assessment, and I think it's exactly how the front office sees it too. These aren't superstar additions in their primes. They're reliable veterans on cheap deals, which is exactly the type of move a contender makes. And the rest of the NFC West is dealing with injuries of its own. The Rams placed Puka Nacua on injured reserve with knee sprains and turf toe, and Davante Adams was already on IR. Seattle is managing Sam Darnold's quad injury and cracked rib. Arizona is 3 and 6 and starting Gardner Minshew. The 49ers lead the division by two and a half games.

But I want to be honest about the risks, because it wouldn't be fair to just cheer.

First, the pass rush. The 49ers didn't record a sack against Kirk Cousins last Sunday, even with Bosa back and Buckner in the lineup. The defense forced five takeaways, which covered it up. But if the edge rush doesn't generate more pressure, a good quarterback in the playoffs could pick this defense apart. The 49ers didn't add a premier edge rusher, so they're betting on Bosa, Buckner, Odighizuwa and the young players to get it done.

Second, age. Buckner is 32. Allen is 34. Both can help, but they're not going to get better with time. And if either one gets hurt, the 49ers have fewer options.

Third, the receiver room. Even with Allen, the group is still heavily dependent on Evans and Deebo getting healthy. If those two stay out, the offense will lean on veterans who are good, but not elite. And with Allen's possible suspension, there's another layer of uncertainty.

There's also one more subplot. With the receiver group getting healthier, there are questions about Brandin Cooks' role. Cooks has done a solid job filling in, and Shanahan said this week that he's been effective. But Allen's arrival could shift his snaps.

Let's go a little deeper on that edge rusher debate, because it's the biggest question hanging over this deadline. Before the Buckner trade, ESPN Research had the 49ers' pressure rate when rushing four or fewer players at 23 percent, second worst in the league. In the loss in Atlanta, they pressured Michael Penix Jr. on just three of his 34 dropbacks. Against the Raiders, with Bosa back and Buckner in the lineup, the number of pressures went up, but they still didn't record a sack. The available edge rushers at the deadline were limited. The Jets kept Will McDonald IV, even after he requested a trade. The Chargers kept Khalil Mack. So the 49ers are betting on internal improvement: Bosa getting back to full speed after his calf injury, Buckner and Odighizuwa collapsing the pocket, Mykel Williams developing, and Keion White continuing his strong season. If that bet pays off, nobody will remember that they didn't trade for a pass rusher. If it doesn't, it'll be the first thing people point to in January.

Now, the latest injury news, because it's Dallas week. On Thursday, both starting cornerbacks, Deommodore Lenoir with his foot injury and Renardo Green with his back injury, missed practice for the second straight day. That's a real concern. And two key starters were downgraded to limited on Thursday: left tackle Trent Williams and linebacker Dre Greenlaw. Mike Evans remains out, and Shanahan calls him week-to-week, with no timetable.

But there's good news too. Deebo Samuel returned to practice on Wednesday after missing three games, and according to Matt Lively, Deebo expects to play on Sunday. Nate Hobbs and Demarcus Robinson were full participants. And defensive tackle C.J. West is back on the practice field. If Green and Lenoir can't play, Hobbs, who started his career with the Raiders before signing with San Francisco this offseason, could make his 49ers debut in a big role.

The Trent Williams news is worth watching closely. Earlier this week, Shanahan said the 38-year-old left tackle is playing at a really high level, maybe as well as he ever has statistically. He got a rest day on Wednesday, which is normal for a veteran. But being listed as limited on Thursday adds a little uncertainty. If Williams can't play at full strength, that's a big deal against a Dallas defense that still has dangerous pass rushers. The same goes for Greenlaw, who had one of his best games of the season against the Raiders, with eight tackles, three tackles for loss and a fumble recovery. Friday's report will tell us a lot about both of them.

And there was one more quiet piece of good news from the deadline. Shanahan said several teams called about backup quarterback Mac Jones in the 48 hours before the deadline, and that he's very glad Jones is still with the team. In a season full of injuries across the league, having a veteran backup who has started plenty of NFL games is real insurance. It's the kind of move, or non-move, that doesn't show up in trade grades but could matter a lot if something happens to Purdy.

Let's look at the Cowboys, too. Dallas is 4 and 5 after beating the Colts 27 to 23 last week. Their injury report has been long. Tight end Jake Ferguson, cornerback Joey Porter Jr., safeties Malik Hooker and P.J. Locke, and several others missed Wednesday's practice. George Pickens was limited with a hamstring injury. The Cowboys' defense has ranked near the bottom of the league in DVOA. The game is Sunday, November 15th, at 1:25 Pacific on FOX.

And here's a fun note. This will be Brock Purdy's first career game in Dallas. He's faced the Cowboys three times before, and all three were in Santa Clara: the 19 to 12 divisional round playoff win in January 2023, the 42 to 10 blowout on Sunday Night Football in 2023, and the 30 to 24 win in 2024. He's 3 and 0 against them. Now he gets to see what it's like in their building.

Let's revisit those three Purdy games against Dallas, because they're a big part of why 49ers fans feel confident heading into this one. In January 2023, as a rookie, Purdy led the 49ers to a 19 to 12 divisional round win over the Cowboys, a tough, physical playoff game where the defense held Dallas in check. In October 2023, on Sunday Night Football, the 49ers destroyed the Cowboys 42 to 10. Purdy threw four touchdown passes that night, and George Kittle caught three of them. And in October 2024, again on Sunday Night Football, the 49ers won 30 to 24, a game remembered for a flea-flicker touchdown to Kittle. Three games, three wins. Purdy has always played well against this team. The difference this time is the setting: a loud road crowd at AT&T Stadium.

And the Cowboys are a team fighting for their season. At 4 and 5, they're behind in the NFC East race and need wins. They'll be desperate, they'll be at home, and they have dangerous players on offense, starting with George Pickens, if his hamstring allows him to play. Road games in November against desperate teams are always tricky. But if the 49ers play their style, running the ball, protecting Purdy and taking care of the football, they should be in great shape.

After Dallas, the schedule gets tougher. The 49ers face the Minnesota Vikings in Mexico City, against one of the best defenses in the NFL, with Jauan Jennings, who chose to stay in Minnesota, potentially on the other sideline. Then Seattle comes to Levi's Stadium on November 30th. That's a three-game stretch that could decide the top seed in the NFC. And it's why the depth Lynch added this month could end up mattering so much.

So what's my overall take? I think the 49ers handled this deadline the right way. They addressed their two biggest needs without mortgaging the future. They didn't panic. They didn't give up a premium pick. They added two veterans who can help right now, plus two depth pieces who give the roster more flexibility. Is it a perfect deadline? No. The lack of an edge rusher is a legitimate question. But the 49ers are 7 and 1. They're first in DVOA. They lead the NFL with a 52.7 percent offensive success rate. And the best news of all is that their stars are returning. Bosa is back. Deebo is close. Evans will return eventually. When this team gets fully healthy, it could be scary, and the rest of the NFC knows it.

Let me also put the Aiyuk situation in context, since it was one of the "misses" in those grades. The NFL reinstated Aiyuk on Monday, and he passed his physical on Tuesday. The 49ers didn't trade him or release him before the deadline. Instead, they're expected to use an unpaid roster exemption that runs through November 23rd. The 49ers also say he owes them around 13 million dollars in bonus money. Shanahan has said the reinstatement means absolutely nothing to the team. So, while it might show up as a "miss" in trade grades, it doesn't affect the football team. And there's a good chance this ends with a release in the coming weeks.

Here's what I'll be watching in the coming days. First, the final injury report on Friday, especially for Deebo, Allen, Green, Lenoir, Trent Williams and Greenlaw. Second, how Buckner and Odighizuwa work together in the middle of the line. Third, whether the pass rush finally finishes plays and gets home. And fourth, how Allen fits into the offense, because his first few games will tell us a lot.

Let me also give you the counterargument one more time, because I think it's important to hear both sides. Some fans believe the 49ers should have been more aggressive. They point to Jacksonville, which added Jessie Bates III, Jordan Davis and Davon Godchaux, or to Chicago, which sent two first-round picks to Tennessee for Jeffery Simmons. Those teams went big. The 49ers went efficient. In January, we'll find out which approach was right. But here's what I'd say: the 49ers already had the better roster before the deadline. They didn't need to overpay to catch up. They needed to fill specific holes. And that's exactly what they did.

So, back to the headline. Did four new players join San Francisco? Yes: two trades and two practice squad additions. Were they shocking? Maybe not in the way the headline suggests. But they show a front office that is calm, smart and focused on January.
