Let's start with the news, because there's been a lot of it since Sunday night. The first major item is the official word on right tackle Colton McKivitz. After the game, Kyle Shanahan said the team believed McKivitz suffered a broken and dislocated left ankle, comparing it to Fred Warner's injury last season. He called it crushing. McKivitz is out for the year, and he's officially listed as out for Sunday. Shanahan confirmed that Vederian Lowe, the veteran swing tackle signed in March on a two-year deal worth $9.25 million in base value, will take over at right tackle for the rest of the season. Shanahan said Lowe has played a lot of football and stepped up really big in the Vikings game, and that he'll continue to do that the rest of the year.

The second item is in the secondary. Deommodore Lenoir left the Mexico City game with a foot injury and didn't return. Shanahan mentioned it at the start of his postgame press conference. It's the same foot that bothered him earlier this month against the Raiders, when he played through the issue in Dallas after barely practicing. According to Sports Illustrated's injury list for this week, Lenoir is questionable for Sunday. That's worth watching closely, because Lenoir made one of the biggest plays of the Vikings game, stripping Justin Jefferson after a catch in the third quarter. Linebacker Dre Greenlaw, who missed the Vikings game with a neck issue, is also questionable, along with defensive end Khalid Kareem.

And that brings us to Nate Hobbs. The veteran cornerback has been practicing fully while on injured reserve, and his 21-day practice window, which opened on November 4th, has reached its deadline. The team's options were to activate him, which would require cutting someone to open a roster spot, let him revert to season-ending injured reserve, or release him. Shanahan explained earlier this month that keeping him on reserve was a roster decision, not a question of health or ability, and said he wouldn't hesitate to activate him if it made sense for the 53-man roster. Make sure to check the latest transaction report before Sunday, because if Lenoir can't go, Hobbs's experience could matter.

The third big item is Brandon Aiyuk. The 49ers released him on Monday, on the final day of his roster exemption, closing one of the strangest chapters in recent franchise history. According to NBC Sports, Aiyuk cleared waivers on Tuesday and is now a free agent, which means the 49ers are no longer responsible for his contract going forward. The release still left the 49ers with a dead cap charge of about $12.35 million, according to OutKick. According to Yahoo Sports, Aiyuk is scheduled to visit the Washington Commanders as a free agent on Wednesday, and NFL Network's Ian Rapoport has called Washington his most likely landing spot. Aiyuk played college football at Arizona State with Commanders quarterback Jayden Daniels. He hasn't played a snap since tearing his ACL in October 2024. For the 49ers, it's closure. For Aiyuk, it's a fresh start. And who knows, these two could see each other again at some point.

It's worth remembering just how good Aiyuk was at his peak, because it makes the ending even more unusual. In 2023, he earned second-team All-Pro honors with a career-high 1,342 receiving yards, and his chemistry with Purdy was a huge part of the 49ers' run to the Super Bowl. After a long contract standoff in the summer of 2024, he signed a four-year, $120 million extension. Just seven games later, he tore his ACL. Within a year, the guaranteed money had been voided, and the relationship never recovered. Lynch said it was safe to say Aiyuk had played his last snap with the 49ers. Shanahan said it was something he had never seen in 22 years of coaching. Now it's officially over.

Now let's turn to Sunday's game. The Seahawks visit Levi's Stadium on Sunday, November 29th, with kickoff at 1:25 p.m. Pacific, 4:25 p.m. Eastern, on FOX. The 49ers are 9 and 1. Seattle is 6 and 4. According to DraftKings, via Sports Illustrated, the 49ers are three-point favorites, with a total of 46.5 points. That's a tight line for a matchup between a 9 and 1 team and a 6 and 4 team, and it tells you oddsmakers respect Seattle. Some sportsbooks have the 49ers at three and a half. Either way, a field goal or so is usually what a home team gets against a quality opponent, so the market sees this as a fairly even matchup with a home-field edge.

And the stakes are big. A win would give the 49ers a regular season sweep of Seattle and push their lead in the NFC West to four games. More importantly, it would keep them firmly in control of the race for the NFC's number one seed, which comes with the conference's only first-round bye and home-field advantage throughout the playoffs. After the Vikings win, one FTN Fantasy projection cited by Niner Noise put the 49ers' chances of earning the top seed at around 81%. Every win from here keeps pushing that number higher.

And let's quickly remember how the 49ers got here, because the Mexico City win was a statement. They beat the Vikings 35 to 10, ending Brian Flores's perfect record against Shanahan's offenses. According to ESPN, Minnesota blitzed Purdy on 20 of his 26 dropbacks, the highest rate he's faced in his career, and he completed 17 of 19 of those throws for 208 yards and two touchdowns. For the game, he went 21 of 26 for 255 yards, three touchdowns and no interceptions. George Kittle caught two touchdowns and passed Terrell Owens for the second-most receiving yards in franchise history. Mike Evans returned from his Achilles injury and caught a touchdown. Ji'Ayir Brown returned an interception for a touchdown. Nick Bosa got his first sack of the season. And the crowd in Mexico City chanted "M-V-P." After the game, Shanahan said simply that you can't hesitate against a defense like that, you've got to let it rip, and that Purdy did exactly that, over and over, while protecting the ball.

Shanahan also gave some memorable quotes about his veterans. He called Mike Evans a first-ballot Hall of Famer. He said people don't talk about Keenan Allen enough, and that Allen and Evans have been two of the best receivers in the league over the last 15 years. And he praised Ji'Ayir Brown, saying he believes the 49ers have as good a safety group as there is in the league. Those are the kinds of players the 49ers will lean on against Seattle.

Now let's go back to the first meeting, because it tells you a lot about this rivalry. On October 11th in Seattle, the 49ers won 32 to 29 to improve to 5 and 0. It was absolute chaos. According to the Associated Press, the two teams combined for seven turnovers, the most in any NFL game this season at the time. Seattle committed four of them, and the 49ers committed three. And here's the key detail: San Francisco's first-half touchdowns were all connected to Seattle mistakes. Two touchdown drives started after turnovers and covered just 11 and 18 yards. And rookie defensive tackle Gracen Halton stripped running back George Holani and recovered the fumble in the end zone for a touchdown. The 49ers led 21 to 13 at halftime.

Then Seattle came roaring back. An interception by Nehemiah Pritchett set up a short Seahawks touchdown drive, with Sam Darnold finding Jaxon Smith-Njigba to give Seattle a 23 to 21 lead. Deebo Samuel lost a fumble on the next 49ers possession, and he also strained his hamstring on that play, which cost him two games. Seattle kicked a field goal to go up 26 to 21. That's when Purdy took over. After an intentional grounding penalty put the 49ers in second-and-21, Purdy hit Mike Evans for 28 yards. He and Evans connected four times for 75 yards on that drive, which ended with a 7-yard touchdown pass to Brandin Cooks. Evans caught the two-point conversion to put the 49ers ahead 29 to 26. Seattle tied it with a field goal. Then Purdy moved the 49ers 43 yards for Eddy Pineiro's 43-yard field goal with 1:56 left. And on Seattle's final drive, Smith-Njigba dropped a third-down pass at the 49ers' 25-yard line, and on fourth down, Malik Mustapha stopped Cooper Kupp one yard short of the first down. Game over. The 49ers escaped Seattle still unbeaten.

Mike Evans was the star of that game. That was also the game in which he caught his 111th career touchdown, tying Tony Gonzalez. Purdy called him a game changer afterward. But it was also the game where Evans first felt the Achilles pain that cost him three games. Now he's healthy again. He caught six passes for 109 yards and a touchdown in his return in Mexico City and said afterward that he feels amazing. Seattle will have to figure out how to handle him all over again, and this time, Keenan Allen is on the field too.

There's one more detail from that first game worth remembering. Dre Greenlaw was ejected in the second quarter after a late hit on Darnold led to a confrontation, during which Greenlaw head-butted linebacker Ernest Jones IV. Greenlaw was later suspended for a game. Emotions run high in this rivalry, and both teams will need to keep their discipline this time around.

Coming off the bye, with Darnold potentially back and two weeks to prepare, they'll be dangerous.

Now let's talk about why this game could be a trap. First, the schedule. The 49ers have just finished one of the most demanding stretches any team will face this season. They beat the Cowboys in Dallas, flew straight to Colorado Springs, practiced at altitude for a full week, flew to Mexico City, played a high-intensity prime-time game, and then flew home. That's two straight weeks away from home. Shanahan himself said before the trip that being on the road for a week can sharpen a team's focus, but that you can't do it every week, because players need their families and need time away from each other. This week, the 49ers finally get that time. But it's fair to wonder whether the physical and emotional toll shows up on Sunday.

Second, Seattle is rested. The Seahawks are coming off their bye week, which means they've had extra time to heal and extra time to prepare. Mike Macdonald, one of the sharpest defensive minds in the league, has had two weeks to study the 49ers' offense, including the way Purdy dismantled Brian Flores's blitz. Expect Seattle to have a very specific plan.

Third, there's the emotional letdown factor. The Mexico City game was billed as the biggest regular season game of the year. The 49ers won it in dominant fashion, heard MVP chants for their quarterback and celebrated with a stadium full of fans. Coming back home to play a 6 and 4 division rival can feel like less of an event. But in this rivalry, there's no such thing as an easy game. The first meeting came down to a fourth-down stop on the final drive.

So what does Seattle bring? Let's start with the defense, because that's the Seahawks' greatest strength. According to Sports Illustrated, Seattle's defense ranks sixth in opponent EPA per play and first in opponent success rate, and it allows just 4.0 yards per play, the lowest in the league. That's an elite unit. It's different from Minnesota's defense. Macdonald doesn't blitz on 70% of snaps like Flores. Instead, his defense is built on disguise, discipline and a physical front that wins at the line of scrimmage. It forces offenses to execute play after play without giving up big plays. That makes it a very different test for Purdy than the one he passed in Mexico City.

Think about what that means in practice. Against Minnesota, the answer to the pressure was speed: get the ball out before the blitz arrives and let the playmakers run in space. Against Seattle, there may not be as many free rushers to beat, but there also won't be as many open spaces. Macdonald's defenses are known for taking away easy throws and forcing quarterbacks to hold the ball a beat longer. That's when a pass rush can get home even without blitzing. So Purdy will need patience as well as speed. He'll need to take the checkdowns when they're there, avoid forcing throws into tight windows, and wait for the explosive play to come to him. That's the kind of game where Evans's ability to win contested catches and Allen's ability to find soft spots in zone coverage become extremely valuable.

And the Seahawks have the personnel to make that plan work. Linebacker Ernest Jones IV is one of the most physical players in the division, and he's been at the center of the emotions in this rivalry. Byron Murphy II gives them a disruptive presence in the middle of the defensive line. And Seattle's secondary was physical enough in the first meeting to make Purdy work for everything until the fourth quarter. Remember, the 49ers' first-half touchdowns that day came mostly from turnovers, not from long drives. If Seattle cleans up its mistakes, the 49ers' offense will have to earn every point.

And that's where the offensive line becomes the biggest story of the week. Without McKivitz, the 49ers will have Vederian Lowe making his first start at right tackle against one of the best defensive fronts in the league. That's a big ask. McKivitz had been one of the most reliable tackles in football this season. According to ESPN, he had started 61 straight games and was leading all qualified tackles in pass block win rate. According to Pro Football Focus, cited by NBC Sports Bay Area, he had the longest active streak among tackles of snaps without allowing a quarterback hit, at 270. That's an enormous amount of consistency to replace.

Lowe has experience. He was ready to start at left tackle in Dallas if Trent Williams couldn't go, and he held up well in Mexico City against the league's most aggressive blitz after McKivitz went down. But Seattle's defensive front is built to win one-on-one battles. Expect the 49ers to give Lowe help, with chip blocks from George Kittle and the running backs, quick throws that get the ball out of Purdy's hands fast, and a run game that keeps the defense honest. That's exactly the blueprint that worked against Minnesota. And with Trent Williams still playing at an elite level on the left side, the 49ers can focus their help on the right.

It's also worth remembering that the 49ers' offensive line has been one of the best in football at protecting the quarterback this season. According to Sports Illustrated, the 49ers had allowed only 13 sacks through their first nine games, the third-fewest in the league, though five of those came in Dallas. In Mexico City, against the league's most aggressive blitz, Purdy was sacked just once. A big reason for that is how quickly Purdy gets the ball out, and how well the receivers and running backs adjust to pressure. That system will help Lowe. But one-on-one, against Seattle's defensive front, he'll have to hold his own. Keep an eye on how often the 49ers keep a tight end or running back in to help him, especially early in the game.

Now let's look at the Seahawks' offense, where the big question is at quarterback. Sam Darnold has missed the last couple of games with quadriceps and cracked rib injuries suffered in Week 8 against the Chicago Bears. According to Yahoo Sports, Darnold returned to practice on November 23rd, and Macdonald said the plan is for Darnold to start against the 49ers, adding that nothing is official yet and that they'd see how Wednesday goes. Darnold is officially listed as questionable. In the meantime, Drew Lock has played well. According to Yahoo Sports, he threw for 240 yards and two touchdowns in a win over the Raiders in Week 10, and he has seven touchdowns, no interceptions and a completion rate above 73% this season. Some Seahawks fans even want Lock to stay in the lineup.

So which quarterback would the 49ers rather face? It's an interesting question. According to Sports Illustrated, Darnold ranks 25th in adjusted EPA per play and 26th in completion percentage over expected among starting quarterbacks this season. He threw two interceptions in the first meeting against the 49ers. But he's also the more experienced starter, with a big arm and the ability to make plays outside the pocket. Lock has been more efficient but has a smaller sample. Either way, the 49ers' defense needs to bring the same energy it brought against Kirk Cousins in Mexico City.

And that energy was impressive. In Mexico City, the 49ers held Minnesota to 10 points. Kirk Cousins threw for 235 yards with no touchdowns and an interception. According to FOX Sports, the 49ers pressured him on 47.6% of his dropbacks and hit him seven times. Fred Warner had eight tackles, a tackle for loss and a pass breakup. And the Vikings averaged under three yards per carry while the starters were on the field. That was a big response from a defense that had been heavily criticized after giving up 40 points in Dallas. If the 49ers can play like that again, Seattle's offense, with a banged-up line and a quarterback returning from injury, could have a very difficult afternoon.

Seattle's offensive line is also banged up. According to Sports Illustrated, guard Quenton Nelson, acquired from the Colts at the trade deadline, is doubtful with a knee injury, and left tackle Charles Cross is questionable. Defensive tackle Leonard Williams is questionable too. Yahoo Sports also mentioned injuries to right tackle Abraham Lucas. If Seattle's line is missing multiple starters, that's a big opportunity for the 49ers' pass rush. Nick Bosa finally recorded his first sack of the season in Mexico City. Mykel Williams, Keion White and the interior of DeForest Buckner and Osa Odighizuwa have been generating pressure for weeks. Against a weakened Seattle line, this could be the game where those pressures turn into sacks and turnovers.

The Seahawks still have dangerous weapons, though. Jaxon Smith-Njigba is one of the best young receivers in football. Cooper Kupp brings veteran savvy. Rashid Shaheed adds speed. And the running back group, led by Zach Charbonnet, can punish a defense that loses its discipline. The 49ers' secondary, especially if Lenoir is limited, will have to communicate better than it did in Dallas.

Now, here's the good news for the Faithful. Brock Purdy has been at his best against the toughest opponents. According to one preview, he's four and oh this season against the Rams, Broncos, Seahawks and Vikings, with ten touchdown passes and just two interceptions in those games. And in his last two games, against Dallas and Minnesota, he's thrown six touchdown passes and no interceptions. According to the Associated Press, he leads the NFL with a 118.2 passer rating. The 49ers lead the league in scoring at 31.6 points per game and in yards per play. And the offense finally has all of its weapons healthy at the same time: Evans, Allen, Deebo Samuel, Kittle, Christian McCaffrey and Jordan Watkins.

There's also an interesting veteran stat from the Associated Press. The 49ers have 34 touchdowns this season from players aged 30 or older, two short of the NFL record. That experience matters in a game like this. Veterans don't get rattled by a hostile atmosphere, a physical defense or a close score late in the fourth quarter. They've been there before.

So what do the 49ers need to do to win? Let's go through it.

First, protect Purdy. With Lowe stepping in at right tackle, the protection has to be sharp. That means quick throws, smart use of chip blocks, and a running game that keeps Seattle's front honest. McCaffrey has a rushing touchdown in eight straight games, and he scored again in Mexico City. Rookie Kaelon Black has added physicality and toughness. If the 49ers can run the ball on first down, Seattle can't pin its ears back.

Second, win the turnover battle. The first meeting had seven turnovers. The 49ers can't count on Seattle giving them three short fields again. In Mexico City, the 49ers didn't turn the ball over at all, and they forced two takeaways. That's the formula. Protect the ball, and take it away when the opportunity comes.

Third, stay disciplined. Penalties have been an issue at times this season. The 49ers were flagged 11 times for 100 yards in Dallas, and they had some costly flags in Mexico City too, including a holding penalty that wiped out a 50-yard punt return. Against an elite Seattle defense, giving away free yards could be the difference.

Fourth, finish the pass rush. Seattle's offensive line is banged up, and the 49ers have been generating pressure for weeks. This is the game to convert that pressure into sacks, hurried throws and turnovers.

And fifth, avoid the letdown. This is a division rival. It's the defending champion. It's rested. It's coached by one of the best young defensive minds in the league. And it knows that a win would cut the 49ers' division lead to two games with six weeks to play. And it would love nothing more than to beat the 49ers at Levi's Stadium and tighten the NFC West race. The 49ers have to bring the same intensity they brought to Mexico City.

Here's my honest take. The 49ers should win this game. They're healthier at the skill positions than they've been all season, their quarterback is playing at an MVP level, and their defense just held a strong Minnesota team to 10 points. But this won't be easy. Seattle's defense is elite, the Seahawks are rested, and the 49ers are playing without McKivitz and possibly Lenoir. Expect a close, physical game, the kind that's decided in the fourth quarter. The first meeting was decided by a fourth-down stop on the final drive. The second one could be just as tight. That's what happens when two teams know each other this well. Every tendency has been studied, every matchup has been tested, and the difference usually comes down to who makes the fewest mistakes and who makes the biggest plays when it matters most. Right now, the 49ers have the quarterback, the weapons and the momentum to be that team. And if the 49ers win it, they'll be in an incredibly strong position heading into December.

There's also a Thanksgiving angle to this week. The 49ers' players will finally be home with their families for the holiday after two straight weeks on the road. That could be exactly what they need to recharge. A short week of rest, some normal routines and a home crowd on Sunday could help them avoid the letdown that so many people are worried about. And after the way the Mexico City crowd embraced them, the Levi's Stadium crowd will want to make sure the home team feels just as welcome back in Santa Clara.
