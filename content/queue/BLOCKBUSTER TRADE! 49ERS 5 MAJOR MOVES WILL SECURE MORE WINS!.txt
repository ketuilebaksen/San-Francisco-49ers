The San Francisco 49ers are five and oh. They’ve beaten the Rams, the Dolphins, the Cardinals, the Broncos, and the Seahawks. Their quarterback is the betting favorite for MVP. And yet, the biggest question around this team right now has nothing to do with what’s happening on the field. It’s about what happens on the phone. Because the NFL trade deadline is November 10th, the market just exploded with a real blockbuster deal, and John Lynch is staring at a roster that’s 5-0 on the scoreboard and banged up everywhere else. So today, we’re going through five moves that could turn this undefeated start into a Super Bowl run. Some of them could be brilliant. Some of them could be expensive mistakes. Let’s figure out which is which.

A two-game lead in the NFC West after that wild 32 to 29 win in Seattle. Brock Purdy sitting at the top of the MVP odds. Mike Evans tying Tony Gonzalez on the all-time touchdown list. A rookie defensive tackle scoring a touchdown on the road. This is a special start. But special starts don’t win championships by themselves. Healthy, deep rosters do. And that’s why this trade deadline matters so much. Before we get into the names, let’s talk about the man who’ll be making these decisions. John Lynch.

Since Lynch and Kyle Shanahan took over in 2017, this front office has never been afraid to make a move in the middle of a season. Think back. In 2017, they traded for Jimmy Garoppolo, and he went on to start for this team for years and take it to a Super Bowl. In 2019, they went out and got Emmanuel Sanders at the deadline, and he became a key part of the team that made it to Super Bowl LIV. That same season, they added pass rusher Jordan Willis. In 2021, they traded for Charles Omenihu, who became a real piece of the defensive line rotation. In 2022, they made the biggest splash of the Shanahan era, trading for Christian McCaffrey. And in 2023, they went after defensive help again, bringing in Randy Gregory and Chase Young.

And look at what those deals actually cost. Garoppolo came over for a second-round pick. Sanders cost a third and a fourth. McCaffrey, the biggest one of all, cost a second, a third, and a fourth-round pick in 2023, plus a fifth in 2024. That’s a lot of draft capital. But ask any Niners fan today if it was worth it. Christian McCaffrey just scored his 100th career touchdown in this uniform. Lynch has proven he’s willing to pay real prices for real difference makers.

So the history is clear. When John Lynch sees the right player at the right price, he picks up the phone. He’s not shy. And this week, one of the most respected voices in franchise history added some fuel to the fire. Steve Young came out and said the 49ers should go all in. His argument was simple: when you’ve got a team this good and a window this open, you don’t sit back and wait. You push your chips in.

But here’s what’s different about this year. In the past, a lot of those moves were made to rescue a season or fill a desperate hole. This time, San Francisco isn’t rescuing anything. They’re 5-0. The goal isn’t to survive. It’s to protect a championship window and make sure that when the games get harder in December and January, there’s no weakness an opponent can exploit over and over again. That’s a very different kind of trade. You’re not looking for a savior. You’re looking for the missing piece.

And let’s be honest about where the weak spots are. Number one, the receiver room. Ricky Pearsall is out for the season. De’Zhaun Stribling is still at least a month away. Demarcus Robinson is on injured reserve. Christian Kirk hasn’t played. And now Deebo Samuel is out for at least a couple of weeks with a hamstring injury. Mike Evans is carrying a huge load with sore ribs at 33 years old. Number two, the pass rush. Nick Bosa has been out with a calf injury since late September. The defense has gotten creative, with Raheem Morris dialing up blitzes and young players like Gracen Halton stepping up, but there’s only so long you can manufacture pressure through scheme. And the numbers show it. A Sports Illustrated analysis pointed out that in the Denver game, Bo Nix faced 13 pressures and wasn’t sacked once. Against Arizona, Jacoby Brissett faced 13 pressures on 54 dropbacks and went down just twice. That’s a lot of quarterbacks with a lot of time to throw. Yes, the defense exploded for five sacks in Seattle, and that was fantastic. But a lot of those came from young players getting their first career sacks and from creative pressure packages. You can’t count on that every week against the best quarterbacks in the NFC.

Now, here’s the twist that changed everything this week. One of the most talked-about receivers on the trade market is already gone. Marvin Harrison Jr., the former top-five pick out of Ohio State, the son of a Hall of Fame receiver, was traded by the Arizona Cardinals to the Houston Texans over the weekend. Arizona got a 2027 second-round pick in return. Harrison had been a popular name in 49ers trade conversations. Through four games, he had just five catches for 85 yards and a touchdown, a sharp drop from his rookie year. A lot of people thought a fresh start in Shanahan’s system could unlock him. Well, that door is closed now. He’s a Texan.

But that trade did something important. It set the market. A second-round pick for a struggling young receiver with huge upside. That’s the benchmark now. If Arizona got a second for Harrison, every team with a receiver to sell is going to start its asking price there. That’s good news for sellers and bad news for buyers like San Francisco. It means Lynch has to be smart about where he spends his picks. And it got the rest of the league talking. Right after the Harrison deal, NFL insider Ian Rapoport said he expected another receiver’s trade market to heat up over the next few days and weeks. And when he was asked about San Francisco, he said that with Deebo out for a couple of weeks, the 49ers make sense. We’ll get to that name in a minute, because it’s a familiar one.

So let’s go through the five moves. Two pass rushers. Two receivers I’d consider. And one receiver I’d stay away from.

Move number one. The future Hall of Famer. Khalil Mack.

Mack is one of the most decorated pass rushers of his generation. He’s a former Defensive Player of the Year, a multiple-time Pro Bowler, and a guy who has made a living wrecking game plans for over a decade. And right now, he’s playing for a Los Angeles Chargers team that’s 0-5. That’s the key. When a team starts 0-5, veterans on short contracts start to become trade chips.

Bleacher Report ranked Mack number one on its trade block big board heading into Week 6. And they named the 49ers as one of the two most logical suitors, along with the Chicago Bears, where Mack spent some of the best years of his career. Here are the numbers. Mack is 35 years old, playing on a one-year deal. Through five games, he has three sacks, 15 tackles, and five tackles for loss. A Sports Illustrated analysis pointed out that his three sacks would lead the 49ers. And his salary cap hit is only around six million dollars. For a half-season rental, that’s very manageable.

There are three reasons why I’d want Lynch to make this call.

First, the cost. Because Mack is 35 and on a one-year deal, he shouldn’t cost a fortune in draft picks. Bleacher Report projected the value at around a conditional 2027 fourth-round pick, and reportedly a couple of executives think it could be closer to a fifth. The Chargers would probably ask for more than that at first, because that’s how negotiations work. But this isn’t a first-round-pick type of deal. Compare that to trying to get a younger pass rusher with years left on his contract, and the price difference is enormous.

Second, the fit. The 49ers need someone who can win one-on-one off the edge while Bosa is out, and even after Bosa comes back, a second proven rusher changes everything. With Bosa on one side and Mack on the other, opposing offenses can’t slide their protection toward one guy anymore. And that frees up Halton, Osa Odighizuwa, Keion White, and Mykel Williams to feast on the inside and in rotation. Mack would also take pressure off Raheem Morris. Right now, Morris has to blitz to create chaos, and blitzing leaves your coverage thinner. A real four-man rush lets you drop more players into coverage, and that’s how you beat good quarterbacks in January.

Third, the experience. Mack isn’t just a pass rusher. He sets the edge against the run. He understands assignments. He’s been in big games. He knows how to prepare. On a defense that’s leaning on so many young players right now, adding a veteran presence like that could help the entire front.

Now, the risks. He’s 35. There’s a reason one scout reportedly said Mack should retire after this season. Bodies break down at that age, and the 49ers already have more than enough injured players. Also, the Chargers haven’t announced they’re selling, and there’s no report that the two teams have talked. And there will be competition. Chicago, Buffalo, and other contenders will be looking at him too. But if the price is a mid-round pick and the money fits, Mack would be at the very top of my list. When you’re 5-0, you don’t add a guy to fix a disaster. You add a guy so that a weakness never becomes a disaster in the first place.

Move number two. The versatile edge rusher. Josh Sweat.

Sweat is a very different kind of target than Mack. He’s younger, he’s under contract longer, and he would cost a lot more. You might remember him from the Philadelphia Eagles, where he was a big part of their pass rush and had a monster game in their Super Bowl win over Kansas City. Then he signed a big four-year deal with the Arizona Cardinals. And last season, he had 12 sacks.

Here’s why his name is coming up. Arizona is 1-4. They just traded Marvin Harrison Jr. And according to reports, Sweat is looking for a new contract and has been named as a trade candidate. Earlier this year, during the summer, the Cardinals made it clear they weren’t planning to trade him. But a lot can change when you start 1-4 and begin selling off pieces.

Sweat is 29 and signed through 2028. That’s the appeal and the problem at the same time. On the positive side, he’s not a rental. He’d be part of this defense for years, rushing next to Bosa and Mykel Williams. He’s played in both four-man and three-man fronts. He can line up as a traditional defensive end with his hand in the dirt or as a stand-up outside linebacker. And he can hold up against the run. That kind of flexibility is gold for a coordinator like Morris, who changes his game plan every single week.

But on the other side, you’re taking on a big contract. Sweat is reportedly looking for a new deal, so whoever trades for him might also be signing up for a raise. And the 49ers already pay big money to Purdy, Bosa, Warner, McCaffrey, and Kittle. Adding another large contract to the defensive line has long-term consequences. Bleacher Report projected his trade value around a conditional 2027 fourth-round pick, and named the Bears and the Packers as likely suitors, but the real price could depend heavily on how much salary Arizona wants to move.

Also, think about the division angle. The Cardinals are an NFC West rival. Trading a starting pass rusher within the division is never easy. Arizona would probably prefer to send him to the AFC. So while Sweat is an interesting name, I see him as more of a long-term idea than a quick fix. If the money works and the Cardinals are motivated, Lynch should absolutely listen. But Mack is the cleaner fit for right now.

Move number three. The reunion. Jauan Jennings.

Now this is the name that’s got Niner Faithful buzzing. Jauan Jennings spent his first six NFL seasons in San Francisco. Over five seasons of playing time with the 49ers, he caught 210 passes for 2,581 yards and 22 touchdowns. In his final season here, he led all of the team’s wide receivers with 55 catches for 643 yards and nine touchdowns. He was Purdy’s trusted guy on third down. The big, physical receiver who would go over the middle, take a hit, and hold on. And who could forget some of the clutch moments he had in red and gold?

So how did he end up in Minnesota? It’s a little complicated. The 49ers tried to sign him to a long-term deal before the 2025 season, but they couldn’t agree. Before training camp, Jennings asked for a new contract or a trade. The two sides worked out some incentives for that season. Then this past offseason, after the 49ers had already agreed to terms with Mike Evans, Jennings signed a one-year, eight million dollar deal with the Vikings.

And it hasn’t worked out the way anyone expected. Through four games in Minnesota, Jennings has just two catches for nine yards on five targets, even though he’s been on the field for about 74 percent of the offensive snaps. He’s playing, but the ball just isn’t coming his way. And after the Marvin Harrison Jr. trade, Ian Rapoport said on The Pat McAfee Show that Jennings’s name could heat up over the next couple of days and weeks. And then he said it: with Deebo out for a couple of weeks, the 49ers make sense.

Here’s why I love this idea. Jennings already knows Shanahan’s offense. He knows the terminology, the timing, the route concepts, the protections. There’s no learning curve. He knows Purdy. They’ve already built that trust on third downs and in big moments. He’s on a one-year deal, so there’s no long-term financial risk. And he’s exactly the kind of player this receiver room is missing right now. A big, tough possession receiver who can work the middle of the field while Evans works the outside.

But let’s be realistic about the obstacles. First, the Vikings are reportedly 5-0. Contending teams don’t usually sell useful players in October. If Minnesota sees him as a piece for a playoff run, he’s not going anywhere. Second, there’s history. Jennings and the 49ers had a contract standoff. Would both sides be comfortable going back? In the NFL, business is business, and players and teams reunite all the time. But it’s worth mentioning. And third, his production this year is concerning. Two catches in four games is not what anybody expected. Is it the scheme in Minnesota, or has something changed? The 49ers would know better than anyone, because they’ve seen every snap he’s ever played.

If the price is a late-round pick and Minnesota is willing to deal him, I think this is one of the smartest, lowest-risk moves Lynch could make. Bring him home.

Move number four. The experienced option. Jerry Jeudy.

Jeudy is a former first-round pick who knows what it’s like to be a number one target. Just two years ago, he made the Pro Bowl after a 1,229-yard season in Cleveland. That’s real production. But this season, he’s fallen out of the Browns’ game plan. Cleveland has rookies and young players like Denzel Boston, KC Concepcion, and tight end Harold Fannin Jr. getting more of the targets, and one report described Jeudy as yesterday’s news in Cleveland. League sources even called him erratic.

So why consider him? Because the price could be very low and the contract is very friendly this year. His 2026 base salary is reportedly only about 1.5 million dollars. That’s almost nothing for a receiver who was a Pro Bowler two years ago. The Browns reportedly want a third- or fourth-round pick, but Bleacher Report projected his value closer to a conditional fifth-rounder, and named the Chiefs and Eagles as possible suitors. Fox Sports also reported that league executives see Jeudy as one of the receivers most likely to be moved before the deadline.

He’s 27, so he’s still in the prime of his career. He’s got experience, he can play multiple receiver spots, and he’s shown he can make big catches. In this offense, with Evans drawing safety help on the outside, Jeudy could feast on the matchups Shanahan creates. You wouldn’t be asking him to be the number one guy. You’d be asking him to be a reliable number two or three while Deebo heals and the other receivers come back from injury.

The concerns? Consistency has been an issue throughout his career. Drops and focus have come up before. And you have to ask the same question we asked about every receiver on this list: once Deebo, Kirk, Stribling, and Robinson come back, how many snaps would Jeudy actually play? If the answer is “not many,” you’re spending a draft pick on a short-term rental. But if the price is a fifth-round pick, that’s a risk you can live with. I’d put Jeudy on the list, but below Jennings. Jennings already knows the system. Jeudy would have to learn it in the middle of a playoff push.

Now move number five. And this one is different, because this is the receiver I’d avoid. Quentin Johnston.

Johnston is a former first-round pick out of TCU, and physically, he has everything you want. He’s 6-foot-3, around 208 pounds, with size and speed that make scouts drool. He’s also had real production, with 16 touchdowns over the last two years for the Chargers. And with Los Angeles sitting at 0-5, executives around the league reportedly see the Chargers as the team most likely to pull the plug and start selling. So Johnston could absolutely be available.

So why would I stay away? Because the knock on Johnston has always been consistency, and especially his hands. Drops have been an issue throughout his career. And Kyle Shanahan’s offense is built on timing, precision, and trust. When Purdy throws a ball on third down, he has to know that receiver is going to be exactly where he’s supposed to be and that the ball is going to be caught. We saw what that trust looks like with Jordan Watkins against Arizona, and with Mike Evans in Seattle. You can’t build that overnight with a receiver who’s known for dropping passes in big moments.

San Francisco worked too hard to get to 5-0 to introduce another big uncertainty at a position that’s already been hammered by injuries. Johnston has potential, but potential alone isn’t enough when you’re chasing a Super Bowl. Unless the coaching staff believes it can unlock something nobody else has, and the price is extremely low, I’d look elsewhere.

And while we’re talking about receivers, there are a couple of other names worth mentioning. Brian Thomas Jr. in Jacksonville was sensational as a rookie, with 1,282 yards and 10 touchdowns, but he’s been buried on the depth chart this season, and the Jaguars reportedly aren’t interested in moving him. His price would be very high, something like a second-round pick and more. And Calvin Ridley in Tennessee, who we talked about in our last video, comes with a massive cap hit of around 29.7 million dollars and declining production. Both are interesting names, but neither feels like the right fit for this team at this moment.

There are a few other names that have come up too. Tampa Bay defensive tackle Vita Vea was reportedly tied to the 49ers over the summer. And Bleacher Report even listed the Niners as a suitor for Eagles guard Landon Dickerson, although he’s on injured reserve right now. Lynch is going to have plenty of options to consider.

So let’s put it all together. If I’m John Lynch, here’s how I rank the five moves.

Number one: Khalil Mack. Low cost, short commitment, massive impact on the pass rush. He makes this defense better whether Bosa is healthy or not.

Number two: Jauan Jennings. Low cost, zero learning curve, and exactly the kind of tough, reliable receiver this offense is missing right now. If Minnesota is willing, bring him home.

Number three: Jerry Jeudy. Cheap contract, proven production, but a bigger learning curve and some consistency concerns.

Number four: Josh Sweat. Talented and versatile, but expensive and long-term, and a division rival might not want to help you.

And number five: Quentin Johnston. The talent is real, but the risk is too high for a team that can’t afford dropped passes in big moments.

And here’s the thing, Faithful. The 49ers don’t need to make all five of these moves. They might not even need two. One smart, well-priced addition on the defensive line and one reliable receiver could be enough to turn a great team into a complete team.

There’s also one more option that people forget: standing pat. And it’s not a crazy idea. Deebo should be back in a few weeks. Kirk, Stribling, and Robinson all have return timelines. Bosa has a chance to return as early as Monday night. Mykel Williams just made his season debut. Matt Judon is on the practice squad. By December, this could be a much healthier roster without spending a single draft pick. The question is whether the 49ers can afford to wait, and whether those players will actually come back at full strength. That’s the gamble Lynch has to weigh. And the calendar matters here. Between now and November 10th, the 49ers will play a few more games, starting with Washington on Monday night. Every one of those games is basically an audition for the current roster. If Cooks, Watkins, and Cowing step up while Deebo is out, the urgency at receiver fades. If the pass rush keeps getting home with Bosa back, the need for Mack shrinks. But if the offense stalls without Deebo, or the defense gives up another 80-play game like it did against Arizona, the phone starts ringing in Santa Clara.

And speaking of the roster, there’s some news from this week you need to know before Monday night.

Dre Greenlaw’s appeal was denied. Hearing officer James Thrash upheld the one-game suspension for headbutting Seahawks linebacker Ernest Jones, so Greenlaw will miss Monday night against Washington. Kyle Shanahan said he was disappointed in the original decision, but added that it is what it is. The 49ers worked out four linebackers this week to prepare for his absence. With Fred Warner carrying an even bigger load, the linebacker depth is something to keep an eye on as well.

And remember what Washington brings to the table. Jayden Daniels is back from his dislocated elbow, and in his return against the Giants, he ran nine times for 103 yards and two touchdowns. That’s a quarterback who can punish a defense that loses contain. Without Greenlaw, the Niners will need Warner and the defensive front to be disciplined all night long.

On the positive side, Nick Bosa, Kyle Juszczyk, and James Thompson Jr. all have a chance to return against Washington. If Bosa is back, that changes the entire conversation about the pass rush, at least for the short term. But even a healthy Bosa doesn’t eliminate the need for depth. If anything, this season has taught us that you can never have too many good players on the defensive line.

And for those who are worried about Purdy, there’s no need. One quarterback ranking did drop him to number two this week, but Steve Young said Purdy has reached a whole new level. The MVP odds still have him right at the top of the conversation. He’s playing his best football with a receiver room held together by duct tape. Imagine what he could do with one more weapon.

So let’s bring it home. The 49ers are 5-0, the trade deadline is coming fast, and John Lynch has a real opportunity. The blockbuster deal that shook up the market this week happened in Arizona, not San Francisco. Marvin Harrison Jr. is a Texan now. But the 49ers still have options. Khalil Mack could transform the pass rush for the price of a mid-round pick. Jauan Jennings could come home and give Purdy a trusted target. Jerry Jeudy is cheap and proven. Josh Sweat is talented but expensive. And Quentin Johnston is a risk I wouldn’t take.

The lesson from Lynch’s history is clear. When the right player becomes available, he acts. The question is whether he sees the right player on this list. And whether the price makes sense for a team that’s already undefeated.
