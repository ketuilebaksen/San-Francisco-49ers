Now, let's go back, because to understand how far this fell, you have to remember how high it was.

Brandon Aiyuk was the 49ers' first-round pick in 2020, the 25th overall selection out of Arizona State. He grew into one of the most precise route runners in the league. And in 2023, he became a legitimate star. He caught 75 passes for 1,342 yards and seven touchdowns, and he was named second-team All-Pro. That season, he was one of the most efficient deep threats in the entire league. He wasn't a gadget player. He was dangerous downfield, dangerous after the catch, and he did it on the biggest stages, during a run to the Super Bowl with Kyle Shanahan's offense humming around him and Brock Purdy throwing him the ball.

So when contract time came in 2024, it wasn't a question of if. It was a question of how much, and how ugly it would get. And it got ugly. Aiyuk skipped minicamp and asked for a trade. He publicly mentioned the Commanders and the Steelers as teams he'd like to play for. At one point, the Steelers were deep enough in talks that a trade to Pittsburgh looked possible. The 49ers held firm. And then, at the end of August 2024, right before the season started, a deal got done instead: four years, 120 million dollars, with about 76 million dollars reported as guaranteed. A big signing bonus. A big option bonus. By every measure, Aiyuk had won the negotiation.

And then came October 20th, 2024, at home against the Kansas City Chiefs. Aiyuk went down with a devastating knee injury, tearing his ACL and MCL, along with meniscus damage. Season over. He had played just seven games on that new contract. And that's the last time he's taken the field in the NFL. We are now more than two years removed from that moment. Two full years without game film, without highlights, without any public evidence of where he is as a player, other than what people say.

In 2025, he missed the entire season recovering. And that's where the relationship between player and team really started to crack.

I want to be fair here, because there are two versions of this story, and the truth probably sits somewhere in the middle. The 49ers' version is blunt. They say Aiyuk didn't complete required rehab sessions and check-ins, and that he became hard to reach. Aiyuk's version, as best as we can piece it together from his own videos, is that he felt the team was treating him unfairly, and that he wanted to handle things his way.

And in July 2025, the 49ers took a step that even Kyle Shanahan said he'd never seen before. They voided roughly 27 million dollars in guaranteed money tied to Aiyuk's 2026 salary. Shanahan later said, "I've been coaching over 20 years, and I've never been in a situation where a contract's been voided." He also said it takes a lot to get a contract voided. Then, in December 2025, Aiyuk stopped showing up at the facility entirely. The 49ers placed him on the reserve/left squad list. Let me translate that for anyone who doesn't speak NFL transaction language. That's the list where a team puts a player who has left the team. While he's on it, he isn't on the active roster, and he isn't being paid. And the only way back is for the player to apply to the league for reinstatement. He could have done that at any point. It would have cost him nothing in terms of paperwork. And for months, he didn't. Why? Now, let's get to the big question. Why now? Why did Aiyuk wait until late October to do something that cost him nothing, that almost everyone recommended for months?

The popular take is simple: he wants to play football. And that might be part of it. He's 28. He says he's healthy. Now let's look at the 49ers' side of the money, because it matters for what they do next.
