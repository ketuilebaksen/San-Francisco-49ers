Let's start with the facts, because this is the kind of news that gets exaggerated quickly. According to ESPN and NFL.com, Purdy injured his ankle in the second quarter of Sunday night's 35 to 10 win over the Vikings. He finished the game. In fact, after the injury, he kept playing at an extremely high level. He finished 21 of 26 for 255 yards and three touchdowns, with no interceptions. According to ESPN, he had both ankles taped for the final two quarters. And Shanahan said the injury didn't affect his play calling or the offensive approach at all.

So when did Shanahan find out? A reporter asked him exactly that on Wednesday. Shanahan said yes, he was aware. He explained that the medical staff always tells him when a player has something, but then they come back and say the player checked out and should be fine. Purdy didn't miss any plays. Nothing changed during the game. That's why most of us had no idea anything was wrong. Even George Kittle said he didn't know about the injury during the game. And Kittle's reaction says a lot about Purdy's toughness. According to ESPN, Kittle said Purdy is going to play through things that none of us know about.

Now, here's the most important part: the type of injury. Shanahan was clear that this is not a high ankle sprain. He said it's a low ankle injury and that Purdy is day-to-day. That distinction matters a lot. A high ankle sprain, which affects the ligaments above the ankle, can keep a player out for several weeks. A low ankle sprain is usually much less serious, and players often return within days. Dr. David Chao, the former head team doctor of the San Diego Chargers, told Heavy that he agrees with Shanahan's assessment, and that chances are good Purdy will play against Seattle, especially since he finished the Vikings game.

But Shanahan didn't rule anything out. When asked whether there's a concern that the injury could threaten Purdy's availability for Sunday, he said that when a player can't practice on Wednesday, it's always a possibility. And he added that the team has to see how it goes. So the next two days are critical. According to Heavy, Purdy will likely need to practice by Friday to keep his status for Sunday on track. If he's limited on Thursday and full on Friday, that's a great sign. If he misses Thursday and Friday too, the conversation changes.

And if Purdy can't go, it's Mac Jones. According to ESPN, Jones will take the first-team reps in practice, and he would start if Purdy is unable to play. That's a very different situation from a few years ago, when the 49ers' backup quarterback was a major question mark. Think back to the 2022 season, when injuries forced the team all the way down to its third-string quarterback, a rookie named Brock Purdy. Nobody knew what to expect then. This time, the backup is a former first-round pick with a winning record as the 49ers' starter. Jones started eight games last season and went five and three, according to Heavy. He has real starting experience in this offense. Several teams called about him before the trade deadline. Shanahan said at the time that he was very glad Jones was still on the roster. Now we might find out why. Rookie Kurtis Rourke is the other quarterback on the roster.

Jones's career has taken some interesting turns. He was a first-round pick of the New England Patriots in 2021 and made the Pro Bowl as a rookie. After some difficult seasons in New England, he spent time in Jacksonville before signing with the 49ers. Last season, when Purdy was out, Jones stepped in and kept the team afloat, which is exactly what a backup is supposed to do. He knows the offense, he knows the receivers, and he's played in big games before. If he has to start against Seattle, the game plan would likely look a little different, with more emphasis on the running game and quick, high-percentage throws. But Shanahan's offense is designed to put any quarterback in position to succeed, and with Evans, Allen, Kittle, McCaffrey and Deebo around him, Jones would have plenty of help.

Let's be honest, though. Losing Purdy, even for one game, would be a big deal. He leads the NFL with a 118.2 passer rating, according to the Associated Press. Through ten games, he has 2,624 passing yards, 23 touchdowns and just five interceptions, according to 49ers Webzone. On ESPN, Dan Orlovsky said flatly that Brock Purdy is the MVP right now. And in Mexico City, against a Vikings defense that blitzed him on 20 of his 26 dropbacks, the highest rate he's ever faced, he completed 17 of 19 passes for 208 yards and two touchdowns against the blitz. Nobody in the league is playing better at the position right now.

Just think about what Purdy did on Sunday night. The 49ers beat Minnesota 35 to 10, ending Brian Flores's perfect five-and-oh record against Shanahan's offenses. They scored 28 points in the first half, more than the Vikings had allowed in any full game this season. Kittle caught two touchdowns and passed Terrell Owens for the second-most receiving yards in franchise history. Evans caught a touchdown in his first game back from his Achilles injury. Ji'Ayir Brown returned an interception for a touchdown. And Purdy did all of that with an ankle that was bothering him from the second quarter on. After the game, Shanahan summed it up by saying you can't hesitate against a defense like that, you've got to let it rip, and Purdy did exactly that, over and over, while protecting the ball. That makes the performance even more impressive in hindsight.

But here's the good news. Everything we've heard points toward this being a minor injury. Shanahan said it's not a high ankle sprain. A respected team doctor believes he'll play. Purdy finished the game. And let's not forget, Purdy has dealt with injuries before and played through them. Kittle wasn't concerned. So while it's fair to watch closely, there's no reason to panic yet.

Purdy's injury history is worth keeping in mind here, because it shows how tough he is. In the 2022 NFC Championship Game in Philadelphia, as a rookie, he tore the UCL in his throwing elbow early in the game and couldn't throw the ball downfield for the rest of the day. He needed surgery that offseason and came back to lead the 49ers to the Super Bowl the following year. In 2025, he dealt with a toe injury that cost him several games and forced him to manage his practice reps for weeks. Through all of it, he's built a reputation as a quarterback who plays through pain whenever possible. And that's exactly what Kittle meant when he said Purdy plays through things nobody knows about. If there's a way for him to be on the field Sunday, he'll find it.

Now let's talk about the roster moves, because the 49ers made some important ones. First, they activated cornerback Nate Hobbs from injured reserve. According to Pro Football Rumors, it's Hobbs's first action of the season after missing the first 11 weeks with an adductor injury. Hobbs had been practicing fully for weeks, and his 21-day window was about to close. With Lenoir dealing with a foot injury, adding a veteran who can play both inside and outside gives the secondary much-needed depth. Hobbs started his career with the Raiders, then played for the Packers, and he brings real experience to a cornerback room that's been tested by injuries all season.

Second, the 49ers officially placed Colton McKivitz on injured reserve with his fractured and dislocated left ankle. That opened the roster spot for Hobbs. And Shanahan had some emotional words about McKivitz on Wednesday. When asked whether McKivitz was playing at a Pro Bowl level before the injury, Shanahan said yes, he thought so. He said McKivitz played close to that level last year and had only gotten better this year, and that it's really hard for him not to be able to finish the season, because he's had a heck of a year. That's a strong statement from a head coach. And the numbers back it up. According to PFF, McKivitz had an 85.3 overall grade this season and hadn't allowed a sack all year. According to ESPN, he had started 61 straight games and was leading all qualified tackles in pass block win rate.

Third, the 49ers opened the 21-day practice window for offensive lineman Austen Pleasants, who has been on injured reserve. Shanahan said Pleasants has been healthy for a few weeks now, and that with the loss of McKivitz, it makes sense to get him out on the practice field. He said the team will see how his practices go this week, and if he's ready, they'll make a decision. According to Pro Football Rumors, if Pleasants is activated, he would become the top swing tackle option behind the starters.

And that brings us to Vederian Lowe, who's now the starting right tackle. Shanahan confirmed it on Wednesday. He said he thought Lowe played really well when he came in against Minnesota, especially considering all the problems that defense presents. He said that's exactly why the team brought him in. Anytime you have a swing tackle, Shanahan explained, that player had better be capable of starting, and Lowe has started a lot of games in this league and has played like a starter. That's why they targeted him. According to Pro Football Focus, Lowe didn't allow a single pressure in his 38 snaps against the Vikings.

Shanahan also talked about how valuable it had been to have the same five starting offensive linemen through the first ten games. He said it's huge, because the more you play together as an offensive line, and as 11 players on offense, the better you get at moving together, and nothing is more important than the offensive line in that area. He said the team has been very fortunate, and that this is the first big loss. But he also explained why he believes Lowe is ready. Lowe always takes tackle reps on Wednesdays, and then the team rotates him at the other positions on the other days. So he's been preparing for this all season.

Lowe's first full start will come against a very physical Seattle front, and the 49ers will likely give him some help, especially early in the game. Expect chip blocks from Kittle and the running backs, moving pockets and quick throws. That's especially true if Purdy's mobility is limited by the ankle, or if Mac Jones is under center. A quarterback who can't move as freely needs even cleaner protection. That's why the Pleasants situation matters too. If Pleasants is activated, he gives the 49ers an extra tackle in case anything else happens on the line. Depth on the offensive line rarely gets attention until it's needed, and the 49ers just learned how quickly that can happen.

Now let's talk about Seattle, because Shanahan gave a great explanation of the challenge, and it tells you a lot about how the game plan might change from last week. A reporter pointed out that the 49ers just faced an elite defense in Minnesota and are now facing another elite defense in Seattle, and asked how they compare. Shanahan said they're similar in that both have really good numbers, but they're two totally different styles. With Minnesota, he said, it's all about the blitzes, with a threat on every single play. Seattle, on the other hand, is a heavy two-shell defense, meaning it plays with two deep safeties most of the time. Shanahan said the Seahawks mix in some blitzes throughout the game like every team does, but definitely not as many.

That's an important distinction. Against a blitz-heavy defense, the answer is to get the ball out quickly and make the defense pay for sending extra rushers. Against a two-high safety defense, the deep passing game is harder to access, because there are two defenders protecting the back end. The answer there is usually patience: running the ball well, attacking the middle of the field and taking the underneath completions. According to Sports Illustrated, Seattle's defense ranks first in opponent success rate and allows just 4.0 yards per play, the lowest in the league. So whether it's Purdy or Mac Jones, the quarterback will need to be disciplined and efficient.

That's also why the running game could be so important on Sunday. Against two-deep looks, there are often fewer defenders in the box, which can open up running lanes. Christian McCaffrey has scored a rushing touchdown in eight straight games, and rookie Kaelon Black has given the 49ers a physical second option. If Mac Jones has to start, leaning on the running game would make even more sense.

And speaking of McCaffrey, Shanahan gave one of the best quotes of the week about him. A reporter pointed out that very few running backs have aged as gracefully as McCaffrey in terms of production, and asked what has allowed him to keep playing at this level. Shanahan said, first of all, McCaffrey takes care of himself and prepares like no one else. He said that's why the team calls him a psycho, because it's not normal. Everything he does, Shanahan explained, is about preparing to play, whether it's how he sleeps or even how he walks down the hallway. He called it amazing.

And then Shanahan said something that applies to the whole roster. He said that a lot of the 49ers' veterans who are Hall of Fame-caliber players are like that, because they're so talented and so different from everybody else. You can't be a Hall of Fame player, he said, if you aren't like that. But he added that this group is also special because of their awareness and how they play the game. He mentioned the wide receivers, the tight end, the linebacker and the pass rusher. He said they're all very smart players, the game comes naturally to them, and even if they aren't exactly the same as they were at 23, they still have a lot of other special qualities.

And that led to another great moment. A reporter mentioned that Mike Evans and Keenan Allen are considered the "uncles" of the locker room, and asked Shanahan what it's been like having them. Shanahan said it's awesome. He said both of them are still unbelievably talented, but what stands out is their intelligence, their poise, how competitive they are and how good they are at whatever you ask them to do. He pointed to how good they are as blockers, and how quickly they pick up routes they've never run before. Whatever the coaches put in, he said, they figure it out. He called them both generational talents, and said the reason they're still playing at such a high level is that they combine that talent with what he called generational awareness. He added that both players have attributes that are great for a quarterback, including their hands and their size, and that they never seem overwhelmed with what the coaches put in.

That's a fascinating window into how Shanahan sees this roster. And it's not just talk. Evans, in his first game back from the Achilles issue, caught six passes for 109 yards and a touchdown in Mexico City, and Shanahan called him a first-ballot Hall of Famer afterward. Allen, acquired from the Colts at the trade deadline for a 2027 sixth-round pick, has quickly become a trusted target. Shanahan said after the Vikings game that people don't talk about Allen enough, and that he and Evans have been two of the best receivers in the league over the last 15 years. According to the Associated Press, the 49ers have 34 touchdowns this season from players who are 30 or older, just two short of the NFL record. This is a veteran team built on intelligence and experience. And if Purdy can't play on Sunday, that veteran savvy will matter even more. Experienced receivers know how to adjust their routes, find soft spots in coverage and bail out a quarterback when a play breaks down. A backup quarterback surrounded by Evans, Allen, Kittle, McCaffrey and Deebo has a lot more margin for error than one surrounded by inexperienced players.
