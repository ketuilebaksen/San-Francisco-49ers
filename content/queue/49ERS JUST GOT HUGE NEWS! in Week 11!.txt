When Colton McKivitz went down in Mexico City with a broken and dislocated ankle, the biggest fear for the 49ers was obvious. Who protects Brock Purdy now? McKivitz had started 61 straight games. He was leading all qualified tackles in pass block win rate. He hadn't allowed a single sack all season. And his replacement was about to face the most aggressive blitz in the NFL with no warning at all. Well, here's the news that should make every member of the Faithful breathe a little easier. According to Pro Football Focus, Vederian Lowe played 38 snaps after McKivitz went down, and he didn't allow a single pressure. Not one sack, not one hit, not one hurry. But the test is about to get much harder. The Seattle Seahawks are coming to Levi's Stadium on Sunday with one of the most physical defensive fronts in football, two weeks of preparation and the league's leading receiver. So today we're going to dig into the numbers behind the news: what Lowe's performance really tells us, what the PFF grades from Mexico City reveal, why Mike Evans's season numbers are even better than you think, and the matchups that will decide the rematch with Seattle.

Let's start with Vederian Lowe, because his performance is the biggest under-the-radar story of the week. Lowe signed with the 49ers in March on a two-year deal worth $9.25 million in base value, to be the team's swing tackle. That's the backup who can play either side of the line, the insurance policy every team hopes it never has to use. In Dallas, he was ready to start at left tackle if Trent Williams couldn't play through his shoulder injury. Williams played. Then in Mexico City, midway through the second quarter, McKivitz went down, and Lowe was suddenly thrown into the fire on the right side.

According to PFF, as reported by 49ers Webzone, Lowe played 38 snaps against Minnesota. He earned a 72.3 run-blocking grade, which is solid. His pass-blocking grade was 55.6, the lowest among the 49ers' offensive linemen in that game. But here's the important detail: across his pass-blocking snaps this season, he hasn't allowed a single sack, hit, hurry or pressure. In other words, the grade reflects some technique issues, but the results have been clean. And that was against a Vikings defense that blitzed Purdy on 20 of his 26 dropbacks, according to ESPN, the highest rate Purdy has ever faced.

There's one more key detail. According to PFF, the 49ers allowed only five pressures in the entire game, and none of them came from Lowe. One each was charged to Jake Brendel, Connor Colby, Trent Williams, George Kittle and Christian McCaffrey. That tells you a lot about how well the protection held up as a unit. And it tells you something about Purdy too. He got the ball out so quickly that Minnesota's blitz rarely had time to get home.

So should the 49ers feel confident? Cautiously, yes. But not everyone is convinced. Michael Moraitis of The Big Lead suggested that John Lynch should consider calling veteran tackle Taylor Decker, who was released by the Detroit Lions in March, if the coaching staff isn't fully confident in Lowe. Decker was once considered one of the better left tackles in football. The catch is that he has only eight career snaps at right tackle, so it wouldn't be a simple plug-and-play move. For now, Shanahan has made his decision clear. After the game, he said this is exactly why the team brought Lowe in, that Lowe has played a lot of football and stepped up really big, and that he's going to continue to do that for the rest of the year.

And let's be honest about what the 49ers lost. According to PFF, McKivitz had an 85.3 overall grade this season, up from 84.2 last year. His run-blocking grade was 90.9, the best on the team. He had posted a pass-blocking grade of at least 71.7 in each of his last three seasons. And he hadn't allowed a sack all year. According to Pro Football Focus, cited by NBC Sports Bay Area, he also had the longest active streak among tackles of snaps without allowing a quarterback hit. Shanahan called the injury crushing and said Colton has had an unbelievable year. Replacing that kind of consistency won't be easy. But Lowe's start has been encouraging.

And it's worth remembering how much the 49ers' protection depends on the whole system, not just one player. Purdy's quick processing is a huge part of it. In Mexico City, he consistently got the ball out before the Vikings' blitz could arrive. Shanahan put it simply after the game: you can't hesitate against a defense like that, you've got to let it rip. And Purdy did, over and over, while protecting the ball. That same quick decision-making will help Lowe on Sunday. When the quarterback knows where he's going before the snap, even a tackle who's still settling in has less time to get beaten.

Now let's go through the rest of the PFF grades from Mexico City, because they tell a fascinating story. According to 49ers Webzone, the highest-graded player on offense was Mike Evans, at 93.9, in his first game back from Achilles tendinitis. McKivitz was second at 92.6 before his injury. Brock Purdy earned a 91.7. Trent Williams was at 84.7, and right guard Dominick Puni was at 82.1, with the second-best pass-blocking grade in the game.

On defense, the top grade went to safety Ji'Ayir Brown, at 93.3, who also had the best coverage grade in the game and that 18-yard pick-six just before halftime. Osa Odighizuwa was at 91.0. Fred Warner was at 90.0. Deommodore Lenoir, who forced a fumble on Justin Jefferson before leaving with a foot injury, earned a 79.8. And DeForest Buckner was at 73.9.

The pass rush numbers were especially encouraging. Nick Bosa led all players with five pressures and a 74.1 pass-rush grade, and he finally recorded his first sack of the season. Odighizuwa had four pressures and an 80.4 pass-rush grade. Rookie Romello Height also had four pressures. And C.J. West, who was activated from injured reserve the day before the game, earned the highest pass-rush grade in the game, at 82.0, with two pressures. That's a strong debut for West after the 49ers waived Brandin Cooks to make room for him. Adding a healthy West to the rotation gives the defensive line fresh legs for the stretch run.

Not everything was perfect, of course. Safety Malik Mustapha had the lowest defensive grade, at 38.8, with two missed tackles. Linebacker Garret Wallow led the team with three missed tackles. And on offense, McCaffrey had a rare low grade in pass protection. But overall, this was one of the most complete performances of the season.

Let's quickly remember just how dominant that performance was. The 49ers beat the Vikings 35 to 10, ending Brian Flores's perfect record against Shanahan's offenses. They scored 28 points in the first half, more than Minnesota had allowed in any full game this season. Purdy completed 21 of 26 passes for 255 yards, three touchdowns and no interceptions. George Kittle caught two touchdowns and passed Terrell Owens for the second-most receiving yards in franchise history, behind only Jerry Rice. Evans caught a touchdown in his return. Brown returned an interception for a touchdown. And the defense held Kirk Cousins without a touchdown pass. The crowd in Mexico City even chanted "M-V-P" for Purdy. It was the kind of performance that changes how the rest of the league sees a team.

Now let's talk about Mike Evans, because his numbers deserve a lot more attention. Before the Vikings game, Evans had 27 catches for 420 yards and three touchdowns in six games. Add his six catches for 109 yards and a touchdown in Mexico City, and he's now at 33 catches for 529 yards and four touchdowns in just seven games, despite missing about a month. That's roughly 16 yards per catch. One preview also credited him with an impressive rate of contested catches and only one drop all season. His PFF grade of 93.9 in Mexico City was the best on the team.

And Shanahan's comments after the game said it all. He called Evans a Hall of Famer, a first-ballot one, which he said is as good as it gets. He said it's cool to see Evans still competing at such a high level. Evans himself said he feels amazing, and he called Purdy the best player in the game right now. The chemistry between the two has become a major weapon. And it's not just Evans. Purdy connected with Keenan Allen in Mexico City just 12 days after the 49ers acquired him from the Colts. Shanahan said people don't talk about Allen enough, and that Allen and Evans have been two of the best receivers in the league over the last 15 years. That kind of trust, built so quickly, is another reason Purdy belongs in the MVP conversation.

And the MVP case keeps getting stronger. According to 49ers Webzone, through ten games, Purdy has 2,624 passing yards, 23 touchdowns and just five interceptions. Before playing on Monday night, Joe Burrow had 2,795 yards, 27 touchdowns and six interceptions in nine games. Burrow has been the betting favorite, with Purdy second. But Purdy has now thrown three touchdown passes with no interceptions in back-to-back games, against two very different defenses. And according to one preview, he's four and oh this season against the Rams, Broncos, Seahawks and Vikings, with ten touchdown passes and just two interceptions in those games. When you consider that he's done it while working with a constantly changing receiver room, his case gets even more convincing.

And speaking of the offense, the numbers are staggering. According to the Associated Press, the 49ers lead the NFL at 31.6 points per game, and Purdy leads the league with a 118.2 passer rating. They also lead in yards per play. They're converting more than half of their third downs, the best rate in the league. And their red zone touchdown rate, which was 82.8% before the Vikings game, is now around 85%, the best in football. In the last three games, against the Raiders, Cowboys and Vikings, the 49ers have put up 414 yards, then 584 yards, then 35 points against the league's top scoring defense. And they didn't turn the ball over once against Dallas or Minnesota.

Now let's turn to Seattle, because this is where the test gets harder. The Seahawks visit Levi's Stadium on Sunday, November 29th, with kickoff at 1:25 p.m. Pacific on FOX. The 49ers are 9 and 1. Seattle is 6 and 4, coming off its bye week. According to DraftKings, via Sports Illustrated, the 49ers are three-point favorites, with a total of 46.5 points.

Seattle's defense is the real challenge. According to Sports Illustrated, it ranks first in opponent success rate and allows just 4.0 yards per play, the lowest in the league. Unlike Brian Flores's Vikings, Mike Macdonald's defense doesn't need to blitz on every play to create problems. Its front is physical and deep, with players like Leonard Williams, Byron Murphy II and Jarran Reed on the interior, and Uchenna Nwosu and DeMarcus Lawrence on the edges, as one preview highlighted. That's a group that can win one-on-one battles without sending extra rushers. And that's exactly why Lowe's matchup matters so much. This will be his first full start in place of McKivitz, against a defensive line that knows the 49ers' protection schemes well and has had two weeks to plan for him. Against Minnesota, Lowe benefited from the fact that the ball was coming out fast. Against Seattle, if the coverage holds up and Purdy has to wait a beat longer, Lowe will have to hold his ground on his own.

Expect Shanahan to help him. That could mean chip blocks from Kittle and the running backs, quick throws to get the ball out before the rush arrives, play-action and bootlegs that move the pocket, and a heavy dose of McCaffrey and Kaelon Black to keep the defensive front honest. Shanahan has always been creative at protecting his offensive line through play design. This week, that creativity may be one of the most important parts of the game plan.

But there's a weakness in Seattle's defense that the 49ers could exploit. According to one preview, despite allowing only about 17.5 points per game, the Seahawks have given up touchdowns on nearly 70% of opponents' red zone trips. If that number holds, it sets up a fascinating clash. The NFL's best red zone offense against a defense that's been vulnerable once opponents get inside the 20. If the 49ers can consistently move the ball into scoring range, they have a real chance to finish drives with touchdowns instead of field goals.

Now let's revisit the first meeting, because it tells you what the 49ers need to do differently this time. On October 11th in Seattle, the 49ers won 32 to 29 in a game with seven combined turnovers, according to the Associated Press, the most in any NFL game this season at the time. Seattle committed four, and the 49ers committed three. And San Francisco's first-half scoring came almost entirely off Seattle mistakes. One Seattle turnover set up a short touchdown drive of 18 yards. Another set up a touchdown drive of just 11 yards. And rookie Gracen Halton stripped running back George Holani and recovered the fumble in the end zone for a touchdown. That's three touchdowns created by the defense.

The only long, sustained touchdown drive the 49ers put together came late, when Purdy and Evans took over. After an intentional grounding penalty left the 49ers in second-and-21, Purdy hit Evans for 28 yards, and the two connected four times for 75 yards on that drive, which ended with a 7-yard touchdown pass to Brandin Cooks. Evans caught the two-point conversion to put the 49ers ahead 29 to 26. Then Eddy Pineiro kicked a 43-yard field goal with 1:56 left, and Malik Mustapha stopped Cooper Kupp a yard short on fourth down to seal it. Evans finished with seven catches for 113 yards and a touchdown.

So here's the lesson. The 49ers can't count on Seattle handing them three short fields again. This time, the offense needs to prove it can sustain drives against Macdonald's defense without depending on turnovers. That's where the healthy receiver room matters. In Week 5, Deebo Samuel strained his hamstring and left the game. Now Deebo is healthy, Allen has arrived, and Evans is back.

That changes the math for Seattle's defense. In October, Macdonald could focus much of his attention on Evans, knowing the 49ers were thin elsewhere. This time, if he rolls coverage toward Evans, Allen can find the soft spots in the middle of the field. If he loads up against the run, Purdy can attack with play-action to Kittle. And if he tries to take away the deep ball, McCaffrey and Deebo can punish him underneath. That's the advantage of having a complete set of weapons. Defenses can't take everything away, and Purdy has shown all season that he'll find whatever the defense leaves open.

On the other side of the ball, the 49ers' defense has a big challenge of its own: Jaxon Smith-Njigba. According to Seattle Sports, Smith-Njigba leads the NFL with 79 catches, 1,207 receiving yards and 10 touchdowns, with seven games to go. No other receiver in the league has even reached 1,000 yards. He accounts for about 52% of Seattle's receiving yards. Against the Raiders before the bye, he caught eight passes for 171 yards and a touchdown, including 144 receiving yards in the third quarter alone, a franchise record for a single quarter. Seattle moves him all over the formation, which makes it extremely difficult to take him away. Even when everybody knows the ball is going his way, he produces.

But the 49ers have had some success against him. In the first meeting, he did catch a 16-yard touchdown from Darnold. But on Seattle's final drive, he dropped a third-down pass at the 49ers' 25-yard line, setting up the fourth-down stop that ended the game. Smith-Njigba said afterward that he has to reel those in. Expect the 49ers to use a lot of safety help over the top, with Ji'Ayir Brown coming off the best coverage grade in the Vikings game. And if Lenoir, who is questionable with his foot injury, can't play, the cornerback depth will be tested again.

Let's think about what that secondary could look like. Renardo Green, who left the Dallas game with back tightness, returned to full practice before the Vikings game and played in Mexico City. Upton Stout handles the nickel role. Rookie Ephesians Prysock has been the next man up on the outside, though he had a tough time in Dallas and earned one of the lower defensive grades in Mexico City. And at safety, Brown and Mustapha will be critical. Mustapha had a rough night by PFF's standards against Minnesota, with two missed tackles, but he's also the player who made the game-winning fourth-down stop on Cooper Kupp in the first meeting. Against a receiver like Smith-Njigba, communication between the corners and safeties has to be perfect. One busted coverage, and he can turn a short throw into a long touchdown.

And Shanahan clearly trusts this group. After the Vikings game, he said he believes the 49ers have as good a safety group as there is in the league. He also praised Brown for how he stepped in as a rookie when Talanoa Hufanga got hurt and helped the team reach the Super Bowl. That trust will be tested on Sunday.

Then there's the quarterback situation. Sam Darnold has missed Seattle's last two games with quadriceps and cracked rib injuries suffered against the Bears in Week 8. According to Yahoo Sports, he returned to practice on November 23rd, and Macdonald said the plan is for Darnold to start, while adding that nothing is official yet. Darnold is listed as questionable. In his absence, Drew Lock went two and oh, beating the Cardinals and the Raiders, and he has seven touchdowns, no interceptions and a completion rate above 73% this season.

If Darnold plays, the 49ers' pass rush could be the key. According to Sports Illustrated, Darnold ranks 25th in adjusted EPA per play and 26th in completion percentage over expected among starting quarterbacks. One preview also pointed out that his completion rate under pressure has been among the lowest in the league. He threw two interceptions in the first meeting. So the formula is clear: take away his first read, get pressure, and force him to make difficult throws under duress. That's exactly what the 49ers did to Kirk Cousins in Mexico City, with Buckner, West and Romello Height all contributing to the pressure. And Seattle's offensive line is banged up. According to Sports Illustrated, guard Quenton Nelson is doubtful with a knee injury and left tackle Charles Cross is questionable. Right tackle Abraham Lucas also left the Raiders game with an ankle injury. If Seattle is missing multiple linemen, the 49ers' pass rush has a real chance to dominate.

And it's worth looking at how Seattle got here. The Seahawks, the defending champions, have had an up-and-down season. They lost 34 to 17 to the Chicago Bears on Monday Night Football in Week 8, the game in which Darnold was injured. Then Lock led them to wins over Arizona and Las Vegas. They added guard Quenton Nelson from the Colts at the trade deadline to strengthen the offensive line, though he's now dealing with a knee injury. Running back Zach Charbonnet has returned from the torn ACL he suffered in January. And according to Seattle Sports, the Seahawks sacked Raiders rookie quarterback Fernando Mendoza six times in their last game. So this is a team that's been finding ways to win, even while dealing with injuries. At 6 and 4, it's in the hunt for a wild-card spot, and it knows a win at Levi's Stadium would put real pressure on the 49ers in the division.

There's also the rest factor. Seattle is coming off its bye, with two weeks to prepare. The 49ers just finished a grueling stretch: a win in Dallas, a week at altitude in Colorado Springs, and a prime-time game in Mexico City. That's two straight weeks on the road. Now they come home for Thanksgiving week, and they'll need to recover quickly. The risk of a letdown game is real. But this is a division rival, a defending champion, and a team that pushed them to the final drive in October. The 49ers know exactly what's at stake.

The 49ers' injury report will be worth watching closely all week. Deommodore Lenoir is questionable after leaving the Vikings game with a foot injury, the same foot that bothered him earlier this month. Dre Greenlaw, who missed the Vikings game with a neck issue, is questionable, as is defensive end Khalid Kareem. McKivitz is out. And then there's Nate Hobbs, the veteran cornerback who practiced fully while on injured reserve. His 21-day practice window, which opened on November 4th, has reached its deadline, and the team had to decide whether to activate him, let him revert to season-ending injured reserve, or release him. Shanahan said earlier this month that keeping him on reserve was a roster decision, not a question of his health or ability. If Lenoir can't go, Hobbs's status becomes even more important. Discipline will matter too. In Dallas, the 49ers were flagged 11 times for 100 yards. In Mexico City, they cleaned it up significantly, but there were still a few costly flags, including a holding penalty on linebacker Tatum Bethune that wiped out a 50-yard punt return. Against a Seattle defense that allows just 4.0 yards per play, free yards are precious. Every penalty that extends a Seahawks drive or pushes the 49ers' offense backward is a gift Seattle will gladly take.

And finally, there's the emotional side of this week. After two straight weeks away from home, the 49ers finally get to spend Thanksgiving with their families. Shanahan said before the trip that being on the road for a week sharpens a team's focus, but that you can't do it every week, because players want to be with their families and need time away from each other. This week gives them that reset. The question is whether they can recover physically and mentally in time to match the intensity of a rested Seattle team. Levi's Stadium should be loud. The Faithful will want to welcome the team home after Mexico City. And a home crowd in a division game can be worth a lot, especially when the opponent's offensive line is banged up and dealing with crowd noise.

And it's a lot. A win would give the 49ers a regular season sweep of Seattle and push their lead in the NFC West to four games. It would also keep them firmly in control of the race for the NFC's top seed. After the Vikings win, one FTN Fantasy projection cited by Niner Noise put the 49ers' chances of earning the top seed at about 81%. A win over Seattle would push that number even higher.

One more piece of news to wrap up the week: Brandon Aiyuk is officially a free agent. The 49ers released him on Monday, on the final day of his roster exemption, and according to NBC Sports, he cleared waivers on Tuesday. Yahoo Sports reported that he's visiting the Washington Commanders as a free agent, and NFL Network's Ian Rapoport called Washington his most likely landing spot. For the 49ers, it's closure on one of the strangest chapters in franchise history, and the focus can stay entirely on the field.

So let's put the whole picture together. The huge news is that the 49ers' biggest worry after Mexico City, the right tackle position, looks more stable than many feared, at least so far. Lowe didn't allow a pressure against the most aggressive blitz in the league. The PFF grades show a team that played well across the board, with Evans, Purdy, Brown, Odighizuwa and Warner leading the way and the pass rush finally finishing. Evans's season numbers are elite, even with the missed time. And the offense leads the league in scoring, yards per play and red zone efficiency.

But Seattle is a different kind of test. An elite defensive front, a rested team, the league's leading receiver, and a coach who's had two weeks to prepare. The 49ers have to protect Purdy, sustain drives without relying on turnovers, and keep Jaxon Smith-Njigba from taking over the game. If they do that, they'll be 10 and 1, with a four-game lead in the division and a firm grip on the NFC's top seed.
