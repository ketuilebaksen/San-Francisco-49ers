Let's start with Jerry Rice's comments, because they set the tone for this whole week. Rice spoke to Bay Area radio station KNBR on Wednesday, shortly after the 49ers acquired Allen from the Colts. He said the 49ers should win the whole thing, pointing to the strength of the receiver group if the players can get healthy at the same time. When he was asked whether Allen could contribute right away, he said, "Yeah, without a doubt with his experience." He described Allen this way: "This guy is not a burner, but he knows how to get open." And he said that once Allen and Brock Purdy get on the same page, using both of their experience, it is going to be a match made in heaven.

Now, is that a bombshell? It's a prediction, not a decision. Rice isn't on the roster and he isn't making personnel calls. But when Jerry Rice, the man who holds just about every major receiving record in NFL history, looks at a receiver room and says it should be a no-brainer, people listen. And he's not the only one feeling it. The 49ers are 7 and 1, ranked second by ESPN and CBS Sports, and they hold the number one seed in the NFC.

And this isn't a new feeling for Rice. Back in the summer, he said he believed 2026 is the year for the 49ers to end their Super Bowl drought. Think about how long that drought has been. The 49ers' last championship came after the 1994 season, in Super Bowl XXIX, a game where Rice himself caught three touchdown passes. Since then, they've come painfully close. They lost Super Bowl LIV to Kansas City after the 2019 season. They lost Super Bowl LVIII, again to Kansas City, in overtime after the 2023 season. That's two heartbreaks in five years. So when Rice says "we should win the whole thing," he's speaking for a fanbase that has waited more than 30 years for a sixth Lombardi Trophy.

And when it comes to receivers, there's nobody more qualified to judge. Rice retired as the NFL's all-time leader in receptions, receiving yards and receiving touchdowns, with 1,549 catches, 22,895 yards and 197 receiving touchdowns. Those records may never be broken. So when he looks at Allen and says this guy knows how to get open, that's the ultimate stamp of approval for a route runner.

So let's talk about the trade itself. Right before the deadline, the 49ers acquired Keenan Allen from Indianapolis for a 2027 sixth-round pick. And I like the move. Does it fit the description of an all-in deal where San Francisco goes out and gets a superstar? No. But it fills a need, in the same way the DeForest Buckner trade did a couple of weeks earlier. It's a different kind of move, one that makes a good team deeper rather than flashier.

What kind of trade is this, then? It's the kind contenders make when they like their core and want to protect it. The 49ers weren't trying to remake the roster. They saw a hole created by injuries, identified a player who fit the need, and acted before the deadline passed. That's a quieter philosophy than going all-in on a star, but it often produces more reliable results, because you're adding to a team that already works.

Consider the profile. Allen has more than 1,000 catches and more than 12,000 receiving yards. He's a six-time Pro Bowler. He's the fastest player in NFL history to reach 1,000 career receptions, doing it in 159 games. He's 6-foot-2, a big body out of the slot and a solid athlete for that size. He's a physical, detailed, nuanced route runner who can play like a power forward, box out defensive backs, and use his frame to win at the catch point. For a 49ers team that has suffered so many injuries at receiver, he deepens the group. And that's a very practical benefit. Depth at receiver is something you only appreciate when you don't have it. And the 49ers have spent most of this season learning that lesson the hard way.

Now, let's do the comparison everyone wants, because it's fascinating: Keenan Allen versus Mike Evans.

Evans is in his 13th season. He has more than 13,000 receiving yards and more than 100 touchdowns. He started his career with 11 straight seasons of at least 1,000 receiving yards, an NFL record. His yards per catch is much higher than Allen's, and his touchdown total is much larger. That tells you exactly who he is: a vertical threat who finishes drives, a big X receiver who wins downfield and in the red zone.

Allen has been in the league one year longer than Evans. He has more catches, 1,095 of them, but fewer yards, at about 12,400, and far fewer touchdowns, with 71. He averages about 11.3 yards per catch for his career. He's not as explosive downfield. But his catch total tells you he's a volume and efficiency player. He's the receiver you throw to on third down when you need to move the sticks.

Put the two together, and you have a complementary pair. One stretches the field. The other sustains drives. Both have been chain-moving machines for more than a decade. And now, they're both on the 49ers.

I understand the concerns about the age of these two players, and the injuries both have dealt with recently. Evans is 33. Allen is 34. Neither one is the player he was in his prime. And honestly, I think Evans is a likely Hall of Famer, while Allen is more of a borderline case. But you don't need to be a Hall of Famer to help a contender win in January. You need to be reliable, and both of them are. But both have had remarkable careers. And both still know how to win in this league.

Allen has also made it clear that he's fired up to be here. Headlines from his first media session described him as energized and even giddy after the trade, going from a struggling Colts team to the top of the NFC. And the turnaround was fast. He was traded on Tuesday afternoon, traveled to the Bay Area, took his physical, and was practicing with his new team on Wednesday. Kyle Shanahan said Allen had only about two hours of installation before that first practice. That's the kind of speed this business demands. Nobody who's running on empty makes that trip and still shows up ready to work.

The quick turnaround also shows what a veteran brings. A rookie who changed teams overnight would struggle to be ready for practice the next afternoon. A veteran with a decade of experience already knows how to read coverages and run routes. The work is mostly about learning a new vocabulary. That's the advantage of acquiring a player with a long track record.

And even when Evans comes back and stays healthy, Allen can be the big-bodied slot receiver alongside him, with Evans at the classic X spot. I think Brock Purdy is probably thrilled about this move. We saw the rapport he built with Evans early in the season. He can build something similar with Allen. And when a quarterback has two reliable, big targets who win with technique and not just speed, the defense has to pick its poison.

Now, let's look at the final injury report, because there was a lot of news on Friday.

The good news starts with Deebo Samuel. He had a full practice on Friday, and he has no injury designation. Shanahan said Deebo is clear to play, adding that it's his first week back and that he had a good, healthy week. Deebo hasn't played since October 11th against Seattle. Before he got hurt, he had 16 catches for 206 yards and three touchdowns, plus 78 rushing yards, in five games. Getting him back changes everything for this offense. He's a receiver, a running back, a decoy and a gadget weapon all in one, and defenses have to know where he is on every snap.

Mike Evans has been ruled out again, for the second straight game, with Achilles tendinitis. But there's encouraging news. Shanahan said Evans' recovery is going well, and that there's a chance he could return next week, though he's not sure yet. The team wants the injury fully healed so it doesn't flare up again. That's the right approach. The goal is to have Evans healthy for the stretch run and the playoffs, not to rush him back for one game in November.

Now, the cornerbacks. This was the biggest concern of the week, and there's some relief. Deommodore Lenoir, with his foot injury, and Renardo Green, with his back injury, both returned to practice on Friday after missing Wednesday and Thursday. They're listed as questionable. So are Upton Stout, with shoulder and neck issues, and Nate Hobbs, with his adductor. Four questionable cornerbacks is not ideal heading into a road game, especially against a team with a receiver like George Pickens. But the fact that Lenoir and Green were back on the field by Friday is a very good sign.

Corners are the position where depth matters most in a game like this, because a receiver can find a mismatch the moment a starter is out. When two starters miss practice and a third is limited, the staff has to think about who plays where, how much help the safeties provide, and how to protect a young player from a bad matchup. That's why Hobbs' return matters so much. He can play inside or outside, and if anyone can't go, he's ready.

But there's a new worry: Trent Williams. The 38-year-old left tackle injured his shoulder on Thursday and missed practice on Friday. He's listed as questionable. Shanahan said that when a player gets hurt on a Thursday, the team isn't fully at ease, but he's hoping there's enough time for Williams to be ready. That's a big one. Williams has been the anchor of this offensive line for years, and he's a big reason the 49ers lead the league in rushing success rate. Protecting Purdy's blind side in a loud road stadium is not a job you want to hand to a backup on short notice. Earlier this week, Shanahan said Williams is playing at a really high level, maybe as well as ever statistically. If he can't play, it would be a significant blow, especially in a road game against a defense that still has dangerous pass rushers.

Dre Greenlaw, with a knee issue, and defensive tackle C.J. West, with his knee, are also questionable. West returned to practice this week, which is huge for the defensive line after Gracen Halton was lost for the season with a fractured and dislocated ankle. West isn't the same kind of pass rusher Halton was, but he gives the group more bodies at a position that badly needs them.

Now, think about what the receiver group looked like in Atlanta three weeks ago. The active receivers were Brandin Cooks, Jordan Watkins, Jacob Cowing and Christian Kirk, who's now on injured reserve. That was a thin group, and it showed in a 29 to 20 loss. Now, just a few weeks later, the 49ers have Deebo back. Demarcus Robinson, who played last week after coming off injured reserve. Allen. The speed of Watkins, who just broke out with four catches for 86 yards and his first career touchdown against the Raiders. And Cowing as a gadget player. That's a much better receiver group than the one in Atlanta, and it gives Shanahan far more options.

It also gives the 49ers more than one way to win. Deebo is back as a do-everything weapon. Watkins and Cowing bring speed. Allen is a physical slot receiver. Robinson is a reliable veteran. The 49ers can change looks from series to series. A defense that has to prepare for all of those skill sets can't sit in one coverage. And that flexibility is how you keep an offense efficient. Remember, this team already leads the NFL with a 52.7 percent offensive success rate, and it's the only team with a rushing success rate above 50 percent. That was with a depleted receiver room. Now imagine what happens when the pieces come together.

There's a neat connection here between Allen and the history of the position, too. When Allen reached 1,000 career catches in 159 games, he broke the record for the fastest player to get there, previously held by Marvin Harrison at 167 games. Rice, of course, eventually passed 1,500 catches. Allen won't get anywhere close to Rice's totals. But he's one of the few receivers in history whose route-running gets mentioned in the same breath as the legends. And now he gets to do it in the same red and gold that Rice made famous.

Now, it's Dallas week, and if you're a member of the Faithful, you know exactly what that means. This rivalry runs deep. It goes back to The Catch, Dwight Clark's leaping grab from Joe Montana in the NFC Championship game in January 1982. It includes three straight NFC Championship games between these teams in the early '90s, with Dallas winning the first two and the 49ers winning the third on their way to that Super Bowl XXIX title. And it continued with recent playoff meetings, including the 49ers' wild card win in Dallas in January 2022. And this one has a new twist: it's Brock Purdy's first career game in Dallas. He's 3 and 0 against the Cowboys, but all three of those games were in Santa Clara: a 19 to 12 divisional round win in January 2023, a 42 to 10 blowout on Sunday Night Football in October 2023, when he threw four touchdown passes and George Kittle caught three of them, and a 30 to 24 win on Sunday Night Football in 2024. Now he gets to do it in their building.

And the matchup itself sets up well for San Francisco. The Cowboys are 4 and 5, and their defense has been one of the worst in the league this season, ranking near the bottom in DVOA. Their final injury report is long too. Linebacker Donovan Ezeiruaku, tight end Jake Ferguson, running back Tyler Goodson, safety P.J. Locke and receiver Jonathan Mingo are all out. Safety Juanyeh Thomas is doubtful. And cornerbacks DaRon Bland, Devin Moore and Joey Porter Jr. are all questionable, along with defensive tackle Jonathan Bullard and safety Alijah Clark. So Dallas has its own cornerback issues, which could create opportunities for Deebo, Allen and Watkins.

Don't overlook the importance of a game like this as a tune-up, either. A struggling defense gives an offense the chance to work in its new pieces in live action, to build timing with Allen, and to find out how Deebo looks after a month off. Because after Dallas, the schedule gets much tougher. Analyst Warren Sharp has pointed out that after this game, the 49ers face one of the toughest stretches of opposing defenses through the rest of the season. It starts with the Minnesota Vikings in Mexico City, one of the best defenses in football. Then Seattle comes to Levi's Stadium on November 30th. That's when we'll find out what this offense is truly made of.

Let's not forget how this team got here, either, because last Sunday's game set the stage for this week. The 49ers beat the Raiders 38 to 17. They scored touchdowns on their first three drives. Purdy completed 17 of 25 passes for 232 yards and two touchdowns and ran for another, and his touchdown pass to McCaffrey was the 100th of his career, in just his 57th regular season game. Only nine other quarterbacks since the merger had reached 100 touchdown passes that quickly. McCaffrey scored twice and passed Marshall Faulk for the most receiving touchdowns by a running back in the Super Bowl era. And the defense forced five takeaways, all in the second half, including a pick-six by rookie linebacker Jaden Dugger on the first play after halftime.

That's the foundation Allen and Deebo are joining. Not a team that needs to be rescued, but a team that's already winning, and now has more weapons. And it's why Pete Prisco wrote this week that Purdy is in the MVP conversation. His career passer rating of 105.6 became the highest in NFL history among quarterbacks with at least 1,500 attempts back in Week 6. He's done all of this while throwing to a constantly changing group of receivers. With Deebo and Allen, and Evans potentially back next week, his numbers could get even better.

And don't forget George Kittle. He's been the go-to target while the receivers have been hurt, and he was the highest-graded player on the offense against the Raiders, according to PFF. With Allen working underneath and Deebo back, defenses won't be able to double Kittle as easily. That could make him even more dangerous over the second half of the season.

So what are the keys to Sunday's game?

First, protect Purdy, especially if Trent Williams is limited or out. That means quick throws, play-action, and a heavy dose of the run game. McCaffrey leads every running back in the NFL in EPA per rush, and rookie Kaelon Black just ran for 70 yards on 11 carries against the Raiders. Shanahan says the two are basically interchangeable in the run game. Against a weak Dallas defense, the 49ers should be able to run the ball.

Second, get Deebo involved early. The sooner he feels the hits and the contact again, the sooner he'll be back to his old self. After a month off, a couple of designed touches, a screen, a jet sweep, a quick slant, can help him get back into rhythm.

Third, find out what Allen can give you. Shanahan said Allen's role would be determined based on how he looked by Saturday. Even a limited package on third down could make a difference. And remember, he played against this exact Dallas defense last week with the Colts, catching four passes for 26 yards. He has fresh film and fresh memories of what they do.

Fourth, get the pass rush going. This is the area that still feels unfinished. Against the Raiders, the 49ers forced five takeaways, but they didn't record a sack. With Bosa getting back into game shape and Buckner and Osa Odighizuwa pushing the pocket, this is a chance for the front to finish plays.

And fifth, win the cornerback chess match. If Green and Lenoir play, the 49ers should be in good shape. If either one is limited, the safeties will need to help, and Hobbs could see real snaps in his 49ers debut.

Now, let's talk about the Cowboys' offense, because the 49ers' secondary questions make this matchup interesting. George Pickens, who was limited earlier in the week with a hamstring injury, wasn't listed on Dallas' final injury report, so he's expected to play. Last season, Pickens caught 93 passes for 1,429 yards and nine touchdowns and made his first Pro Bowl. He's playing on the franchise tag, and his name came up in trade rumors before the deadline, but Dallas kept him. He's the kind of receiver who can take advantage of a banged-up secondary. If the 49ers' corners aren't at full strength, expect Raheem Morris' defense to give Pickens extra attention with safety help over the top. And KaVontae Turpin, one of the most dangerous returners in the league, was also limited earlier in the week. That makes special teams coverage, which Shanahan admitted has to get better, even more important. That's part of why the 49ers brought back KhaDarel Hodge, a former Pro Bowl special teamer, who played well in his return against the Raiders.

On the other side, Jordan Watkins could be a big part of the plan. With Dallas dealing with injuries at cornerback, his speed could create matchup problems. He led all 49ers receivers in snaps against the Raiders, and Shanahan said he's taken a huge step forward. With Deebo and Allen joining him, Watkins should see a lot of one-on-one coverage, and that's when young receivers make their biggest plays.

Let me also give you the honest counterpoint, because it matters. It's easy to look at this roster on paper and get excited. But the 49ers still need everyone to actually get healthy, and stay healthy. Evans hasn't played since October 19th. Trent Williams is now dealing with a shoulder injury. Four cornerbacks are questionable. And Allen still has a possible league suspension hanging over him after being charged with two misdemeanors related to drunk driving before the season. Adam Schefter has reported he faces at least a three-game suspension, and Shanahan said this week that the timing could go either way. So Jerry Rice's "no-brainer" comes with a lot of "ifs." Rice said it himself: it all depends on getting healthy at the same time.

But here's the other side. This team is 7 and 1 without most of its top receivers for weeks. It went 4 and 1 while Nick Bosa was out. It's 3 and 0 against teams that currently have winning records: the Rams, the Broncos and the Seahawks. It's first in DVOA. And now it's getting Deebo back, adding Allen, and potentially getting Evans back as soon as next week. That's a team trending in exactly the right direction. And the timing couldn't be better, because the toughest part of the schedule starts right after this game.

Let's also take a quick look at the bigger picture in the NFC. The Rams are dealing with major injuries at receiver, with Puka Nacua on injured reserve with knee sprains and turf toe, and Davante Adams also on IR. Seattle is managing Sam Darnold's quad injury and cracked rib. Arizona is 3 and 6 and starting Gardner Minshew. The 49ers lead the NFC West by two and a half games. A win in Dallas would push them to 8 and 1, and keep them on track for the top seed and the only first-round bye in the conference.

And one more note on Brandon Aiyuk, since his name keeps coming up. The NFL reinstated him on Monday, and he passed his physical on Tuesday. But the 49ers didn't trade him or release him at the deadline. They're expected to use an unpaid roster exemption that runs through November 23rd. Shanahan has said the reinstatement means absolutely nothing to the team. So while Aiyuk waits, Allen and Deebo are getting ready to play. That's where the focus is, and that's exactly where it should be.

Let's also think about the Allen suspension scenarios one more time, because they matter for Rice's prediction. If the league hands down the suspension soon, the 49ers could lose Allen for at least three games in the middle of this tough stretch. If it doesn't come until 2027, the 49ers get him for the whole stretch run and the playoffs, and he's a free agent after the season anyway. Shanahan said the timing could go either way. For now, the 49ers are using him and planning for both possibilities. That's another reason Evans' recovery matters so much. If Evans is back soon, the receiver room can absorb an Allen suspension. If not, things could get thin again.

And then there's the next game after Dallas. The Vikings in Mexico City. Both teams came into the season's second half among the best in the NFC, and the result could decide who holds the top seed. Minnesota's defense has ranked second in DVOA this season, so it'll be a far bigger test for the 49ers' offense than Dallas. And there's an extra storyline: Jauan Jennings, who turned down a trade back to San Francisco and told Shanahan he was happy in Minnesota, could be on the other sideline. After that, Seattle at Levi's Stadium on November 30th, with the division on the line. These three games, starting tomorrow, will tell us whether Rice's prediction is realistic.
