What an insane football game that was. The San Francisco 49ers outlasted the Dallas Cowboys 46 to 40, improving to 8 and 1, in a game that combined for 86 points and more than a thousand yards of offense. It was a historic offensive performance from Kyle Shanahan, Brock Purdy, Christian McCaffrey, Deebo Samuel and the rest of the offense. And the Faithful showed up and showed out at AT&T Stadium, with a huge wave of red and gold in the stands in Arlington, making a road game feel a lot like home at times. But underneath all the fireworks, there's a story the 49ers can't ignore. The defense has now gone three straight games without a real pass rush. And with the Vikings waiting in Mexico City, that's the decision this team now faces: how do you fix a defense that can't get to the quarterback, with no trade options left? Let's start with just how dominant this offense was, because the numbers are almost hard to believe, even for a team that's been this efficient all season. The 49ers gained 584 yards. That's the most they've gained in any game since Kyle Shanahan arrived in 2017, and it's the most the Cowboys have ever allowed in a home game. They averaged 9.4 yards per play. Think about how crazy that is. On average, almost every single play the 49ers ran nearly picked up a first down. They ran for 214 yards on 29 carries, more than seven yards per carry. Purdy completed 22 of his 28 passes without a turnover. And they scored 46 points, the most any 49ers team has ever scored against Dallas, despite committing a lot of penalties along the way.

I said this before the game, and I'll say it again now: you won't find a bigger gap in the NFL right now between an offensive play-caller and a defensive play-caller than the one we saw on Sunday. On one side, Kyle Shanahan. On the other, Dallas' first-year defensive coordinator Christian Parker, who came over from the Philadelphia Eagles this offseason. Shanahan worked that defense all day long. Shifts, motions, unique personnel groupings, two backs, two tight ends, and then spreading the field out of those same personnel. Dallas simply couldn't line up correctly against it. And after the game, the criticism of Parker was loud. Analysts like Warren Sharp and Marcus Mosher called him out by name, and longtime Cowboys reporter Jon Machota called the defensive performance completely inexcusable. Pro Football Network noted that Dallas ranks last in its defensive impact metric.

And this game had a big stage, too. Tom Brady was on the call for FOX. A sellout crowd of 93,715 packed AT&T Stadium. And it was the 42nd meeting in one of the NFL's great rivalries, a rivalry that goes back to The Catch in January 1982 and three straight NFC Championship games in the early '90s. The 49ers have now won four straight against Dallas. And for a fan base that grew up with those classic battles, a win like this in Arlington feels extra sweet.

Let me explain a little more about why Dallas struggled so much against this scheme, because it's the heart of what makes the 49ers' offense so dangerous. When an offense keeps two backs or two tight ends on the field, the defense has to respect the run. If it puts smaller defenders on the field to cover receivers, those defenders have to take on lead blockers in the run game. If it brings in bigger defenders to stop the run, the offense can motion out and spread the field, and suddenly a linebacker is covering George Kittle or Christian McCaffrey in space. Add in shifts before the snap, and motion across the formation, and the defense has to adjust on the fly every single play. On Sunday, Dallas was a step behind all day. That's how you average 9.4 yards per play.

Brock Purdy was spectacular. He went 22 of 28 for a career-high 389 yards and three touchdowns, with no interceptions, a 154.5 passer rating, and almost 14 yards per attempt. And he did it while getting sacked five times. Dallas got home five times, the roughest day of the season for this offensive line. But Purdy still found ways to create plays out of structure, and those plays are what make him special.

Take the 74-yard touchdown to McCaffrey in the second quarter. Purdy was in the pocket, sidestepped a defender, rolled to his right, and the defender thought he was going to take off. Purdy stopped dead in his tracks, pivoted, backed up and found McCaffrey, who had started the play as a blocker and then released into space. High football IQ from both of them. Purdy said afterward that he wasn't planning to throw it, and then he felt Christian pop open. McCaffrey credited Purdy for keeping the play alive. It turned into the longest touchdown of McCaffrey's career.

This is why a lot of people believe Brock Purdy is the MVP of the league right now. He's dealt with constant injuries at receiver all season. Players have been in and out of the lineup every week. His offensive line had a rough day in pass protection. And still, he carried this team, even when the pocket was collapsing around him. His career passer rating of 105.6 became the highest in NFL history among quarterbacks with at least 1,500 attempts back in Week 6. He threw his 100th career touchdown pass the week before this game, in just his 57th regular season game. And now he's added a career-high 389-yard performance on the road. He's 4 and 0 against the Cowboys in his career, and this was his first game in Dallas.

And let's give some credit to the offensive line, too, despite the sacks. The 49ers ran for 214 yards at more than seven yards per carry. That doesn't happen without the line winning up front. Trent Williams played through a shoulder injury he suffered in Thursday's practice, after being listed as questionable. Shanahan had admitted he wasn't totally at ease about it. Not only did Williams play, he threw a key block on Deebo's 70-yard touchdown. For a 38-year-old left tackle, that's remarkable toughness.

Then there's Christian McCaffrey, who had a hat trick. Three touchdowns, 223 scrimmage yards, including 161 receiving yards on just four catches and 62 rushing yards. According to ESPN, he became the first player in NFL history with at least 160 receiving yards, two receiving touchdowns, 60 rushing yards and a rushing touchdown in the same game. And on his final touchdown, the 66-yarder that pushed the lead to 46 to 32 with just over three minutes left, he was matched up in coverage and simply ran away from the Dallas defense. Even Shannon Sharpe called the coverage on that play a mistake. On two different occasions in this game, McCaffrey looked as fast as he's been all season, pulling away from the entire Cowboys defense. For a running back who's 30 years old, that's remarkable. He's now scored a rushing touchdown in eight straight games.

Deebo Samuel had two touchdowns in his first game back from a hamstring injury. He caught an 11-yard touchdown on the opening drive, and then scored on a 70-yard reverse. Go back and watch that play. Kaelon Black took the handoff, and nearly the entire Cowboys defense flowed toward him. Then he pitched it to Deebo going the other way, and Purdy and Trent Williams threw blocks to spring him. Pure Kyle Shanahan. It's the kind of play he sets up for weeks, waiting for a defense to overcommit, and Dallas overcommitted badly.

The 49ers had two touchdowns of 70 or more yards and another of more than 60. Kaelon Black added a 25-yard touchdown run. George Kittle caught six passes for 74 yards, although he didn't find the end zone, as he so often does against Dallas. And by quarter, the 49ers scored seven points in the first quarter, 10 in the second, 14 in the third and 15 in the fourth.

And then there's Keenan Allen. The 49ers traded for him on Tuesday. He joined the team on Wednesday. He got only three practices with the offense. And he caught five passes for 61 yards, with four of those catches moving the chains, including a diving 23-yard catch on third-and-12. A lot of fans asked me before this game what I thought about the Allen trade. My answer was simple: he came into this season with 40 catches in eight games for the Colts, and he's a big, physical, savvy route runner who doesn't need speed to win. In a lot of ways, his style is similar to Mike Evans'. He comes down with contested catches. He knows how to get open. And on Sunday, he came through in a lot of big spots.

Remember, according to reports, the 49ers first focused on Josh Downs, but Shanahan suggested Allen after watching Colts film. Downs ended up in Kansas City. Over time, we'll see which player turns out to be the better acquisition. Maybe they both will be. But this is exactly why Shanahan loves veterans. He can trust them. A player who barely knows the playbook stepped in and made plays in the biggest moments. And when you're getting this level of play from your quarterback, you've got a chance to win every week, regardless of what your defense is doing.

The players knew how special this offensive performance was. George Kittle said it makes the offense incredibly dangerous, but added that they have to stay on the field more. Deebo Samuel said the ceiling is as high as they set it. And Kyle Shanahan, always measured, said everybody seemed to make a play on offense, but that the game wasn't as clean as the numbers suggest. That's the right message for a team with Super Bowl expectations. Five sacks allowed, a lot of penalties, and a defense that gave up 40 points are all things to clean up.

And don't forget the young receiver who broke out the week before. Jordan Watkins added two catches for 49 yards on Sunday, giving him a strong two-game stretch after his first career touchdown against the Raiders. Demarcus Robinson was back in the rotation too. With Allen, Deebo, Watkins and Robinson all available, plus Kittle and McCaffrey, Purdy had more options than at any point in the last month. And Evans still isn't back.

Let's also remember how this 8 and 1 record was built, because it's been a remarkable first half. The 49ers opened with a 27 to 7 win over the Rams in Melbourne, Australia. They beat Miami 35 to 13, Arizona 36 to 30, Denver 24 to 14, and Seattle 32 to 29 on the road. They beat Washington 23 to 17 on Monday Night Football. Their only loss came in Atlanta, 29 to 20, in a game where they were missing their top five receivers. Then, after the bye, they beat the Raiders 38 to 17 and now the Cowboys 46 to 40. Eight wins in nine games, Faithful, with one of the longest injury lists in football. That's a team with real resilience.

Now, let's finally talk about the defense, because this is where the concern is real.

Dak Prescott completed 30 of 51 passes for 343 yards and five touchdowns, with one interception. Now, a fair amount of that production came in the fourth quarter, when the 49ers were up by multiple scores and playing softer coverage. But in the first half, when this was still a close game, George Pickens was cooking the 49ers' secondary. Renardo Green and Deommodore Lenoir had a hard time with Pickens and, at times, CeeDee Lamb. And once Green left the game with his back injury, rookie Ephesians Prysock looked overmatched. That's not entirely his fault, and nobody should pile on a rookie. He had played only nine defensive snaps all season before Sunday.

Pickens finished with 11 catches for 145 yards and three touchdowns. Lamb had seven catches for 72 yards and a touchdown. Ryan Flournoy added six for 71 and a touchdown. And the Cowboys cut a 19-point deficit to six in the final four minutes. The 49ers needed to recover an onside kick and hold off a Hail Mary to close it out.

And the pass rush, again, wasn't there. No defensive lineman got Prescott on the ground for a full sack. The official scorer credited Dre Greenlaw with a sack, and Sports Illustrated's 49ers site called it "charity." Osa Odighizuwa, Mykel Williams and Keion White each hit Prescott, but Nick Bosa and DeForest Buckner didn't. Bosa was flagged for being offside three times. And whether Raheem Morris called a four-man rush, a five-man rush or a blitz, nothing really worked.

Here's the bigger problem, and it's the one that worries me most. It's not just one game. In the loss in Atlanta, the 49ers pressured Michael Penix Jr. on just three of his 34 dropbacks. Against the Raiders, they didn't sack Kirk Cousins, one of the least mobile quarterbacks in football. And against Dallas, they didn't get a full sack from a lineman against Prescott, who isn't nearly as mobile as he used to be. That's three straight games where this pass rush has been close to non-existent.

And here's what makes it so concerning: the 49ers didn't trade for a pass rusher at the deadline. The Jets kept Will McDonald IV. The Chargers kept Khalil Mack. So there are no outside answers coming. Free agency in November rarely produces difference-making pass rushers, and the trade window is closed. The answers have to come from the players already on this roster: Bosa, Buckner, Odighizuwa, White and Mykel Williams. Going into the game, Odighizuwa, who the 49ers acquired from Dallas in March, already had two sacks and four tackles for loss through eight games. He's been one of their best defensive linemen all year. But the group as a whole has to be better.

It's also worth pointing out how the defense has trended over the last few weeks in terms of points allowed. Atlanta scored 29. The Raiders scored 17, but that game was a lot closer than the final score until the 49ers forced five second-half takeaways. And now Dallas put up 40. Earlier in the season, this defense held the Rams to seven points and Denver to 14. So the talent to be elite is there. The recent trend is the problem.

Let me be fair to the defense too, because context matters. It was missing pieces. Nate Hobbs and C.J. West were inactive. Gracen Halton is out for the season with a fractured and dislocated ankle. Green left during the game. And Dallas has two of the best outside receivers in football. The defense also still has a nose for the football. Before Sunday, the 49ers had forced 12 takeaways in eight games, compared to 16 in all of 2025. Fred Warner, as usual, was everywhere. He had 11 tackles, a quarterback hit, and a forced fumble the officials appeared to miss. Malik Mustapha intercepted Prescott. And the defense made the final stops it needed.

But if the 49ers face a team like the Chicago Bears, or the Vikings, or the Lions in the playoffs, can they really expect to win shootouts every week? This offense is absolutely capable of winning a Super Bowl. But you can't play defense like this week in and week out and expect to lift the Lombardi Trophy. The defense simply has to be better.

So how do you fix a pass rush without making a trade? Let me give you a few ideas. First, rotate more. Buckner is 32, and Bosa is only two games back from a calf injury that cost him five games. Fresh legs in the fourth quarter can make a big difference. That means more snaps for Mykel Williams, last year's first-round pick, and for Keion White, who has been disruptive. Second, use more stunts and games up front. When a pass rush isn't winning with pure power, twists and stunts can create confusion for offensive linemen and free up a rusher. Third, help the rush with coverage. Sometimes a pass rush looks bad because the quarterback has an easy first read. If the secondary can take away quick throws, the rush gets an extra half-second to get home. And fourth, trust the track record. Bosa is a former Defensive Player of the Year. Buckner is a three-time Pro Bowler. These players have been elite before. The talent is there. The question is whether health and rhythm come back in time.

And Bosa's three offside penalties tell you something too. A player who's pressing, trying to make up for weeks on the sideline, often jumps the snap count. As he gets more comfortable, and more confident in that calf, those penalties should go down and the production should go up. The 49ers are betting on that. They didn't have another choice at the deadline.

So what's the plan? Let's talk about the decisions coming this week, because there are several.

First, Nate Hobbs. His practice window opened on November 4th, which gives the 49ers 21 days to activate him, or he stays on injured reserve for the rest of the season. With Green's back acting up again, getting Hobbs on the field in Mexico City looks like a priority. He can play inside or outside, and the 49ers badly need experienced depth at cornerback.

Second, C.J. West. The defensive line needs bodies, and he's close. He's also on the 21-day clock after his practice window opened, and he could add depth to a defensive line that's been stretched thin since the Halton injury.

Third, Renardo Green. Shanahan said Green was the only injury from Sunday's game, and that he'd give a fuller update this week. It may be a flare-up of the back injury that kept him out of two practices last week. The team will likely be careful.

Fourth, Mike Evans. Shanahan said last week that Evans is "real close" to returning, and that it would be hard to keep him out if this were the last game. Mexico City could be his return. Can you imagine what this offense looks like with a healthy Evans and Allen on the field at the same time, plus Deebo, Kittle and McCaffrey? Defenses would have to pick their poison on every single snap.

And fifth, Brandon Aiyuk. The NFL reinstated him last week, and the 49ers have been using an unpaid two-week roster exemption that runs through November 23rd, the day after the Vikings game. A release is widely expected. And after Sunday, with Allen and Deebo both producing, it's hard to see Aiyuk in the 49ers' plans.

There was also one other interesting roster note from Sunday: veteran receiver Brandin Cooks was inactive. With Deebo back, Allen arriving and Demarcus Robinson healthy, the receiver room is suddenly crowded. That's a big change from a month ago, when the 49ers were throwing to their fifth and sixth options in Atlanta.

Now, the Vikings, and the biggest game of the season so far. Minnesota beat the Packers 30 to 10 in Green Bay on Sunday, with Kirk Cousins starting after being traded back to the Vikings at the deadline. They're 7 and 2. The Lions and Bears are 7 and 2 as well. So the 49ers, at 8 and 1, hold the top seed, but the race is tight. In the NFC West, Seattle and the Rams both won on Sunday and are 6 and 4, so the 49ers still lead the division by two and a half games.

And this game has storylines everywhere. Cousins faced the 49ers just two weeks ago with the Raiders, when the 49ers intercepted him three times and forced five second-half takeaways. Now he gets a second shot, with Justin Jefferson and a much better roster around him. Jauan Jennings, who turned down a trade back to San Francisco, could be on the other sideline. And Minnesota's defense has ranked among the best in the league all season.

The 49ers aren't flying home. They'll spend the week practicing at altitude in Colorado Springs before heading to Mexico City, which sits about 7,350 feet above sea level. They did the same thing in 2022 before beating Arizona 38 to 10 there, and the plan clearly worked. And they'll be the designated home team at the renovated Estadio Azteca.

Let's dig a little deeper into the Vikings matchup, because it's a completely different test from Dallas. The Cowboys' defense ranks at or near the bottom of the league. Minnesota's defense has been one of the best in football. That means the 49ers probably won't average 9.4 yards per play again. They'll need to move the ball one first down at a time, and that's where Allen's ability to get open quickly on third down and Kittle's toughness over the middle become so valuable. Purdy will need to get the ball out fast, especially after taking five sacks against Dallas.

On the other side, Cousins is a pocket passer. He's at his best when he has time and a clean platform. In the first meeting two weeks ago, the 49ers didn't sack him, but they pressured him enough to force mistakes, and the secondary made him pay with three interceptions. With Justin Jefferson, though, Cousins has a receiver who can turn any mistake in coverage into a touchdown. That's why the cornerback situation matters so much. If Green can't play, and if Hobbs isn't activated, the 49ers will be relying heavily on Lenoir, Upton Stout and Prysock against one of the best receivers in the world.

And the altitude matters too. Mexico City sits about 7,350 feet above sea level, higher than Denver. Players say exertion there feels like breathing through a straw. The 49ers will spend the week in Colorado Springs to adjust. The Vikings, coming off a big win in Green Bay, may not have the same preparation. In a game this close, that extra week of acclimation could make a difference in the fourth quarter, especially for pass rushers and receivers running deep routes.

Let's also think about what this game means for Dallas, because it was a gut punch for the Cowboys. They fell to 4 and 6, and they host Tennessee next week. Prescott threw five touchdown passes, tying his career high, and Pickens had a career-best three touchdown catches. Dallas had chances to tie or win this game in the final minutes. But its defense gave up 584 yards, the most it has ever allowed at home, and it couldn't get the stops it needed. The pressure on Christian Parker and the Cowboys' defensive staff is going to be intense this week.

And one more thing about Allen. He's facing a possible league suspension after being charged with two misdemeanors related to drunk driving in Indianapolis before the season. Adam Schefter has reported at least three games, and Shanahan said last week that the timing could go either way. If it comes soon, the 49ers would lose a player who just showed how valuable he can be. That's another reason Evans' return is so important. With Evans back, the offense can absorb an Allen suspension. Without him, it could get thin again.

So here's my big-picture take. It's lights out and electric to watch this offense play this way. And this offense isn't even at full strength yet. Think about the receiver depth chart a month ago compared to now. In Atlanta, the active receivers were Brandin Cooks, Jordan Watkins, Jacob Cowing and Christian Kirk, and the 49ers lost 29 to 20. On Sunday, Cooks was inactive because the room was too crowded. That's how much has changed. Evans hasn't played since October 19th. Allen has had three practices. Deebo just came back. When everyone is healthy, this could be one of the best offenses in football in a very long time.

But the defense will decide whether this season ends with a parade. Three straight games without a real pass rush. A secondary that's banged up. And a schedule that's about to get much tougher. If Raheem Morris and this defensive line can fix the pass rush, the 49ers are the favorites in the NFC. If they can't, every playoff game could turn into a shootout. And shootouts are coin flips, no matter how good your offense is.

Let me also give the counterpoint, because some fans will say, "It's only one game." And that's fair. Dallas has elite receivers. The 49ers were short-handed in the secondary. A lot of the damage came in garbage time. And the 49ers still won. But it's not only one game. It's a pattern that goes back three weeks, and the pattern is what worries me.

One last thought on the bigger picture. A month ago, this team lost in Atlanta with four turnovers and a depleted receiver room, and people were questioning whether the offense could carry it. Now, the offense looks like the best in football, and the question has flipped to the defense. That's how quickly things change in the NFL. And it's a reminder that the second half of the season is about solving the next problem. The 49ers solved the receiver problem with Allen, Deebo's return and Watkins' growth. Now they have to solve the pass rush. If they do, Jerry Rice's prediction that this team should win the whole thing might not be so crazy after all.
